\section{The Krull Dimension of Some Rings}

--- If $A$ is a right noetherian ring, we call \emph{right Krull
dimension of $A$} the Krull dimension of the category
$\operatorname{mod}A$. If $G$ is a graded ring, we say that $G$ is a
\emph{right noetherian graded ring} if every increasing sequence of
homogeneous right ideals is stationary; we call \emph{right Krull
dimension of the graded ring $G$} the Krull dimension of the abelian
category of graded modules over the graded ring $G$:
\begin{itemize}
\item An object of this category is a graded $G$-module whose
  underlying set belongs to the universe $\Univ$.
\item If $M$ and $N$ are two objects of the category, a morphism from
  $M$ to $N$ is a $G$-linear map, homogeneous, of degree~$0$ from $M$
  to $N$.
\item The composition of morphisms coincides with the usual
  composition of maps.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}
Let $A$ be a ring filtered by a decreasing sequence of abelian
subgroups $A_n$ ($n\ge0$) such that $A_m.A_n\subset A_{m+n}$.
Suppose that $A$ is the union of the $A_n$ and that $A$ is separated
and complete for the topology defined by the $A_n$.
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] If the associated graded ring
\[
G(A)=\ldots\oplus A_{-1}/A_{-2}\oplus A_0/A_{-1}\oplus A_1/A_0
\oplus\ldots
\]
is right noetherian, the ring $A$ is right noetherian. If every
homogeneous right ideal of $G(A)$ is generated by fewer than $r$
homogeneous elements, every right ideal of $A$ is generated by fewer
than $r$ elements.
\item[\normalfont b.] If the graded ring $G(A)$ is right noetherian and
has right Krull dimension less than $n$, then $A$ is right noetherian
and has right Krull dimension less than $n$.
\end{description}
\end{proposition}

To prove~(a) and~(b), we rely on the following lemmas, which are well
known:

\begin{lemma}
Let $M$ and $N$ be two abelian groups filtered by decreasing sequences
of subgroups $M_n$ and $N_n$ ($n\ge0$). Suppose that the union of the
$M_n$ (resp.~of the $N_n$) equals $M$ (resp.~$N$), that the
intersection of the $M_n$ (resp.~of the $N_n$) is zero and that $M_n$
is complete for the topology defined by the $M_n$. Let $f:M\to N$ be a
homomorphism of filtered groups. If $f$ induces a surjection of the
associated graded groups, then $f$ is a surjection and $N$ is complete
for the topology defined by the $N_n$. If $f$ induces an injection of
the associated graded groups, then $f$ is an injection.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}
Suppose the hypotheses of Proposition~15 satisfied. Let $M$ be an
$A$-module equipped with a filtration by abelian subgroups $M_n$
($n\ge0$), such that $M_m.A_n\subset M_{m+n}$. Suppose that the union
of the $M_n$ equals $M$, that their intersection is zero and that
$G(M)$ is a graded $G(A)$-module generated by $r$ homogeneous
elements. Then $M$ is generated by representatives of the homogeneous
generators of $G(M)$ and $M$ is complete.
\end{lemma}

Let us now prove~(a): if $I$ is a right ideal of $A$, we equip $I$
with the filtration defined by the $I\cap A_n$. Then $G(I)$ is a
homogeneous right ideal of $G(A)$ and it suffices to apply the
preceding lemmas. We also see that $I$ is complete for the filtration
induced by that of $A$.

To show~(b), it suffices to prove that
$\operatorname{Kdim}M\le n$ when $M$ is a right $A$-module of the form
$A/I$, where $I$ is a right ideal. It is therefore manifestly enough
to prove the following lemma:

\begin{lemma}
Let $M$ be a filtered right $A$-module satisfying the conditions of
Lemma~2. Then
$\operatorname{Kdim}M\le\operatorname{Kdim}G(M)$.
\end{lemma}

We proceed by induction on $\operatorname{Kdim}G(M)$: the assertion is
true if $\operatorname{Kdim}G(M)=-1$. Suppose it true if
$\operatorname{Kdim}G(M)<m$ and let us show it if
$\operatorname{Kdim}G(M)=m$:

Otherwise, there would exist an infinite sequence of submodules
$M\supset M^1\supset M^2\supset\ldots$ such that
$\operatorname{Kdim}(M^i/M^{i+1})\ge m$. Equipping the $M^i$ with the
filtration induced by that of $M$ and the $M^i/M^{i+1}$ with the
quotient filtration of that of $M^i$, one would deduce that the Krull
dimension of $G(M^i/M^{i+1})=G(M^i)/G(M^{i+1})$ is greater than or
equal to $m$. The graded module $G(M)$ would therefore have an
infinite sequence of graded submodules whose successive quotients have
Krull dimension greater than or equal to $m$; this is contrary to the
induction hypothesis and to the noetherian character of $G(M)$.
\hfill Q.E.D.

Let now $A$ be a ring, $\sigma$ an automorphism of $A$ and
$A_\sigma[T]$ the ring of \emph{Hilbert polynomials} in $T$ relative
to $\sigma$: this ring consists of the polynomials
$a_0+T.a_1+T^2.a_2+\ldots+T^r.a_r$, with coefficients in $A$, with the
usual addition; on the other hand we impose the commutation relations
$a.T=T.\sigma(a)$ if $a\in A$.

\begin{corollary}
Let $\sigma$ be an automorphism of the ring $A$. If $A$ is right
noetherian, then $A_\sigma[T]$ is right noetherian. If $A$ is right
noetherian and has finite right Krull dimension equal to $n$, then the
right Krull dimension of $A_\sigma[T]$ is equal to $n+1$.
\end{corollary}

We equip the ring $B=A_\sigma[T]$ with the following filtration:
$B_n$ is zero if $n>0$; if $n$ is a positive integer, $B_{-n}$
consists of the Hilbert polynomials of degree $\le n$. In this case,
the ring underlying the graded ring $G(B)$ can be identified with $B$,
$G_{-n}(B)$ being identified with the set of monomials of degree $n$
($n\ge0$). Modulo this identification, the homogeneous right ideals
$\mathfrak{a}$ of $G(B)$ are of the form
\[
\mathfrak{a}_0\oplus T.\mathfrak{a}_1\oplus T^2.\mathfrak{a}_2\oplus
T^3.\mathfrak{a}_3\oplus\ldots\oplus T^i.\mathfrak{a}_i,
\]
where $\mathfrak{a}_i$ is a right ideal of $A$ such that
\[
\ldots\supset\sigma^{-i}(\mathfrak{a}_i)\supset\ldots\supset
\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak{a}_1)\supset\mathfrak{a}_0.
\]

If $A$ is right noetherian, it follows that
$\sigma^{-i}(\mathfrak{a}_i)$ is equal to
$\sigma^{-i-1}(\mathfrak{a}_{i+1})$\ednote{The original prints
$\sigma^{i-1}(\mathfrak{a}_{i+1})$ here; but the increasing sequence
$\sigma^{-i}(\mathfrak{a}_i)$ stabilises as
$\sigma^{-i}(\mathfrak{a}_i)=\sigma^{-i-1}(\mathfrak{a}_{i+1})$, from
which the equality $\sigma(\mathfrak{a}_i)=\mathfrak{a}_{i+1}$ of the
next sentence follows. We print
$\sigma^{-i-1}(\mathfrak{a}_{i+1})$.} for $i$ large enough. There is
therefore an integer $n$ such that
$\sigma(\mathfrak{a}_i)=\mathfrak{a}_{i+1}$ for $i\ge n$; it follows
that $\mathfrak{a}$ is generated by a finite number of homogeneous
elements of degree less than or equal to $n$. Consequently, the graded
ring $G(B)$ is right noetherian and $B$ is right noetherian.

Suppose now that $\operatorname{Kdim}A=n$. If $M$ is an $A$-module, we
equip the $G(B)$-module $M\otimes_AG(B)$ with the evident graduation:
the elements of degree $r$ of $M\otimes_AG(B)$ are of the form
$m\otimes_AT^r$, $m\in M$. We first prove the

\begin{lemma}
Let $M$ be a non-zero $A$-module of finite Krull dimension $n$. The
Krull dimension of the graded $G(B)$-module $M\otimes_AG(B)$ is then
equal to $n+1$.
\end{lemma}

We prove this lemma by induction on $n$. Since $A$ is assumed right
noetherian, it suffices to establish the proof when $M$ is noetherian.
We denote by $\mathbf{A}$ the category of graded $G(B)$-modules and we
use the notations of Chapter~IV (\S1):

If $M$ is simple and if $x$ is a non-zero element of $M$, the only
graded submodules of $M\otimes_AG(B)$ are generated by the
$x\otimes_AT^r$, $r\ge0$. It follows that the image of
$M\otimes_AG(B)$ in the quotient category
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_0$ is a simple object. If $M$ is of finite
length, let $0=M_0\subset M_1\subset\ldots\subset M_s=M$ be a
Jordan--Hölder sequence of $M$. The graded modules
$M_i\otimes_AG(B)$ then define a composition sequence of
$M\otimes_AG(B)$ whose quotients have $1$ for Krull dimension. It
follows that $M\otimes_AG(B)$ has $1$ for Krull dimension.

Suppose now the lemma proved when $n<m$ and let us prove it when $n$
equals $m$: for this we consider $M\otimes_AG(B)$ as an object of the
quotient category $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_{m-1}$. If $M$ is a simple
object of the quotient category
$\operatorname{mod}A/(\operatorname{mod}A)_{m-1}$, the only subobjects
of $M\otimes_AG(B)$ in $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_{m-1}$ are the graded
submodules generated by $M\otimes_AT^r$, $r\ge0$. It follows that the
image of $M\otimes_AG(B)$ in $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_m$ is simple.
Finally, if $M$ is a noetherian $A$-module such that
$\operatorname{Kdim}M=n$, the image of $M$ in
$\operatorname{mod}A/(\operatorname{mod}A)_{n-1}$ is of finite length.
Using composition sequences of $M$, one shows as above that the Krull
dimension of $M\otimes_AG(B)$ is $n+1$.

Lemma~4 shows in particular that the Krull dimension of the graded
ring $G(B)$ is $n+1$. It follows that the Krull dimension of $B$ is
less than or equal to $n+1$ (Proposition~15). Since the decreasing
sequence of right ideals $T^r.B$ is infinite and the Krull dimension
of $T^r.B/T^{r+1}.B$ is manifestly equal to $n$, one also has the
inequality $\operatorname{Kdim}B\ge n+1$. Hence the corollary.

\begin{corollary}
Let $k$ be a commutative field and $\mathfrak{g}$ a
finite-dimensional $k$-Lie algebra:
$[\mathfrak{g}:k]<+\infty$. The enveloping algebra of $\mathfrak{g}$
is a noetherian ring (on the left and on the right) whose Krull
dimension (on the left and on the right) is less than or equal to
$[\mathfrak{g}:k]$.
\end{corollary}

Let indeed $U$ be this enveloping algebra and let $U_{-n}$ be the
vector subspace of $U$ generated by $1$ and the products of the form
$g_1.g_2\ldots g_m$, $0\le n$, $m\le n$, $g_i\in\mathfrak{g}$. It is
known that the graded ring associated with $U$ is a polynomial ring in
$[\mathfrak{g}:k]$ indeterminates. The corollary follows from this fact
and Proposition~15.
