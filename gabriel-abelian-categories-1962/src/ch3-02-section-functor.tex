\section{Properties of the Section Functor}

--- Let $\mathbf A$ be an abelian category, let $\mathbf C$ be a thick
subcategory of $\mathbf A$, let $\mathbf B=\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ be the
quotient category and let $T$ be the canonical functor from $\mathbf A$
into $\mathbf B$.

The reasoning of the preceding paragraph was ``autodual'': it applied as
well to the category $\mathbf A$ as to the dual category
$\mathbf A^{\circ}$. We shall suppose at the beginning of this paragraph
that there exists a functor $S$ adjoint to $T$. This condition is not
self-dual; we leave to the reader the task of formulating the dual
statements of those that follow.

An adjoint functor to $T$ is defined up to isomorphism. We choose once
and for all such a functor $S$ and we call it the \emph{section
functor}. We also use the notation of Chapter~I, \S6; in particular, the
letters $\varphi$, $\psi$, $\Phi$, $\Psi$ will have the following
meanings: $\varphi$ is a functorial isomorphism from
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(.,S.)$ to
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(T.,.)$ and $\psi$ is the inverse
isomorphism of $\varphi$; the letter $\Phi$ (resp.\ $\Psi$) designates
the functorial morphism from $T\circ S$ to $I_{\mathbf B}$ (resp.\ from
$I_{\mathbf A}$ to $S\circ T$) associated with $\varphi$ (resp.\ with
$\psi$).

\begin{proposition}
The section functor is left exact.
\end{proposition}

The assertion is indeed verified for every adjoint functor (Proposition~11,
Chapter~I).

\begin{lemma}
If $M$ is an object of $\mathbf A$, the following assertions are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[a.] For every morphism $u\colon P\to Q$ such that $\operatorname{Ker}u$
      and $\operatorname{Coker}u$ belong to $\mathbf C$, the map
      $\operatorname{Hom}(u,M)\colon
      \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(Q,M)\to
      \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(P,M)$ is a bijection.
\item[b.] Every subobject of $M$ belonging to $\mathbf C$ is zero; moreover,
      every exact sequence
      \[
        0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\;f\;}N\longrightarrow P
        \longrightarrow 0
      \]
      in which $P$ belongs to $\mathbf C$ splits (i.e.\ $f$ induces an
      isomorphism of $M$ onto a direct summand of $N$).
\item[c.] For every object $P$ of $\mathbf A$, the map
      \[
        T(P,M)\colon \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(P,M)
        \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TP,TM)
      \]
      is a bijection.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\emph{(a)}~$\Rightarrow$~\emph{(b)}: let $L$ be a subobject of $M$
belonging to $\mathbf C$. The identity morphism $1_M$ is the image of an
element $v\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M/L,M)$ under the map
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M/L,M)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M,M)$;
in other words, one has an equality $1_M=v\circ p$, where
$p\colon M\to M/L$ is the canonical projection. Hence $p$ is a
monomorphism and $L$ is zero.

Likewise, if
\[
  0\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{\;f\;}N\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow 0
\]
is exact with $P\in\mathbf C$, there exists an element $g\in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(N,M)$ such that $1_M=g\circ f$. This
proves that $f$ is an isomorphism of $M$ onto a direct summand of $N$.

\emph{(b)}~$\Rightarrow$~\emph{(c)}: since every subobject of $M$ belonging
to $\mathbf C$ is zero, $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TP,TM)$ is in
fact the inductive limit of the abelian groups $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf
A}(P',M)$, where $P'$ ranges over the subobjects of $P$ such that
$P/P'$ belongs to $\mathbf C$. In particular, every element of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TP,TM)$ is the image of a morphism
$f\colon P'\to M$.

Let $N=M\Sigma_{P'}P$ be the fibred sum of the diagram defined by $f$ and by the
monomorphism $P'\to P$; the canonical morphism $j\colon M\to N$ is a
monomorphism and $\operatorname{Coker}j\cong P/P'$
(Chapter~I, end of \S5). By assertion~(b), there exists a morphism $p\colon
N\to M$ such that $p\circ j=1_M$; it follows that the morphism
$p\circ k$, where $k\colon P\to N$ is the canonical morphism, is a
morphism $P\to M$ whose restriction to $P'$ is $f$. This proves that
$T(P,M)$ is surjective.

Now let $g\colon P\to M$ be a nonzero morphism. Its image is not zero and
does not belong to $\mathbf C$. It follows that $Tg$ is not zero
(Lemma~2, \S1). This proves that $T(P,M)$ is injective.

\emph{(c)}~$\Rightarrow$~\emph{(a)}: we have the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(P,M) \arrow[r, "T(P{,}M)"] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TP,TM)\\
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(Q,M) \arrow[u, "\operatorname{Hom}(u{,}M)"]
\arrow[r, "T(Q{,}M)"] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TQ,TM) \arrow[u, "\operatorname{Hom}(Tu{,}TM)"]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The maps $T(P,M)$ and $T(Q,M)$ are bijective. If $\operatorname{Ker}u$
and $\operatorname{Coker}u$ belong to $\mathbf C$, then $Tu$ is an
isomorphism (Lemma~4, \S1). It follows that the same holds for
$\operatorname{Hom}(u,M)$.

We shall henceforth say that an object $M$ is
\emph{$\mathbf C$-closed} if it satisfies the equivalent conditions of the
preceding lemma. Our aim is to prove that $M$ is $\mathbf C$-closed if
and only if the morphism $\Psi(M)$ from $M$ into $STM$ is an
isomorphism. For this we shall need the

\begin{lemma}
For every object $N$ of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$, the object $SN$ is
$\mathbf C$-closed.
\end{lemma}

To verify assertion~(a) of Lemma~1, we have the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(P,SN) \arrow[r, "\varphi"] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TP,N)\\
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(Q,SN) \arrow[u, "\operatorname{Hom}(u{,}SN)"]
\arrow[r, "\varphi"] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TQ,N) \arrow[u, "\operatorname{Hom}(Tu{,}N)"]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The maps $\varphi$ are bijective. It follows that
$\operatorname{Hom}(u,SN)$ is a bijection if $Tu$ is an isomorphism.

\begin{proposition}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[a.] The functorial morphism $\Phi$ is an isomorphism from
      $T\circ S$ to $I_{\mathbf B}$.
\item[b.] For every object $M$ of $\mathbf A$, $\operatorname{Ker}\Psi(M)$
      and $\operatorname{Coker}\Psi(M)$ belong to $\mathbf C$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

To prove~(a), let $M$ be an object of $\mathbf A$ and let $N$ be an object
of $\mathbf B$\ednote{The original prints ``an object of $\mathbf A/\mathbf B$'';
we print $\mathbf B=\mathbf A/\mathbf C$, the quotient just defined.}. We have the identity
\[
  \varphi(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TM,\Phi(N))\circ
  T(M,SN).
\]
The map $\varphi(M,N)$ is a bijection, as is $T(M,SN)$ (Lemmas~1 and~2).
It follows that
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TM,\Phi(N))$ is a bijection for every $M$.
Assertion~(a) results from the fact that every object of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$
is of the form $TM$ (Corollary~2 to Proposition~1, Chapter~I).

We now prove~(b) by showing that $T\Psi(M)$ is an isomorphism for every
$M$. Indeed, the composite of $T\Psi(M)$ and of $\Phi(TM)$ is the identity
morphism of $TM$ (Proposition~8, Chapter~I). Since $\Phi$ is a functorial
isomorphism, the result is established.

\begin{unnumberedcorollary}
An object $M$ of $\mathbf A$ is $\mathbf C$-closed if and only if
$\Psi(M)$ is an isomorphism from $M$ onto $STM$.
\end{unnumberedcorollary}

We already know that $STM$ is $\mathbf C$-closed. Suppose therefore that
$M$ is $\mathbf C$-closed: since every subobject of $M$ belonging to
$\mathbf C$ is zero, $\operatorname{Ker}\Psi(M)$ is zero; this proves that
$\Psi(M)$ is a monomorphism. Assertion~(b) of Lemma~1 then shows that
$\operatorname{Coker}\Psi(M)$ is isomorphic to a direct summand of $STM$.
Since every subobject of $STM$ belonging to $\mathbf C$ is zero,
$\operatorname{Coker}\Psi(M)$ is zero; this proves the corollary.

We shall henceforth say that a thick subcategory $\mathbf C$ of
$\mathbf A$ is a \emph{localizing subcategory} of $\mathbf A$ if there
exists a functor $S$ adjoint to $T$. In this case, the left exact functor
$S\circ T$ is called the \emph{localization functor}. This localization
functor is exact if and only if $S$ is exact (Corollary~3 to
Proposition~1).

\begin{proposition}
If $\mathbf C$ is a thick subcategory of $\mathbf A$, the following
assertions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[a.] $\mathbf C$ is a localizing subcategory of $\mathbf A$.
\item[b.] Every object $M$ of $\mathbf A$ contains a subobject which is
      maximal among the subobjects of $M$ belonging to $\mathbf C$;
      moreover, if every subobject of $M$ belonging to $\mathbf C$ is
      zero, there exists a monomorphism from $M$ into a
      $\mathbf C$-closed object.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\emph{(a)}~$\Rightarrow$~\emph{(b)}: with the usual notation it is indeed
clear that $\operatorname{Ker}\Psi(M)$ is maximal among the subobjects
of $M$ belonging to $\mathbf C$. If $\operatorname{Ker}\Psi(M)$ is zero,
$\Psi(M)$ is a monomorphism from $M$ into a $\mathbf C$-closed object.

\emph{(b)}~$\Rightarrow$~\emph{(a)}: let $u\colon M\to N$ be a morphism
of $\mathbf A$. We say that $u$ is a \emph{$\mathbf C$-envelope} of $M$
if $N$ is $\mathbf C$-closed and if $\operatorname{Ker}u$ and
$\operatorname{Coker}u$ are objects of $\mathbf C$. We shall first show
that every object $M$ possesses a $\mathbf C$-envelope.

Let indeed $M'$ be the largest subobject of $M$ belonging to
$\mathbf C$ and let $i$ be a monomorphism from $M/M'$ into a
$\mathbf C$-closed object $R$. Let moreover $N$ be the inverse image in
$R$ of the largest subobject of $\operatorname{Coker}i$ belonging to
$\mathbf C$. Condition~(a) of Lemma~1 shows that $N$ is
$\mathbf C$-closed. If $j$ is the morphism from $M/M'$ into $N$ induced
by $i$, then $j\circ p$, where $p\colon M\to M/M'$ is the canonical
projection, is a $\mathbf C$-envelope of $M$.

We are now in a position to construct a functor adjoint to $T$: every
object $N$ of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ can be considered as an object of
$\mathbf A$; we choose a $\mathbf C$-envelope of this object, say
$u(N)\colon N\to SN$. Every object $M$ of $\mathbf A$ then gives rise to
the following maps:
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TM,N)
  =\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TM,TN)
  \xrightarrow{\;\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TM,u(N))\;}
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TM,TSN)
  \xleftarrow{\;T(M,SN)\;}
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M,SN).
\]
These applications are bijective and they define a functorial isomorphism
from $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(T.,N)$ to
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(.,SN)$. In other words,
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(T.,N)$ is a representable functor, and
the proposition results from Proposition~10 (Chapter~I).

Suppose now that $\mathbf C$ is a localizing subcategory of $\mathbf A$
and consider a morphism $u\colon M\to N$ of $\mathbf A$. Let $M'$
(resp.\ $N'$) be the largest subobject of $M$ (resp.\ of $N$)
belonging to $\mathbf C$. Since $u(M')$ is contained in $N'$, $u$
induces a morphism $u'\colon M/M'\to N/N'$. One has then the following
assertion, which will be very useful in paragraph~3.

\begin{lemma}
With the notations and hypotheses above, $Tu$ is an essential extension
of $TM$ if and only if $u'$ is an essential extension of $M/M'$.
\end{lemma}

Since $T(M/M')$ and $T(N/N')$ are isomorphisms, $Tu$ is an essential
extension if and only if the same holds for $Tu'$. In other words, it
suffices to prove the result when $M'$ and $N'$ are zero. We make this
hypothesis in the sequel, and we choose the subobjects of $TN$ (resp.\ of
$TM$) among the $TP$, where $P$ is a subobject of $N$ (resp.\ of $M$).

Suppose that $u$ is an essential extension of $M$, and let $TP$ be a
subobject of $TN$. If $TP$ is not zero, the same holds for $P$ and for
$u^{-1}(P)$. The latter object does not belong to $\mathbf C$. Since
$(Tu)^{-1}(TP)$ is isomorphic to $T(u^{-1}(P))$, it follows that
$(Tu)^{-1}(TP)$ is not zero and that $Tu$ is an essential extension of
$TM$.

Conversely, suppose that $Tu$ is an essential extension and let $P$ be a
nonzero subobject of $N$. Since every subobject of $N$ belonging to
$\mathbf C$ is zero, $TP$ cannot be zero. It follows that
$u^{-1}(P)$ is not zero.

\begin{lemma}
Let $\mathbf C$ be a localizing subcategory of $\mathbf A$ and let
$(U_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of generators of $\mathbf A$. Then
$(TU_i)_{i\in I}$ is a family of generators of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$.
\end{lemma}

Let indeed $u\colon M\to N$ be a monomorphism of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$
which is not an isomorphism. We must prove that the map
\[
  \prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TU_i,M)
  \longrightarrow
  \prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TU_i,N)
\]
induced by $u$ is not surjective. Now $Su$ is not an isomorphism
(Proposition~3~(a)). It follows that the map
\[
  \prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(U_i,SM)
  \longrightarrow
  \prod_{i\in I}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(U_i,SN)
\]
induced by $Su$ is not surjective. The lemma is a consequence of this
fact and of the properties of adjoint functors.

\begin{lemma}
Let $\mathbf C$ be a localizing subcategory of a
$\Univ$-category with generators $\mathbf A$. Then
$\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ is a $\Univ$-category with generators.
\end{lemma}

Let indeed $M$ and $N$ be two objects of $\mathbf A$. According to
Proposition~5 (Chapter~I), the set of subobjects $M'$ of $M$ such that
$M/M'$ belongs to $\mathbf C$ can be indexed by a set belonging to
$\Univ$. The same holds for the set of subobjects $N'$ of $N$ belonging
to $\mathbf C$. It follows that the inductive limit
$\varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N')$ is an object of the
category $\Univ\mathbf{Ab}$. This proves that $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ is a
$\Univ$-category.

The existence of generators results from Lemma~4.

The ``nature'' harbours many localizing subcategories. The following
proposition sometimes allows one to detect them: let $\mathbf A$ and
$\mathbf B$ be two abelian categories, let $T_1$ be a functor from
$\mathbf A$ into $\mathbf B$ and let $S_1$ be a functor adjoint to
$T_1$. We keep for the letters $\Phi_1$, $T_1$, $\varphi_1$ and
$\psi_1$ their usual meaning (cf.\ Chapter~I, \S7).

\begin{proposition}
Suppose, with the above notation, that $T_1$ is an exact functor and
that $\Phi_1$ is an isomorphism from $T_1\circ S_1$ to
$I_{\mathbf B}$. Then $\operatorname{Ker}T_1$ is a localizing
subcategory of $\mathbf A$ and $T_1$ induces an equivalence between
$\mathbf A/\operatorname{Ker}T_1$ and $\mathbf B$.
\end{proposition}

Let indeed $T$ be the canonical functor from $\mathbf A$ to
$\mathbf A/\operatorname{Ker}T_1$; ($\operatorname{Ker}T_1$ denotes the
thick subcategory of $\mathbf A$ whose objects are annihilated by
$T_1$); let $R$ be the unique functor from
$\mathbf A/\operatorname{Ker}T_1$ to $\mathbf B$ such that
$T_1=R\circ T$. If $f$ is a morphism of $\mathbf A$, then $T_1 f$ is an
isomorphism if and only if $T_1\operatorname{Ker}f$ and
$T_1\operatorname{Coker}f$ are zero, that is, if and only if $Tf$ is an
isomorphism. We apply this remark to the special case in which $f$ is the
morphism $\Psi_1(M)$ from $M$ into $(S_1\circ T_1)M$. We indeed know
that the composite of $T_1\Psi_1(M)$ and of $\Phi_1(T_1 M)$ is the
identity morphism of $T_1 M$. Since $\Phi_1(T_1 M)$ is an isomorphism,
it follows that $T_1\Psi_1(M)$, and hence $T\Psi_1(M)$, are
isomorphisms. Since moreover the objects $M$ of $\mathbf A$ coincide with
the objects $TM$ of $\mathbf A/\operatorname{Ker}T_1$, the morphisms
$T_1\Psi_1(M)$ define an isomorphism from the identity functor of
$\mathbf A/\operatorname{Ker}T_1$ to the composite functor $TS_1 R$. We
deduce from this that $TS_1 R$ and $R TS_1$ are isomorphic to the
identity functors of $\mathbf A/\operatorname{Ker}T_1$ and of
$\mathbf B$. This means that $R$ and $TS_1$ define an equivalence of
$\mathbf A/\operatorname{Ker}T_1$ with $\mathbf B$.

We leave to the reader the proof that $S_1\circ R$ is a functor adjoint
to $T$, which shows that $\operatorname{Ker}T_1$ is a localizing
subcategory of $\mathbf A$.
