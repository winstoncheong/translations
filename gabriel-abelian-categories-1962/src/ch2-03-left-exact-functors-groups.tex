\section{Left Exact Functors with Values in Abelian Groups}
--- In this paragraph, $\Cat$ is still a category whose set of morphisms and
set of objects belong to $\Univ$. We shall complete the study of the
preceding paragraph when $\mathbf{D}$ is the category $\Ab$ of abelian groups.

If $X$ is an object of $\Cat$, $\operatorname{Hom}_{\Cat}(X,\cdot)$ is a left
exact functor of $\Cat$ into $\Ab$, which we shall also denote by $\dot{X}$.
With these notations, the functor $X\leadsto \dot{X}$ of $\Cat^{\circ}$
into $\operatorname{Fonct}(\Cat,\Ab)$ is itself left exact.

\begin{proposition}
--- The functor $X\leadsto \dot{X}$ is an exact functor of $\Cat^{\circ}$
into $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat,\Ab)$. The functors $\dot{X}$ form a family of
generators of $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat,\Ab)$.
\end{proposition}

We already know that $X\leadsto \dot{X}$ is a left exact functor of
$\Cat^{\circ}$ into $\operatorname{Fonct}(\Cat,\Ab)$. It remains to prove
that every monomorphism $u\colon X\to X'$ induces an epimorphism
$\dot{u}\colon \dot{X}'\to \dot{X}$. This means that
$R^{0}(\operatorname{Coker}\dot{u})$ or $R(\operatorname{Coker}\dot{u})$ are
null, which can be stated as follows: for every object $A$ of $\Cat$ and
every $a\in \dot{X}A$, there exists a monomorphism $i\colon A\to B$ and
$b\in \dot{X}'B$ such that
\[
  (\dot{X}i)(a) = \dot{u}(B)(b).
\]
This last assertion is more or less clear. Finally, the second part of the
proposition presents no difficulty, for the $\dot{X}$ already form a family
of generators of $\operatorname{Fonct}(\Cat,\Ab)$.

\begin{corollary}
--- Every injective object $F$ of $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat,\Ab)$ is an exact
functor of $\Cat$ into $\Ab$.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, if $F$ is an injective object, the functor $X\leadsto FX$ is the
composite of two exact functors $X\leadsto \dot{X}$ and
$G\leadsto \operatorname{Hom}(G,F)$.

\begin{corollary}
--- In the category $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat,\Ab)$ every subobject of a
representable functor is an upper bound of representable functors.
\end{corollary}

Let then $Y$ be an object of $\Cat$, and let $i\colon F\to \dot{Y}$ be a
nonzero monomorphism of $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat,\Ab)$. By Proposition 6
there exists a nonzero morphism $\varphi\colon \dot{X}\to F$; moreover
$\operatorname{Im}\varphi = \operatorname{Im}(i\circ\varphi)$ is
representable, which proves that $F$ contains nonzero representable
subobjects. I say that the upper bound $G$ of these subobjects is $F$: in
the contrary case one could indeed choose $\varphi$ in such a way that
$\operatorname{Im}\varphi$ is not a subobject of $G$; this is absurd.
