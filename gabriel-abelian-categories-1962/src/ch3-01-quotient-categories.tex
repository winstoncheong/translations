\section{Quotient Categories}

--- Recall that a full subcategory $\mathbf C$ of the abelian category
$\mathbf A$ is said to be \emph{thick} (\emph{épaisse}) if the following
condition is satisfied \textup{[10]}: for every exact sequence of
$\mathbf A$ of the form $(*)$, the object $M$ is an object of
$\mathbf C$ if and only if $M'$ and $M''$ are objects of $\mathbf C$:
\[
(*)\qquad 0 \to M' \to M \to M'' \to 0.
\]

The data of $\mathbf A$ and $\mathbf C$ allow one to construct a new
abelian category, which we shall denote by $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ and
which is called the \emph{quotient category} of $\mathbf A$ by
$\mathbf C$ \textup{[10]}:

\begin{itemize}
\item The objects of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ coincide with the objects of
      $\mathbf A$.

\item If $M$ and $N$ are two objects of $\mathbf A$, $M'$ and $N'$ two
      subobjects of $M$ and $N$, the canonical morphisms $M'\to M$ and
      $N\to N/N'$ define a linear map
      \[
        \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M,N)\longrightarrow
        \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N').
      \]
      When $M/M'$ and $N'$ are objects of $\mathbf C$, the abelian
      groups $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N')$ form an evident
      inductive system. We set by definition
      \[
        \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A/\mathbf C}(M,N) :=
        \varinjlim \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N').
      \]
      The set $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A/\mathbf C}(M,N)$ is therefore
      endowed with a structure of an abelian group.

\item It remains to define bilinear laws of composition:
      \[
        \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A/\mathbf C}(M,N)\times
        \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A/\mathbf C}(N,P)\longrightarrow
        \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A/\mathbf C}(M,P).
      \]
\end{itemize}

For this, let $f$ be an element of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A/\mathbf C}(M,N)$ and let $g$ be an
element of $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A/\mathbf C}(N,P)$. The element
$f$ is the image of a morphism $f:M'\to N/N'$ where $M/M'$ and $N'$ are
objects of $\mathbf C$. Likewise, $g$ is the image of a morphism
$g:N''\to P/P'$ where $N/N''$ and $P'$ are objects of $\mathbf C$. If
$M''$ denotes the inverse image $f^{-1}((N''+N')/N')$, it is easy to see
that $M/M''$ belongs to $\mathbf C$; we denote by $f''$ the morphism from
$M''$ into $(N''+N')/N'$ induced by $f$. Likewise, $N''\cap N'$
belongs to $\mathbf C$; if $P''$ denotes the sum
$P'+g(N''\cap N')$, it is easy to see that $P''$ belongs to
$\mathbf C$; we denote by $g'$ the morphism from
$N''/(N''\cap N')$ into $P/P''$ induced by $g$. Let $h$ be the
composite of $f''$, of the canonical isomorphism from
$(N''+N')/N'$ onto $N''/(N''\cap N')$, and of $g'$. The image
$\bar h$ of $h$ in
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A/\mathbf C}(M,P)$ depends only on
$f$ and $g$, and not on the chosen lifts. It is therefore legitimate to
define the laws of composition of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ by the formula
$g\circ f=h$. These laws of composition are bilinear; they make
$\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ a category.

In what follows, the letter $T$ will denote the (so-called canonical)
functor from $\mathbf A$ into $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ defined as follows:
$TM=M$ for every object $M$ of $\mathbf A$; if $f:M\to N$ is a morphism
of $\mathbf A$, $Tf$ is the image of $f$ in the inductive limit
$\varinjlim \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N')$.

\begin{lemma}
The category $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ is an additive category and $T$ is an
additive functor from $\mathbf A$ into $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$.
\end{lemma}

This lemma is a direct consequence of Propositions~2 and~3 of
Chapter~I.

\begin{lemma}
Let $u:M\to N$ be a morphism of $\mathbf A$. The morphism $Tu$ is zero
(resp. is a monomorphism, resp. is an epimorphism) if and only if
$\operatorname{Im}u$ (resp. $\operatorname{Ker}u$, resp.
$\operatorname{Coker}u$) belongs to $\mathbf C$.
\end{lemma}

If $\operatorname{Im}u$ belongs to $\mathbf C$, the image of $u$ in
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M,N/\operatorname{Im}u)$ is indeed zero.
The same holds, \emph{a fortiori}, for the image of $u$ in the inductive
limit of the groups $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N')$.
Conversely, if $Tu$ is zero, one may choose the subobjects $M'$ and
$N'$ so that the morphism from $M'$ into $N/N'$ induced by $u$ is
zero. This means that $u(M')$ is contained in $N'$, which belongs to
$\mathbf C$, and therefore that $u(M')$ belongs to $\mathbf C$. As one
has, moreover, an exact sequence of the form
\[
0 \to u(M') \to \operatorname{Im}u \to M/(M'+\operatorname{Ker}u) \to 0,
\]
it follows that $\operatorname{Im}u$ belongs to $\mathbf C$.

Suppose now that $Tu$ is a monomorphism; let $i$ be the canonical
monomorphism from $\operatorname{Ker}u$ into $M$. Since $u\circ i$ is
zero, the same holds for $Tu\circ Ti$. From this one deduces that $Ti$ is
zero, hence that $\operatorname{Ker}u$ belongs to $\mathbf C$.
Conversely, suppose that $\operatorname{Ker}u$ belongs to $\mathbf C$.
Let $f:TP\to TM$ be a non-zero morphism of
$\mathbf A/\mathbf C$: this morphism is the image of a morphism
$f:P'\to M/M'$ where $P/P'$ and $M'$ belong to $\mathbf C$. By replacing
$M'$ with $M'+\operatorname{Ker}u$, we may suppose moreover that $M'$
contains $\operatorname{Ker}u$. In this case $u$ induces a
monomorphism $u'$ from $M/M'$ into $N/u(M')$. Since $f$ is non-zero,
$\operatorname{Im}f$ and $\operatorname{Im}(u'\circ f)$ do not belong
to $\mathbf C$. This shows that $(Tu)\circ f$ is non-zero and that $Tu$ is
a monomorphism.

The last assertion of Lemma~2 is proved in an analogous way.

\begin{lemma}
Let $u:M\to N$ be a morphism of $\mathbf A$, $i:K\to M$ the kernel
of $u$ and $p:N\to C$ the cokernel of $u$. The morphism $Tu$ has a
kernel (resp. a cokernel); moreover $Ti$ (resp. $Tp$) induces an
isomorphism from $TK$ onto the kernel of $Tu$ (resp. from the cokernel
of $Tu$ onto $TC$).
\end{lemma}

We already know that $Ti$ is a monomorphism. Let therefore
$f:TX\to TM$ be a morphism such that $Tu\circ f=0$: we must prove the
existence of a morphism $g:TX\to TK$ such that $Ti\circ g=f$. Now $f$
is the image of an element $f$ of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(X',M/M')$ where $X/X'$ and $M'$ belong
to $\mathbf C$. We are therefore in the presence of the following
commutative diagram:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
K \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & M \arrow[r, "u"] \arrow[d] & N \arrow[d] \\
K/K\cap M' \arrow[r] & M/M' \arrow[r] & N/u(M')
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $i_1$, $q$ and $r$ are the canonical morphisms. Since
$Tu\circ f$ is zero, the image of $X'$ under the induced morphism
$u':M/M'\to N/u(M')$ belongs to $\mathbf C$. If $X''$ denotes the
inverse image of $\operatorname{Ker}u'$ by $f$, it follows that
$X'/X''$ and $X/X''$ belong to $\mathbf C$. Moreover, the restriction of
$f$ to $X''$ factors through a morphism
$g:X''\to K/K\cap M'$ composed with $i_1$. If $\tilde g$ is the image
of $g$ in $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A/\mathbf C}(X,K)$, we have the
desired equality $Ti\circ\tilde g=f$. The second part of the lemma is
proved in a ``dual'' way.

\begin{lemma}
Let $u:M\to N$ be a morphism of $\mathbf A$. Then $Tu$ is an
isomorphism if and only if $\operatorname{Ker}u$ and
$\operatorname{Coker}u$ belong to $\mathbf C$.
\end{lemma}

If $Tu$ is an isomorphism, it is both a monomorphism and an
epimorphism. By Lemma~2, it follows that $\operatorname{Ker}u$ and
$\operatorname{Coker}u$ are objects of $\mathbf C$. Conversely, suppose
that this last condition is satisfied; let $q$ be the canonical
epimorphism from $M$ onto $\operatorname{Coim}u$, $j$ the canonical
morphism from $\operatorname{Im}u$ into $N$ and $s$ the canonical
isomorphism from $\operatorname{Coim}u$ onto $\operatorname{Im}u$. The
identity morphism of $\operatorname{Coim}u$ is an element of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(\operatorname{Coim}u,
M/i(\operatorname{Ker}u))$. This element has an image $q^*$ in
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A/\mathbf C}(\operatorname{Coim}u,M)$ and
it is clear that $q^*$ is an inverse morphism of $Tq$. This shows that
$Tq$ is an isomorphism. In the same way $Tj$ is an isomorphism. It
follows that $Tu=Tj\circ Ts\circ Tq$ is an isomorphism.

\begin{proposition}
If $\mathbf C$ is a thick subcategory of $\mathbf A$, the category
$\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ is abelian. Moreover, the canonical functor from
$\mathbf A$ into $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ is exact.
\end{proposition}

Let indeed $f:TM\to TN$ be a morphism of
$\mathbf A/\mathbf C$. We show that $f$ has a kernel, a cokernel, a
coimage, an image and that the canonical morphism from the coimage into
the image is an isomorphism.

Now $f$ is the image of an element $f$ of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N')$, where $M/M'$ and $N'$ are
objects of $\mathbf C$. From this one deduces the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
TM \arrow[r, "f"] & TN \arrow[d, "Tq_{N'}"] \\
TM' \arrow[u, "Ti_{M'}"] \arrow[r, "Tf'"] & T(N/N')
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $Ti_{M'}$ and $Tq_{N'}$ are isomorphisms. This diagram shows that
$f$ has a kernel, a cokernel, etc., if and only if the same is true of
$Tf$. In other words, one may suppose that $f$ is of the form $Tf$.

In this case Lemma~3 shows that $f$ has a kernel, a cokernel, a coimage
and an image. We denote by $q$, $j$ and $s$ respectively the canonical
morphisms from $M$ into $\operatorname{Coim}f$, from
$\operatorname{Im}f$ into $N$ and from $\operatorname{Coim}f$ into
$\operatorname{Im}f$. Lemma~3 shows that $Tq$ induces an isomorphism
$q'$ from $\operatorname{Coim}Tf$ onto
$T(\operatorname{Coim}f)$; likewise, $Tj$ induces an isomorphism $j'$
from $T(\operatorname{Im}f)$ onto $\operatorname{Im}Tf$. Finally, it is
easy to verify that the canonical morphism from
$\operatorname{Coim}Tf$ into $\operatorname{Im}Tf$ is the composite
$j'\circ Ts\circ q'$; this morphism is therefore an isomorphism.

The last assertion of
proposition 1\ednote{The original prints ``proposition~5''; we print
``proposition~1'', the only one in scope.} follows directly from
Lemma~3.

\begin{corollary}
Let $\mathbf C$ be a thick subcategory of $\mathbf A$ and let
\[
0 \to M \to N \to P \to 0
\]
be an exact sequence of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$. There then exists a
diagram of the form (i), commutative, exact and satisfying the following
conditions: $u$, $v$ and $w$ are isomorphisms of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$;
moreover
\[
0 \to M_1 \to N_1 \to P_1 \to 0
\]
is an exact sequence of $\mathbf A$.
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
0 \arrow[r] & M \arrow[r] & N \arrow[r] & P \arrow[r] & 0 \\
0 \arrow[r] & TM_1 \arrow[r] \arrow[u, "u"] & TN_1 \arrow[r] \arrow[u, "v"] & TP_1 \arrow[r] \arrow[u, "w"] & 0
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The morphism $f$ is indeed the image of an element
$f'\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M,N/N')$, where $M/M'$ and $N'$
belong to $\mathbf C$. Since $Tf$ is a monomorphism, $\operatorname{Ker}f'$
belongs to $\mathbf C$. If $q$ denotes the canonical morphism from
$M$ onto $\operatorname{Coim}f'$, it is therefore possible to make the
following choices: $M_1=\operatorname{Coim}f'$; $N_1=N/N'$;
$f_1=$ morphism induced by $f'$; $P_1=\operatorname{Coker}f_1$;
$g_1=$ canonical morphism; $u=(Tq)\circ(Ti)^{-1}$; $v=Tq_{N'}$;
$w=$ morphism induced by $(Tg_1)\circ v$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}
Let $\mathbf C$ be a thick subcategory of $\mathbf A$ and $G$ an
exact functor from $\mathbf A$ into an abelian category $\mathbf D$. If
$GM$ is zero for every object $M$ of $\mathbf C$, there exists one and
only one functor $H$ from $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ into $\mathbf D$ such
that one has $G=H\circ T$.
\end{corollary}

Let indeed $M$ and $N$ be two objects of $\mathbf A$, $M'$ and $N'$
subobjects of $M$ and $N$ such that $M/M'$ and $N'$ belong to
$\mathbf C$. Consider the following commutative diagram, where
$\varphi$ and $\psi$ denote respectively the maps
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M,N)\to
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N')$ and
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf D}(GM,GN)\to
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf D}(GM',G(N/N'))$:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M,N) \arrow[r, "G"] \arrow[d] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf D}(GM,GN) \arrow[d, "\varphi"] \\
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N') \arrow[r, "G"] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf D}(GM',G(N/N'))
\end{tikzcd}
\]
It is clear that $\varphi$ is a bijective map; the maps
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N')\to
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf D}(GM,GN)$ induced by $G$ and by
$\varphi^{-1}$ therefore define an application $H(M,N)$ from
$\varinjlim \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf A}(M',N/N')$ into
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf D}(GM,GN)$. When $M$ and $N$ range over
the objects of $\mathbf A$ (or of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$), the
applications $H(M,N)$ determine a functor $H$ from
$\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ into $\mathbf D$; this functor associates to
every object $M$ of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ the object $GM$ of
$\mathbf D$; it satisfies the equality $G=H\circ T$. The proof of the
uniqueness of $H$ is left to the reader.

\begin{corollary}
Let $\mathbf C$ be a thick subcategory of $\mathbf A$ and $H$ a functor
from $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$ into an abelian category $\mathbf D$. The
functor $H$ is exact if and only if the functor $H\circ T$ is exact.
\end{corollary}

It is clear that $H\circ T$ is exact if $H$ is exact. Conversely,
suppose that $H\circ T$ is exact. If
\[
0 \to M \to N \to P \to 0
\]
is an exact sequence of $\mathbf A/\mathbf C$, we consider a diagram
of the form (i). As the sequence
\[
0 \to HTM_1 \to HTN_1 \to HTP_1 \to 0
\]
is exact, the same holds for the sequence
\[
0 \to HM \to HN \to HP \to 0.
\]
