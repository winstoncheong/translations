\section{Abelian Categories with Enough Injectives}
--- This paragraph is devoted to a few results about the injective objects
of an abelian category $\mathbf{B}$. These results are connected with the
notion of \emph{derived category}, which is due to \textsc{Cartier} and of
which we present here a ``piece'':

Let $\mathbf{I}$ be an additive category and let $\mathbf{M}$ be the
\emph{category of morphisms} of $\mathbf{I}$: an object of $\mathbf{M}$ is a
morphism of $\mathbf{I}$; if $d\colon M\to N$ and $d'\colon M'\to N'$ are two
objects of $\mathbf{M}$, then $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{M}}(d,d')$ is the
set of pairs $(\alpha,\beta)$, $\alpha\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{I}}(M,M')$,
$\beta\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{I}}(N,N')$, such that the following diagram
is commutative:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
M \arrow[r, "d"] \arrow[d, "\alpha"'] & N \arrow[d, "\beta"] \\
M' \arrow[r, "d'"'] & N'
\end{tikzcd}
\]

It is well known that by defining the composition of morphisms in the obvious
way, one makes $\mathbf{M}$ into an additive category.

With the above notations, we shall say that $(\alpha,\beta)$ is
\emph{homotopic to zero} if there is an element $h$ of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{I}}(N,M')$ such that $\alpha = h\circ d$. The
morphisms homotopic to zero form a subgroup of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{M}}(d,d')$, and we denote by
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}}(d,d')$ the quotient of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{M}}(d,d')$ by this subgroup. It is then clear that
the bilinear maps
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{M}}(d,d') \times
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{M}}(d',d'') \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{M}}(d,d'')
\]
define, by passing to the quotient, the bilinear maps
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}}(d,d') \times
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}}(d',d'') \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}}(d,d'').
\]

We can therefore define a new additive category $\mathbf{KI}$: the objects of
$\mathbf{KI}$ coincide with the morphisms of $\mathbf{I}$ (hence with the
objects of $\mathbf{M}$); if $d$ and $d'$ are such morphisms,
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{KI}}(d,d')$ is chosen equal to
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}}(d,d')$; the laws of composition, finally, are
defined by passing to the quotient from the laws of composition of
$\mathbf{M}$.

In the case which interests us, $\mathbf{I}$ is a full subcategory of
$\mathbf{B}$ whose objects are injectives of $\mathbf{B}$. We denote by
$\operatorname{Ker}$ the functor $d\mapsto\operatorname{Ker}d$ of $\mathbf{M}$
into $\mathbf{B}$. If $d$ and $d'$ are two objects of $\mathbf{M}$, it is clear
that the maps $\operatorname{Ker}(d,d')$ from
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{M}}(d,d')$ into
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(\operatorname{Ker}d,\operatorname{Ker}d')$
define, by passing to the quotient, maps from
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{K}}(d,d')$ into
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(\operatorname{Ker}d,\operatorname{Ker}d')$.
These maps define in fact a functor (still denoted $\operatorname{Ker}$!) of
$\mathbf{KI}$ into $\mathbf{B}$.

\begin{proposition}
Let $\mathbf{B}$ be an abelian category, and let $\mathbf{I}$ be a full
subcategory of $\mathbf{B}$ whose objects are injectives of $\mathbf{B}$.
Suppose the following condition is satisfied: for every object $M$ of
$\mathbf{B}$, there exists a monomorphism of $M$ into an object of
$\mathbf{I}$. Then the functor $\operatorname{Ker}$ defines an equivalence
between $\mathbf{KI}$ and $\mathbf{B}$.
\end{proposition}

Let us say quickly how to construct a functor $S$ adjoint to $\operatorname{Ker}$.
For every object $M$ of $\mathbf{B}$, we choose an exact sequence
\[
  0 \longrightarrow M \xrightarrow{j_M} I_0M \xrightarrow{g_M} I_1M,
\]
where $I_0M$ and $I_1M$ are objects belonging to $\mathbf{I}$. For every
morphism $u\colon M\to N$, we choose morphisms $u_0$ and $u_1$ which make the
following diagram commutative
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
0 \arrow[r] & M \arrow[r, "{j_M}"] \arrow[d, "u"'] &
I_0M \arrow[r, "{g_M}"] \arrow[d, "u_0"'] & I_1M \arrow[d, "u_1"] \\
0 \arrow[r] & N \arrow[r, "{j_N}"'] &
I_0N \arrow[r, "{g_N}"] & I_1N
\end{tikzcd}
\]

One sees easily that the image of $(u_0,u_1)$ in
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{KI}}(g_M,g_N)$ depends only on $u$. If this image
is denoted $Su$ and if $SM$ denotes the morphism $g_M$, the maps
$M\mapsto SM$ and $u\mapsto Su$ define a functor adjoint to
$\operatorname{Ker}$.

\begin{corollary}
Suppose that the hypotheses of the preceding proposition are satisfied. If
the category $\mathbf{I}$ is equivalent to the product of a family
$(\mathbf{I}_n)_{n\in E}$ of additive categories, then the category
$\mathbf{B}$ is equivalent to the product of the family
$(\mathbf{KI}_n)_{n\in E}$.
\end{corollary}

Let $\mathbf{C}$ now be another abelian category. Every additive functor $F$
of $\mathbf{I}$ into $\mathbf{C}$ extends in an obvious way to a functor $KF$
of $\mathbf{KI}$ into $\mathbf{KC}$. By composition, we obtain a left exact
functor $F'$ of $\mathbf{B}$ into $\mathbf{C}$:
\[
  \mathbf{B} \xrightarrow{S} \mathbf{KI} \xrightarrow{KF} \mathbf{KC}
  \xrightarrow{\operatorname{Ker}} \mathbf{C}.
\]
Conversely, if $G$ is a functor of $\mathbf{B}$ into $\mathbf{C}$, we denote by
$G'$ the composite of the canonical functor of $\mathbf{I}$ into $\mathbf{B}$
and of $G$.

\begin{corollary}
Suppose that the hypotheses of the preceding proposition are satisfied. A
functor $G$ of $\mathbf{B}$ into $\mathbf{C}$ is left exact if and only if it
is isomorphic to $(G')'$. Every functor $F$ of $\mathbf{I}$ into $\mathbf{C}$
is isomorphic to $(F')'$.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~2 shows that the study of the left exact functors of $\mathbf{B}$
into $\mathbf{C}$ is equivalent to the study of all the additive functors of
$\mathbf{I}$ into $\mathbf{C}$. We shall make use of this fact in
Corollary~3 and at the beginning of Chapter~II.

\begin{corollary}
Let $\mathbf{B}$ (resp.\ $\mathbf{C}$) be an abelian category, $\mathbf{I}$
(resp.\ $\mathbf{J}$) a full subcategory of $\mathbf{B}$ (resp.\ of
$\mathbf{C}$) satisfying the conditions of Proposition~14. A functor $G$ of
$\mathbf{B}$ into $\mathbf{C}$ defines an equivalence between these two
categories if the following conditions are satisfied: $G$ is left exact;
$GI$ is an object of $\mathbf{J}$ for every object $I$ of $\mathbf{I}$; the
functor $I\mapsto GI$ defines an equivalence between $\mathbf{I}$ and
$\mathbf{J}$.
\end{corollary}

We shall henceforth say that $\mathbf{B}$ has enough injective objects if the
full subcategory of $\mathbf{B}$ which is formed by the injective objects
satisfies the condition of Proposition~14. When this holds, Proposition~14
shows that the datum of this full subcategory defines $\mathbf{B}$ up to an
equivalence. Proposition~14 is therefore useful each time that we know the
injective objects of $\mathbf{B}$ (cf.\ Chapter~IV).

Let us return to the hypotheses of Corollary~2. With the notations used in the
proof of Proposition~14, we see that $(G')'$ is nothing but the functor
$M\mapsto\operatorname{Ker}(Gg_M)$. It follows that $(G')'$ is the zeroth
derived functor of $G$ [6]. Moreover, the morphisms $Gj_M$ define a natural
transformation $\rho\colon G\to (G')' = R^{0}G$, and the pair\ednote{The
original prints $Gf_M$ here; no $f_M$ is defined — the resolution above
provides $j_M\colon M\to I_0M$ and $g_M\colon I_0M\to I_1M$, and
$GM\to\ker(Gg_M)$ comes from $Gj_M$. We print $Gj_M$.}
$(R^{0}G,\rho)$ satisfies the conditions of Proposition~15:

\begin{proposition}
Let $\mathbf{B}$ be an abelian category having enough injective objects. For
every functor $G\colon\mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{C}$ of $\mathbf{B}$ into an abelian
category $\mathbf{C}$, there exist a functor $R^{0}G$ and a morphism
$\rho\colon G\to R^{0}G$ satisfying the following conditions:

The functor $R^{0}G$ is left exact; moreover, if $\sigma\colon G\to H$ is a
morphism of $G$ into a left exact functor $H$, there exists a unique morphism
$\tau\colon R^{0}G\to H$ such that $\sigma = \tau\circ\rho$.
\end{proposition}

Let $\rho'\colon G\to R'G$ be a natural transformation satisfying the same
universal problem as $\rho\colon G\to R^{0}G$; it is then clear that there
exists a unique isomorphism $u$ of $R'G$ onto $R^{0}G$\ednote{The original
prints $F$ in both places; $G$ is the functor in play, as every other
occurrence in this proof shows. We print $G$.} such that
$\rho = u\circ\rho'$. Proposition~15 therefore determines $R^{0}G$ up to a
natural isomorphism. We shall try to extend these results when $\mathbf{B}$
does not have enough injective objects.
