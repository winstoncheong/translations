\section{Noetherian Categories}
--- Let $\Cat$ be an abelian category. An object $M$ of $\Cat$ is called
\emph{noetherian} (resp.\ \emph{artinian}) if every increasing (resp.\
decreasing) sequence of subobjects of $M$ is stationary. The following lemma
is easy to prove:

\begin{lemma}
--- Let $M$ be an object of $\Cat$, $N$ a subobject of $M$. Then $M$ is
noetherian (resp.\ artinian) if and only if $N$ and $M/N$ are noetherian
(resp.\ artinian).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}
--- Let $\Cat$ be a category with generators and exact inductive limits,
$M$ a noetherian object of $\Cat$\ednote{The original sets this $C$ in
italics, where the category, written $\mathbf{C}$ everywhere else, is meant.
We print it bold, as the category.}, $(P_i)_{i\in I}$ a filtered increasing
family of subobjects of an object $P$ ($I\in\Univ$). Then the application
\[
  \varphi_M\colon \sup_i \operatorname{Hom}(M,P_i)
    \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}\Bigl(M,\sup_i P_i\Bigr)
\]
is bijective.
\end{lemma}

It suffices to show that $\varphi_M$ is surjective. Let then $u$ be a
morphism from $M$ into $\sup_i P_i$. The equality
$u^{-1}(\sup_i P_i) = \sup_i u^{-1}(P_i)$ shows that $u^{-1}(P_i)$ is equal
to $M$ for $i$ large enough. This proves the lemma.

One proves the dual statement in the same way:

\begin{lemma}
--- Let $\Cat$ be a category with cogenerators and exact projective limits,
$M$ an artinian object of $\Cat$, $(P_i)_{i\in I}$ a filtered decreasing
family of subobjects of an object $P$ ($I\in\Univ$). Then the application
\[
  \varphi_M\colon \sup_i \operatorname{Hom}(P/P_i,M)
    \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}\Bigl(P/\operatorname*{Inf}_i
    P_i,M\Bigr)
\]
is bijective.
\end{lemma}

We shall henceforth say that an abelian category $\Cat$ is \emph{noetherian}
(resp.\ \emph{artinian}) if the following conditions are satisfied:

\begin{itemize}
\item[---] Every object of $\Cat$ is noetherian (resp.\ artinian).
\item[---] There exists a family $(M_i)_{i\in I}$ of objects of $\Cat$ such
      that $I$ is an element of $\Univ$ and that every object of $\Cat$ is
      isomorphic to an object of the family.
\end{itemize}

The second condition merely ensures that the universe $\Univ$ has been chosen
large enough. It follows that every noetherian (resp.\ artinian) category is
equivalent to a noetherian (resp.\ artinian) category whose set of objects
belongs to $\Univ$.

An abelian category which is both noetherian and artinian will be called
\emph{finite}: all the objects of such a category are of finite length.

We shall say that an abelian category $\Cat$ is \emph{locally noetherian} if
the following conditions are satisfied:

\begin{itemize}
\item[---] $\Cat$ is a category with exact inductive limits.
\item[---] There exists a family $(M_i)_{i\in I}$ of noetherian generators
      of $\Cat$ whose set of indices $I$ is an element of $\Univ$.
\end{itemize}

The second condition means that the noetherian objects of $\Cat$ form a
noetherian category, and that every object $M$ is an upper bound of its
noetherian subobjects. If there exists a family $(M_i)_{i\in I}$ of
generators of finite length, we shall say that $\Cat$ is \emph{locally
finite}.

Now let $\Cat$ be an artinian category and suppose, to simplify, that the
set $\mathbf{OC}$ of the objects of $\Cat$ is an element of $\Univ$. If $X$
is an object of $\Cat$, every filtered increasing family of representable
subobjects of $\dot{X}$ contains a maximal element. Corollary 2 of
Proposition 6 implies that every subobject of $\dot{X}$ belonging to
$\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat,\Ab)$ is representable. Then every quotient of a
representable functor is representable, every representable functor is
noetherian, every functor is an upper bound of representable functors, every
noetherian functor is representable.

\begin{proposition}
--- If $\Cat$ is an artinian category, $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat,\Ab)$ is a
locally noetherian category.
\end{proposition}

We have already established the proposition when the set $\mathbf{OC}$
belongs to $\Univ$. In the general case $\Cat$ is equivalent to an artinian
category $\Cat'$ such that $\mathbf{OC}'$ belongs to $\Univ$. Moreover the
category $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat,\Ab)$ is equivalent to
$\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat',\Ab)$. The proposition follows.

\begin{theorem}
--- (cf.\ [11], [19].) Let $\Cat$ be a noetherian category. There exists a
locally noetherian category $\mathbf{D}$ such that $\Cat$ is equivalent to
the category of noetherian objects of $\mathbf{D}$. Moreover this condition
determines $\mathbf{D}$ up to an equivalence.
\end{theorem}

Indeed, the dual category $\Cat^{\circ}$ is artinian. It follows from the
preceding remarks that $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat^{\circ},\Ab)$ is a locally
noetherian category and that the functor
$X\leadsto \operatorname{Hom}_{\Cat}(.,X)$ defines an equivalence of
$\Cat$ with the category formed of the noetherian objects of
$\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat^{\circ},\Ab)$.

It remains to prove the uniqueness of $\mathbf{D}$ by showing that $\mathbf{D}$
is necessarily equivalent to the category
$\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat^{\circ},\Ab)$. We shall suppose, to simplify, that
$\Cat$ is the category of the noetherian objects of $\mathbf{D}$. We then
associate to every object $X$ of $\mathbf{D}$ the left exact functor
$Y\leadsto \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(Y,X)$ of $\Cat^{\circ}$ into
$\Ab$; this defines a functor $T\colon
X\leadsto \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(.,X)$ of $\mathbf{D}$ into
$\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat^{\circ},\Ab)$. We complete the proof by showing
that $T$ defines an equivalence:

\begin{itemize}
\item[---] For this, we first show that every object $F$ of
      $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat^{\circ},\Ab)$ is isomorphic to a functor of
      the type $TX = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(.,X)$: this is
      obviously true when $F$ is noetherian; in the general case, the set
      of noetherian subobjects of $F$ can be indexed by a set belonging to
      $\Univ$. It follows that there exists an inductive system of
      noetherian objects of $\mathbf{D}$, say $(X_i,u_{ji})_{i,j\in J}$,
      such that $J$ is an element of $\Univ$, that the $u_{ji}$ are
      monomorphisms, and that $F$ is isomorphic to the inductive limit of
      the functors $TX_i$. If $X$ is the inductive limit of the $X_i$, the
      equality
      \[
        T\Bigl(\sup_i X_i\Bigr) = \sup_i TX_i \quad\text{(lemma 2)}
      \]
      shows that $F$ is isomorphic to $TX$.

\item[---] We next prove the equality
      $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(X,Z) = \operatorname{Hom}(TX,TZ)$:
      if $X$ and $Z$ are noetherian, this follows from Corollary 1 of
      Proposition 1 (Chapter~I). We pass from there to the case where $X$ is
      noetherian and $Z$ arbitrary: if $Z'$ is a noetherian subobject of
      $Z$, we have the diagram
      \begin{center}
      \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=7em]
      \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(X,Z')
        \arrow[r, "{\operatorname{Hom}(X,i^{Z}_{Z'})}"]
        \arrow[d, "{T(X,Z')}"]' &
      \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(X,Z)
        \arrow[d, "{T(X,Z)}"]' \\
      \operatorname{Hom}(TX,TZ')
        \arrow[r, "{\operatorname{Hom}(TX,T i^{Z}_{Z'})}"] &
      \operatorname{Hom}(TX,TZ)
      \end{tikzcd}
      \end{center}
      The map $T(X,Z')$ is an isomorphism. By passing to the inductive limit
      over the noetherian subobjects of $Z$, we see that $T(X,Z)$ is bijective
      (lemma 2). The general case is then proved by an argument of the same
      type [if $X$ and $Z$ are arbitrary, consider the projective system
      formed by $\operatorname{Hom}(X',Z)$ where $X'$ runs through the
      noetherian subobjects of $X$].
\end{itemize}

\begin{lemma}
--- A functor $F$ of $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat^{\circ},\Ab)$ is exact if and
only if $F$ is an injective object of $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat^{\circ},\Ab)$.
(We always suppose the category $\Cat$ to be noetherian.)
\end{lemma}

Corollary 2 of Proposition 6 proves one half of the lemma. Conversely,
suppose $F$ exact. We have seen that the functors $\dot{X}$ form a family of
generators and that every subobject of $\dot{X}$ is representable ($\Cat$ is
a noetherian category). Every monomorphism $i\colon G\to \dot{X}$ therefore
induces a surjection of $FX = \operatorname{Hom}(\dot{X},F)$ onto
$\operatorname{Hom}(G,F)$. The assertion follows consequently from a lemma
of \textsc{Grothendieck} ([10], lemma 1 of theorem 1.10.1).

In the equivalence between $\mathbf{D}$ and
$\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat^{\circ},\Ab)$, the injective objects of
$\mathbf{D}$ correspond to the exact functors and the inductive limits of
$\mathbf{D}$ correspond to the inductive limits of functors. We deduce the
following corollary.

\begin{corollary}
--- Every inductive limit of injective objects of $\mathbf{D}$ is an injective
object. If $(X_i,u_{ji})_{i,j\in J}$ is an inductive system of $\mathbf{D}$
and if $Y$ is a noetherian object, we have the equality
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}\Bigl(Y,
    \lim_{\substack{\longrightarrow\\ i}} X_i\Bigr)
  = \lim_{\substack{\longrightarrow\\ i}}
    \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(Y,X_i).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}
--- Every locally noetherian category is a category with projective limits.
\end{corollary}

This is so for the category $\operatorname{Sex}(\Cat^{\circ},\Ab)$.

We leave to the reader the task of formulating the dual statements of the
preceding statements.
