\section{Adjoint Functors}
--- The reader will find in [20] the proof of the following results:

Let $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ be two categories, and let
$T\colon \mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}$ and $S\colon \mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{A}$
be functors. We denote by $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(T,.,.)$ and
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,S,.)$ the following functors:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(T,.,.)\colon\quad
    &(A,B)\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(TA,B),\\
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,S,.)\colon\quad
    &(A,B)\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(A,SB).
  \end{aligned}
\]

These functors are defined in the product category
$\mathbf{A}^{\circ}\prod\mathbf{B}$ and they take their values in the
category $\Ens$ of sets. A natural transformation
$\psi\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(T,.,.)\to
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,S.)$ defines, for every pair $(A,B)$ of
objects of $\mathbf{A}$ and of $\mathbf{B}$, a map
\[
  \psi(A,B)\colon
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(TA,B)\longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(A,SB)
\]

In particular, if $B$ is equal to $TA$, the image of the identity morphism
of $TA$ under the map $\psi(A,TA)$ is a morphism
$\Psi(A)\colon A\to STA$.

\begin{lemma}
The morphisms $\Psi(A)$ define a natural transformation $\Psi$ from the
identity functor $I_{\mathbf{A}}$ of $\mathbf{A}$ to the functor
$S\circ T$. The map $\psi\rightsquigarrow\Psi$ is bijective.
\end{lemma}

We recall only how one can reconstruct $\psi$ knowing $\Psi$: the functor
$S$ defines, for every pair $(A,B)$, a map
\[
  S(TA,B)\colon
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(TA,B)\longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(STA,SB).
\]
The map $\psi(A,B)$ is the composite of $S(TA,B)$ and of the map from
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(STA,SB)$ to
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(A,SB)$ which is defined by $\Psi(A)$.

Similarly, a natural transformation
$\varphi\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,S.)\to
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(T,.,.)$ defines, for every object $B$ of
$\mathbf{B}$, a morphism $\Phi(B)\colon TSB\to B$.

\begin{lemma}
The morphisms $\Phi(B)$ define a natural transformation $\Phi$ from the
functor $T\circ S$ to the identity functor $I_{\mathbf{B}}$ of
$\mathbf{B}$. The map $\varphi\rightsquigarrow\Phi$ is bijective.
\end{lemma}

With the preceding notations, suppose that natural transformations $\psi$
and $\varphi$ are given, and let $\Psi$ and $\Phi$ be the morphisms
associated to $\psi$ and $\varphi$ by the preceding lemmas:

\begin{proposition}
For the composite natural transformation $\psi\circ\varphi$ to be the
identity morphism of the functor $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,S.)$,
it is necessary and sufficient that the composite morphism

\begin{tikzcd}
S \arrow[r, "\Psi S"] & STS \arrow[r, "S\Phi"] & S
\end{tikzcd}

be the identity morphism of the functor $S$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}
For the composite natural transformation $\varphi\circ\psi$ to be the
identity morphism of the functor $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(T.,.)$,
it is necessary and sufficient that the composite morphism

\begin{tikzcd}
T \arrow[r, "T\Psi"] & TST \arrow[r, "\Phi T"] & T
\end{tikzcd}

be the identity morphism of the functor $T$.
\end{proposition}

If there exist two natural transformations $\varphi$ and $\psi$ such that
$\psi\circ\varphi$ and $\varphi\circ\psi$ are identity morphisms, we say
that the functor $S$ is \emph{adjoint} to $T$. In this terminology $S$
may be adjoint to $T$ without $T$ being adjoint to $S$. If $S$ and $S'$
are two functors adjoint to $T$, there exists a natural isomorphism of
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,S.)$ onto
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,S'.)$. This isomorphism defines, for
every object $B$ of $\mathbf{B}$, an isomorphism of the functor
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,SB)$ onto the functor
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,S'B)$. The corollary to
Proposition~1 entails the following proposition:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:uniqueness-of-adjoints}
If $S$ and $S'$ are two functors adjoint to $T$, there exists a natural
isomorphism of $S$ onto $S'$. Likewise, if $S$ is a functor adjoint to
two functors $T$ and $T'$, there exists a natural isomorphism of $T$ onto
$T'$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:adjunction-representable}
If $T$ is a functor of $\mathbf{A}$ into $\mathbf{B}$, the following
assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] There exists a functor adjoint to $T$.
\item[\normalfont b.] For every object $B$ of $\mathbf{B}$, the functor
      $A\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(TA,B)$ is representable.
\end{description}
\end{proposition}

It is clear that (a) entails (b). Conversely, for every object $B$ of
$\mathbf{B}$ choose a representing object $SB$ for the functor
$A\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(TA,B)$ and a natural isomorphism
$\varphi(B)\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,SB)\xrightarrow{\sim}
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(T.,B)$. If $f\colon B\to B'$ is a
morphism of $\mathbf{B}$, there is one and only one morphism
$Sf\colon SB\to SB'$ such that the following diagram is commutative:

% The commas in the two vertical labels are braced: pgfkeys splits tikz-cd
% options on top-level commas before the quotes library sees them.  Primes
% inside a label are written \prime for the same reason.
\begin{tikzcd}
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,SB)
  \arrow[r, "\natt{\varphi}(B)"]
  \arrow[d, "\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.{,}Sf)"'] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(T.,B)
  \arrow[d, "\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(T.{,}f)"'] \\
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,SB\prime)
  \arrow[r, "\natt{\varphi}(B\prime)"] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(T.,B\prime)
\end{tikzcd}

One sees without difficulty that the maps $B\mapsto SB$ and $f\mapsto Sf$
define a functor adjoint to $T$.

When $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are two \emph{additive categories}, we
shall suppose, in accordance with our conventions, that $S$ and $T$ are
additive functors. What precedes then remains valid if one agrees to
consider that the functors $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(T,.,.)$ and
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(.,S,.)$ take their values in the category
$\Ab$ of abelian groups. This implies that the maps $\psi(A,B)$ and
$\varphi(A,B)$ are linear. We have in addition the following proposition:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:adjoints-exactness}
Let $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ be two abelian categories, $T$ a functor
(additive) of $\mathbf{A}$ into $\mathbf{B}$, $S$ a functor adjoint to
$T$. Then $S$ is left exact and $T$ is right exact.
\end{proposition}

Let indeed
\[
  o\longrightarrow B'\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{g}B''\longrightarrow o
\]
be an exact sequence of $\mathbf{B}$. We have the following commutative
diagram ($A$ is an arbitrary object of $\mathbf{A}$):

% The commas in the three vertical labels are braced, and their primes are
% written \prime: pgfkeys splits tikz-cd options on top-level commas before
% the quotes library sees them.
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
o \arrow[r] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(A,SB')
  \arrow[r] \arrow[d, "\natt{\varphi}(A{,}B\prime)"] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(A,SB)
  \arrow[r] \arrow[d, "\natt{\varphi}(A{,}B)"] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(A,SB'')
  \arrow[d, "\natt{\varphi}(A{,}B\prime\prime)"] \\
o \arrow[r] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(TA,B') \arrow[r] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(TA,B) \arrow[r] &
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(TA,B'')
\end{tikzcd}

The second row is exact and the vertical arrows represent bijections. It
follows that the first row is exact and that $S$ is left exact. One
proves analogously that $T$ is right exact.
