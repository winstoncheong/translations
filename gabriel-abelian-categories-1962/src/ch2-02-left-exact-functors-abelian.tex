\section{Left Exact Functors with Values in an Abelian Category}
--- We now suppose that $\mathbf{C}$ is an abelian category whose set of
morphisms and set of objects belong to $\Univ$. If $\mathbf{D}$ is an
abelian category, we wish to study here the full subcategory of
$\operatorname{Fonct}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$ whose objects are the left
exact functors of $\mathbf{C}$ into $\mathbf{D}$. This full subcategory shall
be denoted $\operatorname{Sex}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$ ($S$ as
\emph{sinister}, $ex$ as \emph{exact}).

\begin{proposition}
\itshape
--- Let $\mathbf{C}$ be a $\Univ$-category which is abelian, whose set of
morphisms and set of objects belong to $\Univ$. Let $\mathbf{D}$ be a
$\Univ$-category which is abelian, with generators and exact inductive
limits. For every functor $F$ of $\mathbf{C}$ into $\mathbf{D}$ there
exist a functor $R^{0}F$ and a morphism $\rho\colon F\to R^{0}F$ which
satisfy the following conditions:

\emph{The functor $R^{0}F$ is left exact}; moreover, if
$\varphi\colon F\to G$ is a morphism of $F$ into a left exact functor $G$,
there exists a morphism
\[
  \tau\colon R^{0}F\to G
\]
\emph{and one only such that $\sigma = \tau\circ\rho$.}
\end{proposition}

We have already proved Proposition~4 when $\mathbf{C}$ has enough injective
objects (cf.\ Chap.~I, Proposition~15). It is well known on the other hand
that the derived functors of $F$ can be defined under the hypotheses of
Proposition~4. In this study we are interested only in the $0$th derived
functor of $F$; we use a method which \textsc{Buchsbaum} also uses in the
construction of satellite functors.

A few lemmas will precede the proof of Proposition~4: for every object $A$
of $\mathbf{C}$ we consider the set $\mathbf{S}_A$ of exact sequences of
the form
\[
  S\colon\quad \mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u}M\xrightarrow{v}N\to
  \mathrm{o}.
\]
If
\[
  S = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u}M\xrightarrow{v}N\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  S' = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u'}M'\xrightarrow{v'}N'\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)
\]
are two elements of $\mathbf{S}_A$, we shall agree to write $S\succeq S'$
if there is a diagram of the following type
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
A \arrow[r, "u"] \arrow[d, "i_A"'] &
M \arrow[r, "v"] \arrow[d, "m"'] &
N \arrow[r] \arrow[d, "n"'] &
\mathrm{o} \\
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
A \arrow[r, "u'"] &
M' \arrow[r, "v'"] &
N' \arrow[r] &
\mathrm{o}
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad(\star)
\end{equation*}

\begin{lemma}
\itshape
The relation $S\succeq S'$ is a preorder, which filters to the right.
\end{lemma}

It is clear that we are dealing with a preorder. Let then
\[
  S = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u}M\xrightarrow{v}N\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  S' = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u'}M'\xrightarrow{v'}N'\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)
\]
be two elements of $\mathbf{S}_A$. We consider the commutative diagram
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}
A \arrow[r, "u'"] \arrow[d, "u"'] & M' \arrow[d, "i_{M'}"] \\
M \arrow[r, "i_M"'] & M\,\Sigma_A\,M'
\end{tikzcd}
\]
By the end of paragraph~7 (Chap.~I), $j_M$ and $j'_M$ are monomorphisms.
It follows that the sequence
\[
  S'' = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{j_{M'\circ u}}
  M\,\Sigma_A\,M'\to\operatorname{Coker}(j_M\circ u)\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)
\]
belongs to $\mathbf{S}_A$ and that $S''\succeq S$, $S''\succeq S'$.

If $F$ is a functor of $\mathbf{C}$ into $\mathbf{D}$, and if $S\succeq S'$,
diagram $(\star)$ yields the equality
$(Fn)\circ(Fv) = (Fv')\circ(Fm)$. It follows that $Fm$ induces a morphism
$f$ of $\operatorname{Ker}(Fv)$ into $\operatorname{Ker}(Fv')$.

\begin{lemma}
\itshape
The morphism $f$ does not depend on the choice of $m$ and of $n$.
\end{lemma}

Let indeed $m'\colon M\to M'$ be another morphism such that
$u' = m'\circ u$; let $n'\colon N\to N'$ be the unique morphism such that
$n'\circ v$ is equal to $v'\circ m'$. Then
$(m'-m)\circ u = \mathrm{o}$; hence there exists $w\colon N\to M'$ with
$m'-m = w\circ v$. It follows that $Fm'$ is equal to
$Fm + (Fw)\circ(Fv)$. The lemma follows from the fact that
$(Fw)\circ(Fv)$ vanishes on $\operatorname{Ker}(Fv)$.

Henceforth we denote by $FS$ the object $\operatorname{Ker}(Fv)$ and by
$F(S',S)$ the morphism $f$ of $\operatorname{Ker}(Fv)$ into
$\operatorname{Ker}(Fv')$. If $S'\succeq S$ and $S'\succeq S'$, the
preceding lemma shows that $F(S',S)$ is an isomorphism whose inverse is
$F(S,S')$. It is therefore legitimate to give the definition
\begin{equation*}
  (RF)A = \lim_{\overline{\longrightarrow}\,F\in\mathbf{S}_A} FS.
\end{equation*}

Let us give moreover a morphism $g\colon A\to A'$. For every sequence
\[
  S = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u}M\xrightarrow{v}N\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)
\]
there is the following commutative exact diagram (end of \S6, Chap.~I):
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
A \arrow[r, "u"] \arrow[d, "f"'] &
M \arrow[r, "v"] \arrow[d, "i'_M"'] &
N \arrow[r] \arrow[d, "i_N"'] &
\mathrm{o} \\
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
A' \arrow[r, "i_{A'}"] &
A'\,\Sigma_A\,M \arrow[r] &
N \arrow[r] &
\mathrm{o}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
Let $fS$ be the sequence of the second row. The morphism $Fj'_M$
obviously induces a morphism of $FS$ into $F(fS)$. We denote by $F(f,S)$
the composite of this morphism with the canonical morphism of $F(fS)$
into $(RF)A'$. The reader will verify the relations
$F(f,S) = F(f,S')\circ F(S',S)$ when $S'\succeq S$. By passing to the
limit the morphisms $F(f,S)$ therefore define a morphism $(RF)f$ of
$(RF)A$ into $(RF)A'$.

\begin{lemma}
\itshape
\leavevmode\par\vspace{2pt}
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] The maps $A\mapsto (RF)A$ and $f\mapsto (RF)f$
define a functor $RF$ of $\mathbf{C}$ into $\mathbf{D}$.

\item[\normalfont b.] $RF$ is zero if and only if $F$ satisfies the
following condition: for every object $A$ of $\mathbf{C}$, $FA$ is the
supremum of the $\operatorname{Ker}(Fi)$ as $i$ ranges over the
monomorphisms with source $A$.

\item[\normalfont c.] The functor $F\leadsto RF$ is left exact.

\item[\normalfont d.] If $\varphi\colon F\to G$ is a natural
transformation and $R(\operatorname{Coker}\varphi)$ is zero, then
$R(\operatorname{Coker}R\varphi)$ is zero.

\item[\normalfont e.] The functor $F\leadsto RF$ commutes with inductive
limits.
\end{description}
\end{lemma}

We leave to the reader the verification of (a), (b), (e). Let us prove
(c): let
$\mathrm{o}\to F'\xrightarrow{\varphi}F\xrightarrow{\psi}F''\to\mathrm{o}$
be an exact sequence of functors of $\mathbf{C}$ into $\mathbf{D}$. For
every object $A$ of $\mathbf{C}$ and every element
$S = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u}M\xrightarrow{v}N\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)$
of $\mathbf{S}_A$, there is the diagram
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
F'M \arrow[r, "\varphi^{(M)}"] \arrow[d, "F'v"'] &
FM \arrow[r, "\psi^{(M)}"] \arrow[d, "Fv"'] &
F''M \arrow[r] \arrow[d, "F''v"'] &
\mathrm{o} \\
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
F'N \arrow[r, "\varphi^{(N)}"] &
FN \arrow[r, "\psi^{(N)}"] &
F''N \arrow[r] &
\mathrm{o}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
The morphisms $\varphi(M)$ and $\psi(M)$ therefore induce an exact
sequence
\[
  \mathrm{o}\to F'S\to FS\to F''S
\]
([4], Lemma~III, p.~32). Assertion (c) follows from this exact sequence
by passing to the inductive limit.

For the sake of clarity of the exposition we prove assertion (d) only when
$\mathbf{D}$ is the category $\mathbf{Ab}$ of abelian groups; the condition
$R(\operatorname{Coker}\varphi) = \mathrm{o}$ can then be stated as
follows: for every object $A$ of $\mathbf{C}$ and every $a\in GA$, there
exists a monomorphism $i\colon A\to B$ and $b\in FB$ such that
$(Gi)(a) = \varphi(B)(b)$.

We are going to see that this property is still true when we replace $F$
by $RF$, $G$ by $RG$ and $\varphi$ by $R\varphi$: let indeed $a$ be an
element of $(RG)A$. There is an exact sequence
\[
  S = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u}M\xrightarrow{v}N\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)
\]
and an element $a'$ of $GS$ whose image under the canonical map of $GS$
into $(RG)A$ is $a$. There is therefore also a monomorphism
$j\colon M\to B$ and an element $b'$ of $FB$ such that
$(Gj)(a') = \varphi(B)(b')$. If $b$ is the image of $b'$ in $(RF)B$ and
if $i = j\circ u$, we obtain the required equality:
\[
  ((RG)i)(a) = ((R\varphi)(B))(b).
\]

\begin{lemma}
\itshape
If $g\colon A'\to A$ is a monomorphism, it is so for $(RF)g$ as well.
\end{lemma}

Associate indeed to every sequence
$S = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u}M\xrightarrow{v}N\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)$
the diagram
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
A \arrow[r, "u"] &
M \arrow[r, "v"] &
N \arrow[r] &
\mathrm{o} \\
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
A' \arrow[r, "u\circ g"] \arrow[u, "g"'] &
M \arrow[r, "p^M_{N'}"] \arrow[u, "1_M"'] &
N' \arrow[r] \arrow[u] &
\mathrm{o}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
where $N'$ is the cokernel of $u\circ g$. If $g^{-1}S$ denotes the
sequence of the second row, it is clear that the sequences $g^{-1}S$
form a cofinal subset of $\mathbf{S}_A$. We deduce from this that $(RF)A'$
is equal to $\lim_{\overline{\longrightarrow}\,S}F(g^{-1}S)$ and that
$(RF)g$ is the inductive limit of the canonical monomorphisms of
$F(g^{-1}S)$ into $FS$. This proves the lemma.

\begin{lemma}
\itshape
We suppose that for every monomorphism $g$ of $\mathbf{C}$, $Fg$ is a
monomorphism. Then:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] The morphisms $F(S',S)$ are monomorphisms.

\item[\normalfont b.] The functor $RF$ is left exact.
\end{description}
\end{lemma}

To prove (a) we use the notation of Lemma~1. If $S'\succeq S$, the exact
sequence
\[
  S'' = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{j_{M'\circ u}}
  M\,\Sigma_A\,M'\to\operatorname{Coker}j_M\circ u\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)
\]
``dominates'' both $S$ and $S'$ (i.e.\ $S''\succeq S$, $S''\succeq S'$);
moreover $F(S'',S)$ is induced by the morphism $Fj_M$. We deduce from
this that $F(S'',S) = F(S'',S')\circ F(S',S)$ and that $F(S',S)$ are
monomorphisms.

Let us prove (b): let
$\mathrm{o}\to A'\xrightarrow{f}A\xrightarrow{g}A''\to\mathrm{o}$ be an
exact sequence of $\mathbf{C}$ and let
$S = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u}M\xrightarrow{v}N\to\mathrm{o}\bigr)$
be an element of $\mathbf{S}_A$. There is the following commutative exact
diagram:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large, row sep=1.1em]
& \mathrm{o} & & \mathrm{o} & \\
& & \downarrow & & \downarrow & \\
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
A' \arrow[r, "f"] \arrow[d, "1_{A'}"'] &
A \arrow[r, "g"] \arrow[d, "u"'] &
A'' \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
\mathrm{o} \\
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
A' \arrow[r, "u\circ f"] &
M \arrow[r, "i_M"] \arrow[d, "v"'] &
M\,\Sigma_A\,A'' \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
\mathrm{o} \\
& & N \arrow[r, "1_N"] & N & \\
& & \downarrow & & \downarrow & \\
& & \mathrm{o} & & \mathrm{o} &
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
Using notation already employed, we deduce from it the exact diagram
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge]
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
F(f^{-1}S) \arrow[r] \arrow[d, <->] &
FS \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
F(gS) \arrow[d] \\
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
F(f^{-1}S) \arrow[r] &
FM \arrow[r] &
F(M\,\Sigma_A\,A'')
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
By passing to the limit it follows that there is an exact sequence
\[
  \mathrm{o}\to (RF)A'\to (RF)A\to
  \lim_{\overline{\longrightarrow}\,S}F(gS).
\]
Moreover, assertion (a) shows that the canonical morphism of
$\lim_{\overline{\longrightarrow}\,S}F(gS)$ into $(RF)A''$ is a
monomorphism; this completes the proof.

We are now in a position to prove Proposition~4: let $A$ be an object of
$\mathbf{C}$ and let $\sigma$ be a natural transformation of $F$ into a
left exact functor $G$. For every exact sequence
\[
  S = \bigl(\mathrm{o}\to A\xrightarrow{u}M\xrightarrow{v}N\to\mathrm{o}\bigr),
\]
there is the commutative diagram
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}
& FM \arrow[r, "Fv"] \arrow[d, "\sigma(M)"'] &
FN \arrow[d, "\sigma(N)"] \\
\mathrm{o} \arrow[r] &
GA \arrow[r, "Gu"] &
GM \arrow[r, "Gv"] &
GN
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
Let $\rho_F(A,S)$ be the morphism of $FA$ into $FS$ which is induced by
$Fu$. There exists a unique morphism $\tau_F(A,S)$ whose composite with
$\rho_F(A,S)$ is equal to $\sigma(A)$. By passing to the inductive limit we
see that there exists a unique morphism $\tau_F(A)$ of $(RF)A$ into $GA$
whose composite with
$\rho_F(A) = \lim_{\overline{\longrightarrow}\,S}\rho_F(A,S)$ is equal to
$\sigma(A)$. If the functor $RF$ is left exact, the proposition is
proved. In the contrary case we start the operation again. Lemmas~4 and
~5 show that the functor $R(RF)$ is left exact. In every case we may
therefore choose $R^{0}F$ equal to $R(RF)$, $\rho$ being equal to
$\rho_{RF}\circ\rho_F$.

\begin{proposition}
\itshape
Suppose the hypotheses of Proposition~4 to be verified. The category
$\operatorname{Sex}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$ of left exact functors of
$\mathbf{C}$ into $\mathbf{D}$ is an abelian category with exact inductive
limits. If $\mathbf{D}$ is a category with projective limits, it is the
same for $\operatorname{Sex}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$.
\end{proposition}

It is clear that $\operatorname{Sex}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$ is an
additive category. If $\varphi\colon F\to G$ is a morphism of left exact
functors, $\operatorname{Ker}\varphi$ is the left exact functor
$M\mapsto\operatorname{Ker}\varphi(M)$. Likewise, if $H$ is the functor
$M\mapsto\operatorname{Coker}\varphi(M)$, the left exact functor
$\operatorname{Coker}\varphi$ is nothing but $R^{0}H$. This shows that
axiom $\mathbf{C}\,\boldsymbol{Ab}\,1$ is verified.

Let us show that axiom $\mathbf{C}\,\boldsymbol{Ab}\,2$ is verified: let
$K$ and $L$ be the functors
$M\mapsto\operatorname{Coim}\varphi(M)$ and
$M\mapsto\operatorname{Im}\varphi(M)$. The canonical morphisms
$\zeta(M)$ of $\operatorname{Coim}\varphi(M)$ into
$\operatorname{Im}\varphi(M)$ define a natural isomorphism $\zeta$ of $K$
onto $L$. Moreover we have exact sequences of functors:
\[
  \mathrm{o}\to\operatorname{Ker}\varphi\to F\to K\to\mathrm{o},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{o}\to L\to G\to H\to\mathrm{o}.
\]
Since $R^{0} = R\circ R$ is a left exact functor, we have the exact
sequences:
\[
  \mathrm{o}\to\operatorname{Ker}\varphi\to F\to R^{0}K,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{o}\to R^{0}L\to G\to R^{0}H.
\]
The second exact sequence shows that $R^{0}L$ is nothing but
$\operatorname{Im}\varphi$; moreover $R^{0}K$ equals
$\operatorname{Coim}\varphi$ and $R^{0}\zeta$ is the canonical morphism of
$\operatorname{Coim}\varphi$ into $\operatorname{Im}\varphi$: it is indeed
an isomorphism. The rest of the proposition is clear.

\begin{corollary}
\itshape
The functor $F\leadsto R^{0}F$ is an exact functor of
$\operatorname{Fonct}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$ into
$\operatorname{Sex}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$.
\end{corollary}

We already know that $F\leadsto R^{0}F$ is a left exact functor. It is
therefore enough to prove that $R^{0}v$ is an epimorphism of
$\operatorname{Sex}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$ if $v$ is an epimorphism of
$\operatorname{Fonct}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$. But the equality
$\operatorname{Coker}v = \mathrm{o}$ [in the category
$\operatorname{Fonct}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$] entails, by
Lemma~3~(d), the equalities
$R(\operatorname{Coker}Rv) = \mathrm{o}$ and
$R(\operatorname{Coker}R(Rv)) = R(\operatorname{Coker}R^{0}v)
= \mathrm{o}$.

When $\mathbf{C}$ has enough injective objects, Proposition~5 also
follows from Proposition~1: indeed, if $\mathcal{I}$ denotes the full
subcategory of $\mathbf{C}$ formed by the injective objects of $\mathbf{C}$,
the categories $\operatorname{Sex}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$ and
$\operatorname{Fonct}(\mathcal{I},\mathbf{D})$ are equivalent
(cf.\ Chap.~I, Corollary~2 of Proposition~14).
