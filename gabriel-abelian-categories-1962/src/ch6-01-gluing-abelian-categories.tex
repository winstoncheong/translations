\chapter{Applications to Quasi-Coherent Sheaves}
\markboth{Chapter VI. Applications to Quasi-Coherent Sheaves}{}

We wish to study here the injective quasi-coherent sheaves on a
noetherian prescheme \textup{\cite{grothendieck-dieudonne}}. Whenever we
speak of a prescheme $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$, it will be understood that the
set underlying $X$ and the étalé space associated with
$\mathcal{O}_X$ are elements of the universe $\Univ$. Whenever we speak
of an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module $M$, it will be understood that $M$ is
quasi-coherent and that the étalé space associated with $M$ is an
element of $\Univ$. We denote by $\mathbf{F}_X$ the category of
$\mathcal{O}_X$-modules; with our conventions, $\mathbf{F}_X$ is an
abelian $\Univ$-category with exact inductive limits.

We show in the first section that $\mathbf{F}_X$ can be obtained by
``gluing'' module categories. What follows is devoted to the properties
of module categories which are preserved under ``gluing''.

\section{Gluing of Abelian Categories}

--- Consider the diagram
\[
(\star)\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathbf{C} \arrow[dr, "F"] \\
& \mathbf{B} \\
\mathbf{D} \arrow[ur, "G"']
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $\mathbf{C}$, $\mathbf{D}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are abelian categories
and where $F$ and $G$ are exact functors. We call \emph{gluing}
(\emph{recollement}) of $\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{D}$ \emph{along}
$\mathbf{B}$, and we denote by
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ the following category:

\begin{itemize}
\item An object of $\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ is a
      triple $(C, D, \sigma)$ such that $C$ is an object of
      $\mathbf{C}$, $D$ an object of $\mathbf{D}$ and $\sigma$ an
      isomorphism from $FC$ onto $GD$. One sometimes says that
      $(C, D, \sigma)$ is gluing data (\emph{donnée de recollement}).

\item If $(C', D', \sigma')$ and $(C, D, \sigma)$ are two objects, a
      morphism from the first to the second is a pair $(u, v)$ such that
      $u$ is a morphism from $C'$ to $C$ and $v$ a morphism from $D'$
      to $D$; one imposes in addition the equality
      \[
        \sigma \circ (Fu) = (Gv) \circ \sigma'.
      \]

\item Composition of morphisms is defined by the formula
      \[
        (u', v') \circ (u, v) = (u' \circ u, v' \circ v).
      \]
\end{itemize}

The hypotheses we have made imply that
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ is an abelian category
and that the functors $(C, D, \sigma) \leadsto C$ and
$(C, D, \sigma) \leadsto D$ are exact. These functors are said to be the
canonical projections of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ onto $\mathbf{C}$ and
$\mathbf{D}$. The notation
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ is justified by the
following statement:

\begin{proposition}
Let $\mathbf{A}$ be a category, $S : \mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{C}$ and
$T : \mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{D}$ two functors such that $F \circ S$ is
isomorphic to $G \circ T$. There then exists a functor
$R : \mathbf{A} \to
\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$, unique up to isomorphism,
such that $S$ and $T$ are isomorphic to the composites of $R$ with the
canonical projections of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ into $\mathbf{C}$ and
$\mathbf{D}$.
\end{proposition}

Let $\tau$ be a functorial isomorphism from $F \circ S$ onto
$G \circ T$. We leave to the reader the task of verifying that
Proposition~1 holds if one takes for $R$ the functor
$A \leadsto (SA, TA, \tau(A))$\ednote{The original prints
$(FA, GA, \tau(A))$; since $F$ and $G$ are defined on $\mathbf{C}$ and
$\mathbf{D}$, not on $\mathbf{A}$, this is meaningless. We print
$(SA, TA, \tau(A))$, the only reading under which the triple is gluing
data.}. If one regards $\mathbf{C}$, $\mathbf{D}$, $\mathbf{B}$ and
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ as objects of the
category $\mathbf{E}$ of Chapter~I (\S8), and if one replaces $F$ and $G$
by the isomorphism classes of these functors, Proposition~1 implies that
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ is the fibred product of
the diagram $(\star)$. In particular, this shows that Proposition~1
determines the category
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ up to equivalence.

Consider now a prescheme $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ and let $U$ and $V$ be two
open subsets covering $X$. If $M$ (resp.\ $N$) is an
$(\mathcal{O}_X|U)$-module [resp.\ an $(\mathcal{O}_X|V)$-module], then
$M|U \cap V$ (resp.\ $N|U \cap V$) is an
$(\mathcal{O}_X|U \cap V)$-module\ednote{The original prints here
$F|U \cap V$ (resp.\ $G|U \cap V)$; since $F$ and $G$ are not defined on
sheaves, this is meaningless. We print $M|U \cap V$ (resp.\ $N|U \cap V)$,
the only reading consistent with the restriction functors $\rho_U$ and
$\rho_V$ defined below.}; we denote by $\rho_U$ (resp.\ by $\rho_V$)
the restriction functor $M \leadsto M|U \cap V$ (resp.\ $N \leadsto
N|U \cap V$). One thus has the diagram $(\star\star)$:
\[
(\star\star)\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}
\mathbf{F}_U \arrow[dr, "\rho_U"] \\
& \mathbf{F}_{U \cap V} \\
\mathbf{F}_V \arrow[ur, "\rho_V"']
\end{tikzcd}
\]

If $P$ is an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module, the sheaves $(P|U)|U \cap V$,
$P|U \cap V$ and $(P|V)|U \cap V$ evidently coincide; if $\sigma_P$ is
the identity morphism of $P|U \cap V$, the triple
$(P|U, P|V, \sigma_P)$ is thus gluing data.

\begin{proposition}
Let $U$ and $V$ be two open subsets of a prescheme $X$ such that
$U \cup V = X$. The functor $P \leadsto (P|U, P|V, \sigma_P)$ defines an
equivalence between $\mathbf{F}_X$ and the gluing of $\mathbf{F}_U$ and
$\mathbf{F}_V$ along $\mathbf{F}_{U \cap V}$.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~2 follows directly from Proposition~12 (Chapter~I) (cf.\
also \textup{\cite{grothendieck-dieudonne}}, prop.~0.3.3).

\begin{proposition}
Let $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ be a prescheme and $U$ an open subset of $X$
such that the canonical injection $j : U \to X$ is a quasi-compact
morphism. Let $T$ be the functor which associates to every
$\mathcal{O}_X$-module $M$ the restriction $M|U$ of $M$ to the
prescheme $(U, \mathcal{O}_X|U)$. The functor $T$ defines, on passing to
the quotient, an equivalence between the categories
$\mathbf{F}_X/\operatorname{Ker} T$ and $\mathbf{F}_U$.
\end{proposition}

Denote indeed by $j_*(N)$ the direct image in $X$ of an
$(\mathcal{O}_X|U)$-module $N$. By Proposition~9.4.2 of
\textup{\cite{grothendieck-dieudonne}}, $j_*(N)$ is a quasi-coherent
$\mathcal{O}_X$-module; it follows that $S : G \leadsto j_*(G)$ is a
functor adjoint to $T$. The assertion thus follows from Proposition~5
(Chapter~III).

Propositions~2 and~3 will be used in the following way: if $X$ is a
noetherian prescheme, $X$ is the union of a finite sequence of affine
open subsets $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$. The category
$\mathbf{F}_{X_1 \cup X_2}$ is then equivalent to the gluing of
$\mathbf{F}_{X_1}$ and $\mathbf{F}_{X_2}$ along one and the same
quotient category $\mathbf{F}_{X_1 \cap X_2}$. In other words,
$\mathbf{F}_{X_1 \cup X_2}$ is equivalent to the gluing of two module
categories along a ``common'' quotient category. Likewise,
$\mathbf{F}_{X_1 \cup X_2 \cup X_3}$ is equivalent to the gluing of
$\mathbf{F}_{X_1 \cup X_2}$ and of a module category
$\mathbf{F}_{X_3}$ along a ``common'' quotient category
$\mathbf{F}_{(X_1 \cup X_2) \cap X_3}$. One continues in this way until
one arrives at $\mathbf{F}_X$; this category is thus obtained by
``successive gluings'' of module categories.
