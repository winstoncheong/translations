\section{Stability under Injective Envelopes}

--- Let $A$ be a ring and $\mathbf{C}$ a full subcategory of
$\operatorname{mod} A$ satisfying the following conditions:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont $(\star)$] If $M$ is an $A$-module belonging to
  $\mathbf{C}$, every submodule of $M$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$.
\item[\normalfont $(\star\star)$] If $M$ and $N$ are two $A$-modules
  belonging to $\mathbf{C}$, the direct sum $M \oplus N$ belongs to
  $\mathbf{C}$.
\end{description}

Let us now denote by $M$ an arbitrary $A$-module. If $M'$ and $M''$ are
two submodules of $M$ such that $M/M'$ and $M/M''$ belong to
$\mathbf{C}$, then $M/(M' \cap M'')$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$; this module
is indeed isomorphic to a submodule of $M/M' \oplus M/M''$. The
submodules $M'$ of $M$ such that $M/M'$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$ thus
form a neighbourhood base of $0$ for a topology $T_{\mathbf{C}}M$ which
makes $M$ a topological group. The reader will verify that the topology
$T_{\mathbf{C}}A_d$ makes $A$ a topological ring. If $A$ is equipped with
this topology, the topology $T_{\mathbf{C}}M$ makes $M$ a topological
$A$-module. Moreover, every $A$-linear map $f : M \to N$ is continuous
when $M$ and $N$ are equipped with the topologies $T_{\mathbf{C}}M$ and
$T_{\mathbf{C}}N$. The situation may be summed up by saying that the
data of $\mathbf{C}$ defines on the one hand a topological ring
structure on $A$; on the other hand it defines a functor from
$\operatorname{mod} A$ to the category of topological $A$-modules.

\begin{proposition}
Let $A$ be a ring and $\mathbf{C}$ a full subcategory of
$\operatorname{mod} A$ satisfying conditions $(\star)$ and
$(\star\star)$. The following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] For every $A$-module $M$ and for every submodule
  $N$ of $M$, the topology $T_{\mathbf{C}}N$ coincides with the
  restriction to $N$ of the topology $T_{\mathbf{C}}M$.
\item[\normalfont b.] For every $A$-module $M$ belonging to
  $\mathbf{C}$, the injective envelope of $M$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$.
\end{description}
\end{proposition}

$(a)\Rightarrow(b)$: it amounts indeed to the same to say that $M$
belongs to $\mathbf{C}$ or to say that the topology $T_{\mathbf{C}}M$ is
discrete. If $M$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$ and is contained in an
$A$-module $I$, assertion (a) implies that there exists a submodule $Q$
of $I$ such that $I/Q$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$ and $Q \cap M = 0$. If $I$
is the injective envelope of $M$, $Q$ is necessarily zero. Whence
assertion (b).

$(b)\Rightarrow(a)$: let indeed $N'$ be a submodule of $N$ such that
$N/N'$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$. We have to show that there exists a
submodule $M'$ of $M$ such that $M/M'$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$ and
$N \cap M'$ is contained in $N'$. We shall choose for $M'$ a submodule
of $M$ which is maximal for the equality $N \cap M' = N'$. Then $M/M'$
is an essential extension of $N/N'$ and (a) follows from (b).

\begin{corollary}
Let $A$ be a ring and $\mathbf{C}$ a full subcategory of
$\operatorname{mod} A$ satisfying conditions $(\star)$, $(\star\star)$
and conditions (a) and (b) of Proposition~9. Let $M$ be an $A$-module
and let $N$ be the intersection of the submodules $M'$ of $M$ such that
$M/M'$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$. There is then no submodule $N'$ of $N$,
distinct from $N$ and such that $N/N'$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, the topology $T_{\mathbf{C}}N$ coincides with the topology
induced by $T_{\mathbf{C}}M$. This last topology is coarse.

We suppose from now on that $A$ is right noetherian. Proposition~9 and
Corollary~1 are then often used as follows: let $\mathfrak{i}$ be a
two-sided ideal of $A$ and let $\mathbf{C}$ be the localizing
subcategory of $\operatorname{mod} A$ whose noetherian objects are the
$A$-modules annihilated by a power of $\mathfrak{i}$. If $\mathbf{C}$ is
stable under injective envelopes [i.e.\ if $\mathbf{C}$ satisfies
assertion (b) of Proposition~9], Proposition~9\ednote{The original
prints ``proposition 11''; Proposition~11 is stated in \S6 and concerns
finite extensions of commutative rings, whereas the two statements
reformulated below are assertion~(a) of Proposition~9 and Corollary~1.
We print Proposition~9.} and Corollary~1 may be formulated as follows:
if $N$ is a submodule of a noetherian $A$-module $M$, the
$\mathfrak{i}$-adic topology of $N$ (i.~e.\ $T_{\mathbf{C}}N$) is the
restriction to $N$ of the $\mathfrak{i}$-adic topology of $M$; moreover,
if $R$ is the intersection of the submodules $M.\mathfrak{i}^n$ of $M$,
$R.\mathfrak{i}$ is equal to $R$. These propositions evoke well-known
results of Artin-Rees and Krull.

In section~6, we use Proposition~9 in a slightly different manner: if
$A$ is a right noetherian ring, we take for $\mathbf{C}$ the localizing
subcategory $(\operatorname{mod} A)_0$ whose objects are the $A$-modules
of Krull dimension $\le 0$. If $M$ is an $A$-module, we then denote by
$\hat{M}$ the completion of $M$ for the topology $T_{\mathbf{C}}M$; this
completion $\hat{M}$ is the projective limit of the modules $M/M'$,
where $M'$ runs through the submodules of $M$ such that
$\operatorname{Kdim} M/M' \le 0$. If $M$ is noetherian, the quotients
$M/M'$ are of finite length. In particular, $\hat{A}$ is a right
pseudocompact ring; if $M$ is noetherian, $\hat{M}$ is a right
pseudocompact module over $\hat{A}$.

It is clear that $(\operatorname{mod} A)_0$ identifies with the category
of discrete topological right $\hat{A}$-modules. Moreover, one has the

\begin{corollary}
Let $A$ be a right noetherian ring. The functor which associates with
every noetherian right $A$-module $M$ the right pseudocompact $A$-module
$\hat{M}$ is right exact. This functor is exact if the injective
envelope of an $A$-module of Krull dimension zero has Krull dimension
zero.
\end{corollary}

This corollary follows easily from Proposition~11 and Lemma~1, \S3,
Chapter~IV.

\begin{proposition}
If $A$ is a commutative noetherian ring, every localizing subcategory of
$\operatorname{mod} A$ is stable under injective envelopes.
\end{proposition}

Let indeed $\mathbf{C}$ be such a localizing subcategory and let $M$ be
an $A$-module belonging to $\mathbf{C}$. If the prime ideal
$\mathfrak{p}$ is associated with $M$, the module $A/\mathfrak{p}$
belongs to $\mathbf{C}$ (Proposition~7). We shall show that, for every
prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$, the injective envelope of $A/\mathfrak{p}$
belongs to the localizing subcategory generated by $A/\mathfrak{p}$; if
$M$ is an $A$-module and if $a$ is an element of $A$, we denote by $a_M$
the homothety $x \mapsto x.a$ of $M$. We say that $a_M$ is
\emph{almost nilpotent} if every noetherian submodule of $M$ is
annihilated by a power of $a_M$.

\begin{lemma}
Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal of a commutative noetherian ring $A$
and $M$ an $A$-module. The following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] $M$ belongs to the localizing subcategory of
  $\operatorname{mod} A$ generated by $A/\mathfrak{p}$.
\item[\normalfont b.] For every element $a$ of $\mathfrak{p}$, $a_M$ is
  almost nilpotent.
\end{description}
\end{lemma}

If $I$ is an ideal of $A$, the quotient $A/I$ belongs indeed to the
localizing subcategory generated by $A/\mathfrak{p}$ if and only if $I$
contains a power of $\mathfrak{p}$ (Corollary~1 to Proposition~7). It
follows that $M$ belongs to the localizing subcategory generated by
$A/\mathfrak{p}$ if and only if every element of $M$ is annihilated by a
power of $\mathfrak{p}$.

\begin{lemma}
Let $A$ be a commutative noetherian ring and $I$ an indecomposable
injective $A$-module. For every element $a$ of $A$, the homothety $a_I$
is either bijective or almost nilpotent.
\end{lemma}

Let indeed $M$ be a non-zero noetherian submodule of $I$. Since $M$ is
noetherian, the following equality holds for $n$ large enough:
\[
\operatorname{Ker} a_M^n \cap \operatorname{Im} a_M^n = 0.
\]

Since the intersection of two non-zero submodules of $I$ is non-zero,
one has either $a_M^n = 0$ or $\operatorname{Ker} a_M^n = 0$. In the
latter case, $a_M$ is a monomorphism from $I$ to $I$, hence an
automorphism, since $I$ contains no injectives distinct from $I$ or $0$.
In the former case, every noetherian submodule of $I$ is annihilated by
a power of $a$.

We are now in a position to prove the proposition: if $I$ is the
injective envelope of $A/\mathfrak{p}$, $\operatorname{Ker} a_I$ is
different from $0$ for every element $a$ of $\mathfrak{p}$. It follows
that $a_I$ is almost nilpotent (Lemma~2) and that $I$ belongs to the
localizing subcategory of $\operatorname{mod} A$ generated by
$A/\mathfrak{p}$ (Lemma~1).

Corollary~2 to Proposition~9 therefore applies to every commutative
noetherian ring. The same goes for the remarks following Corollary~1
(lemmas of Artin-Rees and Krull).
