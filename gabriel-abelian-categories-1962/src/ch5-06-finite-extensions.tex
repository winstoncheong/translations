\section{Finite Extensions of Commutative Noetherian Rings}

--- In this paragraph, $R$ is a commutative noetherian ring. We denote
by $A$ a unitary $R$-algebra, finite and faithful (cf.~\S1). Such an
algebra has enough two-sided ideals. We take an interest on the one
hand in the relations between the prime spectrum of $A$ and the prime
spectrum of $R$; on the other hand we study the structure of the
indecomposable injective $A$-modules. The results we arrive at are
those everyone expects; the methods used are those everyone uses.

It is permissible to suppose $R$ contained in the centre $Z(A)$ of
$A$. We then say that $A$ is a \emph{finite extension} of $R$. For
every prime two-sided ideal $\mathfrak{P}$ of $A$, $R\cap\mathfrak{P}$
is then a prime ideal of $R$.

\begin{proposition}
Let $A$ be a finite extension of a commutative noetherian ring $R$.
The following assertions are true:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] The map
  $\mathfrak{P}\leadsto\mathfrak{P}\cap R$ is a surjection from
  $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ onto $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$.
\item[\normalfont b.] If $\mathfrak{P}$ and $\mathfrak{Q}$ are two
  prime two-sided ideals of $A$, the conditions
  $\mathfrak{P}\supset\mathfrak{Q}$ and
  $\mathfrak{P}\ne\mathfrak{Q}$ imply
  $\mathfrak{P}\cap R\ne\mathfrak{Q}\cap R$.
\item[\normalfont c.] If $\mathfrak{p}$ is a prime ideal of $R$ and if
  the $R$-module $A$ can be generated by $n$ elements, the inverse
  image of $\{\mathfrak{p}\}$ in $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ contains at
  most $n$ elements.
\end{description}
\end{proposition}

The proposition will follow from the following lemmas:

\begin{lemma}
Let $A$ be a ring, $S$ a multiplicative set contained in the centre
$Z(A)$ of $A$ and $\varphi$ the canonical map from $A$ into $A_S$.
The maps $\mathfrak{P}\leadsto\mathfrak{P}_S$ and
$\mathfrak{Q}\leadsto\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{Q})$ define a bijective
correspondence between the prime two-sided ideals of $A$ which do not
meet $S$ and the prime two-sided ideals of $A_S$.
\end{lemma}

The proof of Lemma~1 is left to the reader.

\begin{lemma}
The hypotheses and the notations are those of Proposition~11. For
every prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$,
$A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a finite non-zero
algebra over the commutative field
$R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.R_{\mathfrak{p}}$. Moreover, the map
$\mathfrak{P}\leadsto\mathfrak{P}_{\mathfrak{p}}/
\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ defines a bijection from the set of
prime two-sided ideals $\mathfrak{P}$ of $A$ satisfying
$\mathfrak{P}\cap R=\mathfrak{p}$, onto the set of prime two-sided
ideals of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$.
\end{lemma}

It is clear that $A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a
finite algebra over $R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.R_{\mathfrak{p}}$.
Since $\mathfrak{p}.R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is the Jacobson radical of
$R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ and $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is an $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module
of finite type, the quotient
$A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is not zero
(Nakayama's lemma).

On the other hand, the prime two-sided ideals of
$A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ are in bijective
correspondence with the prime two-sided ideals of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$
which contain $\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$. Lemma~1 shows that the
latter correspond bijectively to the prime two-sided ideals
$\mathfrak{P}$ such that $\mathfrak{P}\cap R=\mathfrak{p}$. This proves
Lemma~2.

Assertion~(a) of the proposition follows from the fact that
$A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a non-zero artinian
ring; the prime two-sided ideals of this ring are the maximal two-sided
ideals. Assertion~(b) of the proposition follows from the fact that
$A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ contains no distinct
``nested'' prime two-sided ideals. Finally, let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be
generators of the $R$-module underlying $A$; the images of these
generators in $A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$
generate the $(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.R_{\mathfrak{p}})$-module
underlying $A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$; whence the
formula
$[A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}:
R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}.R_{\mathfrak{p}}]\le n$; this
proves~(c).

\begin{corollary}
The notations and the hypotheses are those of Proposition~11. Let
$\mathfrak{p}_0\subset\ldots\subset\mathfrak{p}_n$ be a chain of prime
ideals of $R$ and
$\mathfrak{P}_0\subset\ldots\subset\mathfrak{P}_i$ a chain of prime
two-sided ideals of $A$ such that
$\mathfrak{P}_j\cap R=\mathfrak{p}_j$, $0\le j\le i<n$. There exists a
chain
$\mathfrak{P}_i\subset\mathfrak{P}_{i+1}\subset\ldots\subset
\mathfrak{P}_n$ formed of prime two-sided ideals of $A$ such that
$\mathfrak{P}_k\cap R=\mathfrak{p}_k$ for $i\le k\le n$.
\end{corollary}

It follows indeed from the equality
$\mathfrak{P}_i\cap R=\mathfrak{p}_i$ that $A/\mathfrak{P}_i$
contains $R/\mathfrak{p}_i$ and is a finite extension of it. By
assertion~(a) of Proposition~11, there is therefore a prime two-sided
ideal $\mathfrak{Q}_{i+1}$ of $A/\mathfrak{P}_i$ such that
\[
\mathfrak{Q}_{i+1}\cap(R/\mathfrak{p}_i)=
\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}/\mathfrak{p}_i.
\]
The inverse image $\mathfrak{P}_{i+1}$ of $\mathfrak{Q}_{i+1}$ in $A$
is a prime two-sided ideal of $A$ and satisfies the equality
$\mathfrak{P}_{i+1}\cap R=\mathfrak{p}_{i+1}$.

We construct $\mathfrak{P}_{i+2}$ from $\mathfrak{P}_{i+1}$ as we
constructed $\mathfrak{P}_{i+1}$ from $\mathfrak{P}_i$; the
construction continues by induction~\ldots.

\begin{corollary}
The notations and the hypotheses are those of Proposition~11. If $M$
is an $A$-module, we denote by $\rho M$ the $R$-module underlying $M$.
The Krull dimension of $M$ is finite if and only if the Krull
dimension of $\rho M$ is finite. In the latter case, these two
dimensions are equal.
\end{corollary}

Let indeed $\mathbf{C}$ (resp.~$\mathbf{D}$) be the localizing
subcategory of $\operatorname{mod}A$ (resp.~of
$\operatorname{mod}R$) generated by $M$ (resp.~by $\rho M$). It is
known that $\mathbf{C}$ can be generated by modules $A/\mathfrak{P}$,
where $\mathfrak{P}$ is a prime two-sided ideal of $A$. Likewise,
$\mathbf{D}$ can be generated by the $R$-modules
$\rho(A/\mathfrak{P})$, where $\mathfrak{P}$ runs through the prime
two-sided ideals of $A$ such that
$A/\mathfrak{P}\in\mathbf{C}$. From this we deduce the following
formulas:
\[
\operatorname{Kdim}M=
\sup_{A/\mathfrak{P}\in\mathbf{C}}\operatorname{Kdim}A/\mathfrak{P},
\qquad
\operatorname{Kdim}\rho M=
\sup_{A/\mathfrak{P}\in\mathbf{C}}
\operatorname{Kdim}\rho(A/\mathfrak{P}).
\]

In other words, it suffices to prove the corollary when $M$ is of the
form $A/\mathfrak{P}$, where $\mathfrak{P}$ is a prime two-sided ideal
of $A$. In this case, the annihilator of the noetherian $R$-module
$\rho(A/\mathfrak{P})$ is $\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{P}\cap R$. It is
known that the equality
$\operatorname{Kdim}\rho(A/\mathfrak{P})=
\operatorname{Kdim}R/\mathfrak{p}$ follows from this.

By Corollary~3 to Proposition~7, it therefore remains to prove the
equivalence of the following assertions:
\begin{itemize}
\item Every chain of prime two-sided ideals of $A$ containing
  $\mathfrak{P}$ has at most $n+1$ elements.
\item Every chain of prime ideals of $R$ containing $\mathfrak{p}$ has
  at most $n+1$ elements.
\end{itemize}

The equivalence of these assertions follows from Corollary~1 and
assertion~(b) of Proposition~11.

We shall henceforth say that the map
$\mathfrak{P}\leadsto\mathfrak{P}\cap R$ is the \emph{canonical map}
from $\operatorname{Spec}(A)=\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{mod}A)$
onto $\operatorname{Spec}(R)=\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{mod}R)$.
If $\mathbf{D}$ is a localizing subcategory of $\operatorname{mod}R$,
we denote by $\rho^{-1}(\mathbf{D})$ the following localizing
subcategory of $\operatorname{mod}A$: an $A$-module $M$ belongs to
$\rho^{-1}(\mathbf{D})$ if and only if $\rho M$ belongs to
$\mathbf{D}$. The spectrum of the category $\rho^{-1}(\mathbf{D})$ is
evidently the inverse image of $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{D})$ under
the canonical map from $\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{mod}A)$ onto
$\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{mod}R)$. The reader will verify the
converse: a localizing subcategory $\mathbf{C}$ of $\operatorname{mod}A$
is of the form $\rho^{-1}(\mathbf{D})$ if and only if
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C})$ is the inverse image of a subset of
$\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{mod}R)$.

\begin{proposition}
Let $A$ be a finite extension of a commutative noetherian ring $R$.
Let $\mathbf{D}$ be a localizing subcategory of $\operatorname{mod}R$
and $\rho$ the functor which associates to every $A$-module $M$ the
$R$-module underlying $M$. The localizing subcategory
$\rho^{-1}(\mathbf{D})$ of $\operatorname{mod}A$ is stable under
injective envelopes.
\end{proposition}

The proof is analogous to that of Proposition~10; we give only a
sketch of it: if $\mathfrak{P}$ is a prime two-sided ideal of $A$, one
shows that the injective envelope of $A/\mathfrak{P}$ belongs to the
localizing subcategory generated by the modules $A/\mathfrak{Q}$,
where $\mathfrak{Q}$ runs through the prime two-sided ideals such that
$\mathfrak{Q}\cap R=\mathfrak{P}\cap R$. An $A$-module $M$ belongs to
this subcategory if and only if $a_M$ is almost-nilpotent for every
element $a$ of $\mathfrak{P}\cap R$. Finally one shows that if $I$ is
an indecomposable injective and $a$ an element of $R$, the homothety
$a_I$ is either bijective or almost nilpotent (cf.~Lemma~2, \S5).

\begin{unnumberedcorollary}
Let $A$ be a ring satisfying the hypotheses of Proposition~12. If the
Krull dimension of an $A$-module $M$ is zero, the Krull dimension of
its injective envelope is zero.
\end{unnumberedcorollary}

Corollary~2 to Proposition~9 therefore applies to the ring $A$. In
what follows we borrow the notations of that corollary. For example,
if $\mathfrak{p}$ is a prime ideal of $R$, $\hat R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is
the completion of $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ for the
$\mathfrak{p}.R_{\mathfrak{p}}$-adic topology.

In the following proposition, $\Omega R$ denotes the set of maximal
ideals of $R$.

\begin{proposition}
Let $A$ be a finite extension of a commutative noetherian ring $R$.
As $M$ runs through the noetherian $A$-modules, the functor
$M\leadsto\hat M$ is isomorphic to the functor
$M\leadsto\prod_{\mathfrak{m}\in\Omega R}M\otimes_R
\hat R_{\mathfrak{m}}$.
\end{proposition}

Let indeed $M'$ be a submodule of $M$ such that $M/M'$ is of finite
length. Then $\rho(M/M')$ is a noetherian $R$-module whose Krull
dimension is zero. It follows that $\rho(M/M')$ is of finite length.
On the other hand, let $\mathfrak{a}$ be an ideal of $R$ such that
$R/\mathfrak{a}$ is of finite length; then $M/M.\mathfrak{a}$ is an
$A$-module of finite length.

It follows from these remarks that the $A$-submodules of $M$ of finite
colength define the same topology as the $R$-submodules of finite
colength. It therefore suffices to prove that the completion of $M$
for the latter topology is the product
$\prod_{\mathfrak{m}\in\Omega R}M\otimes_R\hat R_{\mathfrak{m}}$.
This is a well-known proposition of commutative algebra.

\begin{corollary}
The notations and the hypotheses are those of Proposition~13. If
$Z(A)$ is the centre of $A$, the centre of $\hat A$ is the ring
$\widehat{Z(A)}$.
\end{corollary}

The ring $Z(A)$ is indeed an $R$-module of finite type. By the
preceding proposition, $\widehat{Z(A)}$ is therefore equal to the
product
$\prod_{\mathfrak{m}\in\Omega R}Z(A)\otimes_R\hat R_{\mathfrak{m}}$.
It remains to show that $Z(A)\otimes_R\hat R_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is the
centre of $A\otimes_R\hat R_{\mathfrak{m}}$:

\begin{lemma}
Let $u:R\to S$ be a homomorphism of commutative rings with unit. If
$S$ is $R$-flat \cite{lesieur-croisot-1}, $Z(A)\otimes_RS$ is the
centre of $A\otimes_RS$.
\end{lemma}

Let indeed $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ be generators of the $R$-algebra $A$; let
$v_i:A\to A$ be the map defined by the formula
$v_i(a)=a_i.a-a.a_i$, $1\le i\le r$.

The centre $Z(A)$ of $A$ is the intersection of the kernels
$\operatorname{Ker}v_i$. Since $S$ is $R$-flat, it follows that
$Z(A)\otimes_RS$ is the intersection of the kernels of the maps
$v_i\otimes_RS$. The formulas
$(v_i\otimes_RS)(x)=(a_i\otimes_R1).x-x.(a_i\otimes_R1)$ show that this
last intersection is the centre of $A\otimes_RS$.

Let now $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal of $Z(A)$. We denote by
$(\operatorname{mod}A)_{\mathfrak{p}}$ the localizing subcategory of
$\operatorname{mod}A$ defined as follows: an $A$-module $M$ belongs to
$(\operatorname{mod}A)_{\mathfrak{p}}$ if and only if $a_M$ is
almost-nilpotent for every element $a$ of $\mathfrak{p}$. Likewise, we
denote by $(\operatorname{mod}A)_0$ the localizing subcategory formed
of the $A$-modules whose Krull dimension is zero.

\begin{corollary}
The notations and the hypotheses are those of Proposition~13. The map
$\mathfrak{m}\leadsto(\operatorname{mod}A)_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is a
bijection from the set of maximal ideals of $Z(A)$ onto the set of
connected components of the locally finite category
$(\operatorname{mod}A)_0$.
\end{corollary}

Let indeed $Z$ be the centre of the category
$\mathbf{A}=(\operatorname{mod}A)_0$. Suppose the ring $Z$ isomorphic
to the product of a family of rings $(Z_i)_{i\in I}$. We then identify
$Z$ with this product. If $e_i$ is the unit element of $Z_i$, we
denote by $\mathbf{A}_i$ the localizing subcategory of $\mathbf{A}$
whose objects are the $A$-modules $M\in\Ob\mathbf{A}$ such that
$e_j(M)=0$ for $j\ne i$. It is clear that the category $\mathbf{A}$ is
equivalent to the product of the categories $\mathbf{A}_i$. In other
words, there is a bijective correspondence between the decompositions
of $Z$ into a product of rings and the decompositions of $\mathbf{A}$
into a product of categories.

Now we have seen in the preceding paragraph that the category
$(\operatorname{mod}A)_0$ is identified with the category of discrete
topological right modules over the topological ring $\hat A$. If $M$
is an $A$-module of dimension zero, one defines in particular a
bilinear map $M\times\hat A\to M$ extending the bilinear map defining
the $A$-module structure of $M$; we still denote by $m.a$ the image of
$(m,a)\in M\times\hat A$ under this map. If $a$ is an element of the
centre of $\hat A$, we denote by $a_M$ the $A$-linear map
$m\mapsto m.a$ from $M$ to $M$. As $M$ varies, the maps
$M\leadsto a_M$ define an isomorphism from the centre
$\widehat{Z(A)}$ of $\hat A$ onto $Z$. The corollary follows from this
and the preceding remarks.

\begin{corollary}
The notations and the hypotheses are those of Proposition~13. The
following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] The map
  $\mathfrak{P}\leadsto\mathfrak{P}\cap Z(A)$ from
  $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ onto $\operatorname{Spec}(Z(A))$ is
  bijective.
\item[\normalfont b.] Every localizing subcategory of
  $\operatorname{mod}A$ is stable under injective envelopes.
\end{description}
\end{corollary}

$(a)\Rightarrow(b)$: This follows from Proposition~12.

$(b)\Rightarrow(a)$: If every localizing subcategory of
$\operatorname{mod}A$ is stable under injective envelopes, the same
holds \emph{a fortiori} for every quotient category of
$\operatorname{mod}A$. In particular, if $\mathfrak{p}$ is a prime
ideal of $Z(A)$, every localizing subcategory of
$\operatorname{mod}A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is stable under injective
envelopes.

Let therefore $\mathfrak{P}$ be a prime two-sided ideal of $A$ such
that $\mathfrak{P}\cap Z(A)=\mathfrak{p}$: let $\mathbf{C}$ be the
localizing subcategory of $\operatorname{mod}A_{\mathfrak{p}}$
generated by $A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{P}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$; let
$\mathbf{D}$ be the localizing subcategory of
$\operatorname{mod}A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ generated by the simple
$A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-modules not annihilated by $\mathfrak{P}$. Since
$\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{D}$ are stable under injective envelopes,
the category $(\operatorname{mod}A_{\mathfrak{p}})_0$ is equivalent to
the product $\mathbf{C}\prod\mathbf{D}$ (cf.~the proof of the corollary
to Theorem~2, Chapter~IV). By Lemma~3, $(Z(A))_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is the
centre of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$. It follows that the category
$\mathbf{D}$ is zero (Corollary~2); this completes the proof.

Assertion~(a) of Corollary~3 is for example satisfied if the
$Z(A)$-algebra $A$ satisfies the equivalent conditions of
Proposition~2. It is also satisfied for the maximal orders of
arithmetic.

We end this paragraph with the study of the pseudocompact ring
associated with the locally finite category $(\operatorname{mod}A)_0$.
If $\mathfrak{m}$ is a maximal ideal of the commutative ring $R$, we
denote by $E_{\mathfrak{m}}$ the injective envelope of the $R$-module
$R/\mathfrak{m}$. We denote by $E$ the direct sum of the $R$-modules
$E_{\mathfrak{m}}$ as $\mathfrak{m}$ runs through the maximal ideals
of $R$.

Let $M$ be a right $A$-module of finite length, $a$ an element of $A$
and $f$ an $R$-linear map from $M$ to $E$. We denote by $a.f$ the
$R$-linear map from $M$ to $E$ defined by the following formula:
\[
(a.f)(m)=f(m.a).
\]
The map $(a,f)\leadsto a.f$ defines a left $A$-module structure on the
abelian group $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,E)$. In what follows,
$\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,E)$ will always be equipped with this
structure:

\begin{proposition}
Let $A$ be a finite extension of a commutative noetherian ring $R$.
Let $E$ be the direct sum of the injective envelopes of the
$R$-modules $R/\mathfrak{m}$, where $\mathfrak{m}$ runs through the
maximal ideals of $R$. The functor
$M\leadsto\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,E)$ defines a duality between the
right $A$-modules of finite length and the left $A$-modules of finite
length.
\end{proposition}

Let $\mathbf{A}$ be the category of right $A$-modules of finite
length, $\mathbf{B}$ the category of left $A$-modules of finite length.
One sees as in the proof of Corollary~6 to Theorem~4 (Chapter~IV) that
$\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,E)$ is an $R$-module of finite length, \emph{a
fortiori} therefore an $A$-module of finite length. This shows that
$M\leadsto\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,E)$ is a contravariant functor from
$\mathbf{A}$ to $\mathbf{B}$; this functor will be denoted $T$ in the
rest of this proof.

Consider now a left $A$-module $N$ of finite length. One then equips
the abelian group $\operatorname{Hom}_R(N,E)$ with a right $A$-module
structure: if $a$ is an element of $A$ and $g$ an element of
$\operatorname{Hom}_R(N,E)$, $g.a$ is defined by the formula
$(g.a)(n)=g(a.n)$. One shows as before that $\operatorname{Hom}_R(N,E)$
is a right $A$-module of finite length; the functor
$N\leadsto\operatorname{Hom}_R(N,E)$ from $\mathbf{B}$ to $\mathbf{A}$
will be denoted $S$.

By Proposition~12 (Chapter~I), it suffices to prove that the functors
$S\circ T$ and $T\circ S$ are isomorphic respectively to the functors
$I_{\mathbf{A}}$ and $I_{\mathbf{B}}$: if $M$ is an object of
$\mathbf{A}$ and if $m$ is an element of $M$, we denote by $m'$ the
$R$-linear map\ednote{The original prints ``$A$-linear''
(\textit{A-linéaire}) here; since $E$ carries no $A$-module
structure, the map $m'$ can only be $R$-linear --- and indeed must be,
to belong to $S(T(M))=\operatorname{Hom}_R(T(M),E)$.
We print $R$-linear.} from $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,E)$ to $E$
defined by the formula
\[
m'(f)=f(m).
\]

As $M$ varies, the maps $m\leadsto m'$ define a morphism from the
identity functor $I_{\mathbf{A}}$ to $S\circ T$. One sees as in the
proof of Corollary~6 to Theorem~4 (Chapter~IV), that the map
$m\leadsto m'$ is an isomorphism of $R$-modules; since this map is
$A$-linear, it is also an isomorphism of $A$-modules. It follows that
$I_{\mathbf{A}}$ is isomorphic to $S\circ T$. One would prove in the
same way that $I_{\mathbf{B}}$ is isomorphic to $T\circ S$.
\hfill Q.E.D.

It follows from Proposition~13 that the topology of $\hat A$ can be
defined by two-sided ideals $\mathfrak{a}$ of finite colength
(i.e.~the right $\hat A$-module and the left $\hat A$-module
underlying $\hat A/\mathfrak{a}$ are of finite length). The
topological ring $\hat A$ is therefore pseudocompact on the left and
on the right. Moreover, it is clear that the duality we have exhibited
between $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ extends to a duality between
$(\operatorname{mod}A)_0$ and the category $PC(\hat A)$ formed of the
pseudocompact left $\hat A$-modules. Hence the

\begin{corollary}
The notations and the hypotheses are those of Proposition~14. The
pseudocompact ring associated with the category of right $A$-modules
of Krull dimension zero, is equivalent to the ring $\hat A$. The dual
ring of $\hat A$ is therefore equivalent to the ring opposite to
$\hat A$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}
The notations and the hypotheses are those of Proposition~14. Let $I$
be an indecomposable injective right $A$-module, $\mathfrak{P}$ the
two-sided ideal associated with $I$ and $\mathfrak{p}$ the
intersection $\mathfrak{P}\cap R$. Then $I$ is a right artinian module
over the ring $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$.
\end{corollary}

Let indeed $\mathbf{C}$ be the localizing subcategory of
$\operatorname{mod}A$ whose noetherian objects are the right
$A$-modules annihilated by an element of $R-\mathfrak{p}$. Since $I$
is $\mathbf{C}$-closed, the $A$-module structure of $I$ extends in one
and only one way to a right $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module structure. Since
the quotient category $\operatorname{mod}A/\mathbf{C}$ is identified
with $\operatorname{mod}A_{\mathfrak{p}}$, $I$ is an indecomposable
injective $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module (corollary to Proposition~3). The
prime two-sided ideal associated with this injective
$A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module is $\mathfrak{P}.A_{\mathfrak{p}}$. It
follows that $I$ is the injective envelope of a simple
$A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module; the corollary to Proposition~12 therefore
shows that $I$ is an $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module whose Krull dimension
is zero. Now the category $(\operatorname{mod}A_{\mathfrak{p}})_0$ is
dual to the category of pseudocompact left $\hat A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-modules
(Corollary~1). Since $\hat A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a noetherian ring,
the projective envelope of a simple object of
$PC(\hat A_{\mathfrak{p}})$ is noetherian. Corollary~2 follows by
duality.

We leave to the reader the pleasure of continuing this investigation.
He may in particular seek the pseudocompact rings associated with the
categories
$(\operatorname{mod}A)_{n+1}/(\operatorname{mod}A)_n$. He may also
seek their connected components.
