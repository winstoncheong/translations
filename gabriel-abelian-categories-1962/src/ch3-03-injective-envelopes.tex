\section{Categories with Injective Envelopes}
--- We shall use the notations of the preceding paragraph.

\begin{proposition}
Let $\mathbf{C}$ be a thick subcategory of an abelian category
$\mathbf{A}$. Let $i\colon M\to I$ be an injective envelope of an object
$M$ of $\mathbf{A}$ which contains no nonzero object of $\mathbf{C}$. Then
$I$ is $\mathbf{C}$-closed and the morphism $Ti$ is an injective envelope
of $TM$.
\end{proposition}

Let us take in fact $N$ a subobject of $I$ belonging to $\mathbf{C}$; then
$i^{-1}(N)$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$ and is therefore zero; since $i$ is an
essential extension, it follows that $N$ is zero. This proves that $I$ is
$\mathbf{C}$-closed [lemma~1 (b), \S~2].

An argument already used in the proof of lemma~3 (\S~2) shows that $Ti$ is
an essential extension. It remains therefore to show that $TI$ is
injective: let us give ourselves for this purpose a monomorphism
$f'\colon TI\to TM$; this monomorphism is the image of a morphism
$f\colon I'\to M/M'$, where $I'$ and $M'$ are subobjects of $I$ and of
$M$ such that $I/I'$ and $M'$ belong to $\mathbf{C}$\ednote{The next
sentence uses $Tp^{M}_{M/M'}$ as an isomorphism, and that holds exactly
when $M/M'$ --- not $M'$ --- belongs to $\mathbf{C}$; the original may
well be meant to print $M/M'$. We leave the text as printed, since the
surrounding argument does not decide between the two readings.}
Since $Tf$ is a monomorphism, $\operatorname{Ker}f$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$
and is therefore zero. In other words, $f$ is a monomorphism and the
canonical morphism $i$ of $I'$ into $I$ is the composite of $f$ and of a
morphism $g\colon M/M'\to I$. We thus arrive at the following commutative
diagram:

% The primes inside the labels are written \prime, and the slashes too:
% pgfkeys splits tikz-cd options on top-level commas and chokes on the
% bare apostrophe, so both are made explicit here.
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}
TI\prime \arrow[r, "Tf"] \arrow[d, "Ti"'] &
T(M/M\prime) \arrow[ld, "Tg"'] \arrow[d, "Tp^{M}_{M/M\prime}"] \\
TI \arrow[r, "f\prime"'] & TM
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}

Since $Ti$ and $Tp^{M}_{M/M'}$ are isomorphisms, $f'$ is an isomorphism of
$TI$ onto a direct factor of $TM$.\hfill C.Q.F.D.

\begin{corollary}
If $\mathbf{C}$ is a thick subcategory of a category with injective
envelopes $\mathbf{A}$, the following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[a.] $\mathbf{C}$ is a localizing subcategory of $\mathbf{A}$.
\item[b.] Every object $M$ of $\mathbf{A}$ contains a subobject which is
      maximal among the subobjects of $M$ belonging to $\mathbf{C}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

The corollary follows from Propositions~6 and 4.

\begin{corollary}
Let $\mathbf{C}$ be a localizing subcategory of a category with injective
envelopes $\mathbf{A}$. Then $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$ is a category with
injective envelopes; every injective object of $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$ is
isomorphic to an object $TI$, where $I$ is an injective object of
$\mathbf{A}$ containing no nonzero subobject of $\mathbf{C}$.
Conversely, every injective object $J$ of $\mathbf{A}$ is isomorphic to a
direct sum $J_1\oplus SJ_2$, where $J_1$ is the injective envelope of an
object of $\mathbf{C}$ and where $J_2$ is an injective object of
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$. Moreover, these conditions determine $J_1$ and
$J_2$ up to isomorphism.
\end{corollary}

According to Corollary~1, every object of $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$ is
indeed isomorphic to an object $TM$, where $M$ contains no nonzero
subobject of $\mathbf{C}$. The first two assertions therefore follow
directly from Proposition~6. To prove the rest, we shall take for $J_1$
the injective envelope of the largest subobject of $J$ belonging to
$\mathbf{C}$.

\begin{corollary}
The hypotheses are those of Corollary~2. Let us suppose in addition that
the injective envelope (in $\mathbf{A}$) of an object of $\mathbf{C}$
belongs to $\mathbf{C}$. Then $TJ$ is an injective object of
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$ if $J$ is an injective object of $\mathbf{A}$.
\end{corollary}

With the notations of Corollary~2, $TJ$ indeed reduces to $TSJ_2$.

Corollary~3 is often used in the following way: let
$F\colon\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{D}$ be a functor of
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$ into an abelian category $\mathbf{D}$, with
$\mathbf{C}$ satisfying the conditions of Corollary~3. Since the functor
$T$ is exact and transforms injectives into injectives, we know from [10]
that the derived functors $R^{n}(F\circ T)$ are identified with the
functors $(R^{n}F)\circ T$. This is true in particular if $F$ is the
functor $N\leadsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}}(TM,N)$. We
deduce Corollaries~4 and 5:

\begin{corollary}
The hypotheses are those of Corollary~3. The functor
$N\leadsto\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}}(TM,TN)$ is
isomorphic to the $n$th derived functor of the functor
$N\leadsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(M,STN)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}
The hypotheses are those of Corollary~3. The homological dimension of
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$ is less than or equal to the homological dimension
of $\mathbf{A}$.
\end{corollary}

Let us recall that the homological dimension of a category $\mathbf{A}$ is
the smallest integer $n$ such that
$\operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}_{\mathbf{A}}(M,N)$ is zero for every pair
$(M,N)$ of objects of $\mathbf{A}$.

The groups $\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}}(M,N)$ can be
computed in another way when the section functor is exact: indeed $S$
transforms the injective objects of $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$ into injective
objects of $\mathbf{A}$. Consequently, if $G$ is a functor of $\mathbf{A}$
into an abelian category $\mathbf{D}$, the functors $(R^{n}G)\circ S$ and
$R^{n}(G\circ S)$ are still isomorphic. This is true in particular if $G$
is the functor $X\leadsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(M,X)$.

\begin{corollary}
The hypotheses are those of Corollary~2. Let us suppose in addition that
the section functor is exact. Then the functors
$N\leadsto\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}}(TM,N)$ and
$N\leadsto\operatorname{Ext}^{n}_{\mathbf{A}}(M,SN)$ are then
isomorphic.
\end{corollary}

The following proposition examines the case in which the section functor
is exact:

\begin{proposition}
Let $\mathbf{C}$ be a localizing subcategory of a category with injective
envelopes $\mathbf{A}$. The following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[a.] The localization functor (or the section functor) is exact.
\item[b.] If $N$ is a subobject of $M$ and if $N$ and $M$ are
      $\mathbf{C}$-closed, then $M/N$ is $\mathbf{C}$-closed.
\item[c.] If $I$ is an injective containing no nonzero subobject of
      $\mathbf{C}$, and if $N$ is a $\mathbf{C}$-closed subobject of $I$,
      then $I/N$ is $\mathbf{C}$-closed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

$(a)\Rightarrow(b)\Rightarrow(c)$: clear.

$(c)\Rightarrow(a)$: if $L$ is the localization functor $S\circ T$, we
are going to show that the first derived functor $R^{1}L$ of $L$ is zero.

Let us first suppose that $M$ is an object of $\mathbf{C}$; let
$i\colon M\to I$ be an injective envelope of $M$\ednote{The original
prints ``an injective envelope of $I$'', which is impossible for a
morphism with source $M$; we print ``of $M$'', which is what the rest of
the argument requires.} and let $p\colon I\to N$ be the cokernel of $i$.
Since $Lp$ is an isomorphism, the exact sequence
\[
  0\to 0=LM \xrightarrow{Li} LI \xrightarrow{Lp} LN \to R^{1}LM \to 0
\]
shows that $R^{1}LM$ is zero.

Let us now suppose that $M$ contains no nonzero object of $\mathbf{C}$,
and let us keep the preceding notations. Since $Lp$ is an epimorphism,
$R^{1}LM$ is zero.

In the general case, let $M'$ be the largest subobject of $M$ belonging
to $\mathbf{C}$ and let $M''=M/M'$. The exact sequence
$R^{1}LM'\to R^{1}LM\to R^{1}LM''$ proves that $R^{1}LM$ is zero.

\begin{unnumberedcorollary}
Let $\mathbf{C}$ be a localizing subcategory of a category with injective
envelopes $\mathbf{A}$. If the homological dimension of $\mathbf{A}$ is
$0$ or $1$, the localization functor is exact.
\end{unnumberedcorollary}
