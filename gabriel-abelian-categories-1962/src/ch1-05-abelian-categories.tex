\section{Abelian Categories}
--- Let $\Cat$ be an additive category and let $f\colon M\to N$ be a morphism
of $\Cat$. We suppose that $f$ has a kernel, a cokernel, an image and a
coimage \cite{grothendieck-1}. Let $i$ and $j$ (resp.\ $p$ and $q$) be the canonical morphisms
from the kernel $\operatorname{Ker} f$ into $M$ and from the image
$\operatorname{Im} f$ into $N$ (resp.\ from $N$ into the cokernel
$\operatorname{Coker} f$ and from $M$ into the coimage $\operatorname{Coim}
f$). We know that there exists a morphism $\mathfrak{S}$ of $\operatorname{Coim}
f$ into $\operatorname{Im} f$, and one only, such that
$f = j\circ\mathfrak{S}\circ q$. We shall say that $\mathfrak{S}$ is the
\emph{canonical morphism} of $\operatorname{Coim} f$ into $\operatorname{Im} f$.

An additive category $\Cat$ is \emph{abelian} if the following axioms hold:

\begin{description}
\item[$\mathrm{CAb}\,1$] For every morphism $f$, the kernel and the cokernel
      of $f$ exist.

\item[$\mathrm{CAb}\,2$] For every morphism $f$, the canonical morphism of
      $\operatorname{Coim} f$ into $\operatorname{Im} f$ is an isomorphism.
\end{description}

The dual category of an abelian category is abelian.

We assume the reader to be familiar with the elementary reasoning of abelian
categories. When only a finite number of objects and morphisms is considered,
the arguments used are those of abelian groups.

To increase this resemblance, we constantly use the following notations: if
$N$ is a subobject of $M$, $M/N$ denotes the cokernel of $i_N^M$; if $N$ and
$P$ are two subobjects of $M$, $N + P$ denotes the image of the morphism of
$N \oplus P$ into $M$ which is defined by $i_N^M$ and $i_P^M$; likewise
$N \cap P$ denotes the kernel of the morphism of $M$ into $M/N \oplus M/P$
which is defined by the canonical epimorphisms of $M$ onto $M/N$ and $M/P$.\ednote{The
original prints $M\cap N$ here; the kernel of $M\to M/N\oplus M/P$ is
$N\cap P$. We print $N\cap P$.}
If $f\colon M\to N$ is a morphism of an abelian category and $P$ a subobject
of $M$, $f(P)$ denotes the image of $f\circ i_P^M$; likewise, if $Q$ is a
subobject of $N$, $f^{-1}(Q)$ denotes the kernel of the composite of $f$
with the canonical morphism of $N$ into $N/Q$.

Noether's isomorphism theorems remain valid in an abelian category. The same
is true of the results on the composition series of an object, on the
Jordan--Hölder series and on the length of an object. Likewise, we refer the
reader to \cite{cartan-eilenberg} for the customary remarks on exact sequences, exact functors,
functors exact on the left or on the right, direct factors and injective
objects.

\begin{proposition}
If $\Cat$ is an abelian category, there exists a fibred sum (resp.\ a fibred
product) for diagram~(1) of \S~3 [resp.\ diagram~(2) of \S~3].
\end{proposition}

We recall only the construction of $B\Sigma_A C$: if $j_B$ and $j'_C$ are the
canonical morphisms of $B$ and $C$ into $B \oplus C$, then $B\Sigma_A C$ is
the cokernel of the morphism $j_B\circ h - j'_C\circ k$. The canonical
morphism $j$ of $B$ into $B\Sigma_A C$ is the composite of $j_B$ and of the
canonical morphism of $B \oplus C$ onto $B\Sigma_A C$. The kernel of $j$ is
the image by $h$ of $\operatorname{Ker} k$; the canonical morphism of $C$
into $B\Sigma_A C$ induces an isomorphism of $\operatorname{Coker} k$ onto
$\operatorname{Coker} j$.

Let us also recall the construction of $B\Pi_A C$: if $q_B$ and $q'_C$ are the
canonical morphisms of $B \oplus C$ into $B$ and $C$, then $B\Pi_A C$ is the
kernel of the morphism $f\circ q_B - g\circ q'_C$. The canonical morphism $q$
of $B\Pi_A C$ into $B$ is the composite of the canonical morphism of
$B\Pi_A C$ into $B \oplus C$ and of $q_B$. The cokernel of $q$ is the
quotient of $B$ by $f^{-1}(g(C))$; the canonical morphism of $B\Pi_A C$ into
$C$ induces an isomorphism of $\operatorname{Ker} q$ onto $\operatorname{Ker} g$.
