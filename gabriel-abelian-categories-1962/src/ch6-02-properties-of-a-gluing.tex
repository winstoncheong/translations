\section{Properties of a Gluing of Abelian Categories}

--- We keep the notations of Section~1.

\begin{lemma}
Let $T$ be the functor $(C, D, \sigma) \leadsto D$. Suppose that
$\operatorname{Ker} F$ is a localizing subcategory of $\mathbf{C}$ and
that $F$ defines, on passing to the quotient, an equivalence between
$\mathbf{C}/\operatorname{Ker} F$ and $\mathbf{B}$. The functor
$M \leadsto (M, 0, 0)$ is then an isomorphism from
$\operatorname{Ker} F$ onto $\operatorname{Ker} T$,
$\operatorname{Ker} T$ is a localizing subcategory of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$, and $T$ defines, on
passing to the quotient, an equivalence between
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}/\operatorname{Ker} T$ and
$\mathbf{D}$.
\end{lemma}

Let indeed $H$ be a functor adjoint to $F$, $\chi$ an isomorphism from
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(., H.)$ onto
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(F., .)$ and $X$ the morphism from
$F \circ H$ onto $I_{\mathbf{B}}$ associated with $\chi$ (cf.\
Chapter~I, \S7); $X$ is known to be a functorial isomorphism. If $S$
denotes the functor $D \leadsto (HGD, D, X(GD))$, $T \circ S$ is the
identity functor of $\mathbf{D}$. The identity morphisms $1_D$ thus
define a functorial isomorphism $\Phi$ from $T \circ S$ onto
$I_{\mathbf{D}}$. This isomorphism $\Phi$ makes $S$ a functor adjoint to
$T$, and the lemma follows from Proposition~5 (Chapter~III).

\begin{lemma}
Suppose that $\operatorname{Ker} F$ (resp.\ $\operatorname{Ker} G$) is a
localizing subcategory of $\mathbf{C}$ (resp.\ of $\mathbf{D}$) and that
$F$ (resp.\ $G$) defines, on passing to the quotient, an equivalence
between $\mathbf{C}/\operatorname{Ker} F$ and $\mathbf{B}$ (resp.\
between $\mathbf{D}/\operatorname{Ker} G$ and $\mathbf{B}$). If the
categories $\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{D}$ are locally noetherian, the
same holds for
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$.
\end{lemma}

It is indeed clear that
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ is a $\Univ$-category
with exact inductive limits. If $(C', D', \sigma')$ is a proper
subobject of $(C, D, \sigma)$, let us show that there exists a
noetherian subobject of $(C, D, \sigma)$ which is not contained in
$(C', D', \sigma')$. For this one may suppose $C'$ different from $C$.
There then exists a noetherian subobject $C''$ of $C$ which is not
contained in $C'$. It thus remains to ``lift'' $FC''$ to a noetherian
subobject of $D$.

It remains to prove that there exists a set belonging to $\Univ$ and
having the same cardinality as the set of types of noetherian objects of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$; this follows from the
corresponding properties of $\mathbf{C}$ and of $\mathbf{D}$; we leave
the proof to the reader.

Suppose the hypotheses of Lemma~2 still satisfied and use the notations
introduced in the proof of Lemma~1. If $I$ is an indecomposable
injective object of $\mathbf{D}$, $SI$ is an indecomposable injective
object of $\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$. The map
$I \leadsto SI$ induces an injection from $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{D})$
into
$\operatorname{Sp}(\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}})$.

In accordance with the conventions of Chapter~IV, we identify
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{D})$ with the image of this injection. We
likewise identify $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{B})$ and
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C})$ with subsets of
$\operatorname{Sp}(\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}})$.
Lemma~1 and the remarks of Chapter~IV (\S1) show that
$\operatorname{Sp}(\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}})$ is the
union of the disjoint subsets $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{D})$ and
$\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{Ker} T)$. Since
$\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{Ker} T)$ coincides with
$\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{Ker} F)$ and is contained in
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C})$, one sees that
$\operatorname{Sp}(\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}})$ is the
union of $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C})$ and of
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{D})$. Since
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{D})$ is the union of
$\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{Ker} G)$ and of
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{B})$, one sees that
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{B})$ is the intersection of
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C})$ and of $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{D})$;
one thus has the formulae
\[
(\star\star\star)\qquad
\operatorname{Sp}\left(
\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}\right)
= \operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C}) \cup \operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{D}),\qquad
\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{B})
= \operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C}) \cap \operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{D}).
\]

\begin{theorem}
Let $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ be a noetherian prescheme. One has the
following assertions:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] The category $\mathbf{F}_X$ is locally noetherian.
\item[\normalfont b.] The support $\operatorname{Supp}(I)$ of an
      indecomposable injective $\mathcal{O}_X$-module is a closed
      irreducible subset of $X$. Every non-zero
      $\mathcal{O}_X$-submodule of $I$ has the same support as $I$.
\item[\normalfont c.] The map $I \leadsto \operatorname{supp}(I)$
      induces a bijection from the spectrum of the category
      $\mathbf{F}_X$ onto the set of irreducible closed subsets of $X$.
\end{description}
\end{theorem}

When $X$ is the affine scheme associated with a noetherian ring, the
theorem follows from Proposition~8 and Corollary~2 to Proposition~14 of
Chapter~V. By what was said at the end of Section~1, it thus suffices
to prove that the theorem holds for $X$ if it holds for two open subsets
$U$ and $V$ of $X$ such that $X = U \cup V$. In this case we identify
$\mathbf{F}_X$, $\mathbf{F}_U$, $\mathbf{F}_V$,
$\mathbf{F}_{U \cap V}$, $\rho_U$ and $\rho_V$ with
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$, $\mathbf{C}$,
$\mathbf{D}$, $\mathbf{B}$, $F$ and $G$ respectively (cf.\
Proposition~2). Assertion~(a) is then an immediate consequence of
Lemma~2.

Let us prove assertion~(b): if $I$ is an indecomposable injective
$\mathcal{O}_X$-module, three cases are possible:
--- $I$ belongs to $\operatorname{Sp}(\operatorname{Ker} T)$; in other
words, $I$ contains a non-zero $\mathcal{O}_X$-submodule whose support
is contained in $V' = X - V$. It follows that $I$ belongs to
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C})$, in other words is isomorphic to the
direct image of an indecomposable injective $(\mathcal{O}_X|U)$-module
$J$. Since $U$ satisfies the theorem, the support of $J$ is a closed
irreducible subset contained in $V'$. The support of $I$ consequently
coincides with the support of $J$; assertion~(b) follows.
--- $I$ contains a non-zero $\mathcal{O}_X$-submodule whose support is
contained in $U' = X - U$. An argument analogous to the preceding
argument then shows that the support of $I$ is a closed irreducible
subset contained in $U'$.
--- $I$ belongs to $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{B})$; in other words, the
support of every non-zero $\mathcal{O}_X$-submodule of $I$ meets
$U \cap V$. If $j$ is the canonical injection of $U \cap V$ into $X$,
$I$ is then isomorphic to the direct image $j_*(K)$ of an
indecomposable injective $(\mathcal{O}_X|U \cap V)$-module $K$. Since the
direct image of $K$ in $V$ coincides with $j_*(K)|V$ and is an
indecomposable injective $(\mathcal{O}_X|V)$-module, the support of
$I|V$ is a closed irreducible subset of $V$; moreover, every non-zero
submodule of $I|V$ has the same support as $I|V$. For the same reason,
the support of $I|U$ is closed and irreducible and every non-zero
submodule of $I|U$ has the same support as $I|U$. Assertion~(b) follows.

Assertion~(c) finally follows from the classification we have just made
and from the fact that every closed irreducible subset of $X$ is the
closure of a closed irreducible subset of $U$ or of $V$.

Let us now return to the diagram $(\star)$, and let $R$ and $T$ be the
canonical projections of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ onto $\mathbf{C}$ and
$\mathbf{D}$. We suppose that the categories $\mathbf{C}$ and
$\mathbf{D}$ are locally noetherian, that the hypotheses of Lemma~2
hold and that the localizing subcategories $\operatorname{Ker} F$ and
$\operatorname{Ker} G$ are stable under injective envelopes. It follows
that the subcategories $\operatorname{Ker} T$ and
$\operatorname{Ker} R$ of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ are stable under
injective envelopes and that gluing data $(I, J, \sigma)$ is an
injective object of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ if and only if $I$ and
$J$ are injective objects of $\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{D}$. We propose
to investigate the localizing subcategories of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$; if $\mathbf{C}'$
(resp.\ $\mathbf{D}'$) is a localizing subcategory of $\mathbf{C}$
(resp.\ of $\mathbf{D}$), we denote for this purpose by $F\mathbf{C}'$
(resp.\ by $G\mathbf{D}'$) the smallest localizing subcategory of
$\mathbf{B}$ containing the objects $FC$ (resp.\ $GD$), as $C$
(resp.\ $D$) ranges over the objects of $\mathbf{C}'$ (resp.\ of
$\mathbf{D}'$).

\begin{lemma}
Let $\mathbf{C}'$ and $\mathbf{D}'$ be localizing subcategories of
$\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{D}$ such that
$F\mathbf{C}' = G\mathbf{D}'$. Under the hypotheses above,
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}'}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}'}$ is a localizing
subcategory of $\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ and every
localizing subcategory is of this type. If $\mathbf{C}'$ and
$\mathbf{D}'$ are stable under injective envelopes, the same holds for
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}'}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}'}$.
\end{lemma}

It is clear that the category
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}'}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}'}$ is ``contained'' in
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$. We shall content
ourselves with showing that every localizing subcategory $\mathbf{A}$ of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$ is of the form
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}'}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}'}$: for this, let
$K = (I, J, \tau)$ be an injective containing no non-zero subobject of
$\mathbf{A}$; let $E = (C, D, \sigma)$ be an arbitrary object of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$. We shall first show that
the canonical map from $\operatorname{Hom}(E, K)$ to
$\operatorname{Hom}(C, I)$ is surjective: let indeed $J'$ be the largest
subobject of $J$ annihilated by $G$; since $\operatorname{Ker} G$ is
stable under injective envelopes, $J$ is the direct sum of $J'$ and of
an injective object $J''$ such that $GJ'' = GJ$; it follows that, for
every $f : C \to I$, $\tau \circ Ff \circ \sigma^{-1}$ lifts to a
morphism from $D$ to $J''$, hence also to a morphism $g$ from $D$ to
$J$; in other words, $f$ is the image of the morphism
$(f, g) : E \to K$.

Let thus $\mathbf{C}'$ and $\mathbf{D}'$ be the localizing
subcategories of $\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{D}$ consisting of the objects
$C$ and $D$ such that
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(C, I) = 0$ and
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(D, J) = 0$ as $(I, J, \tau)$ ranges over
the injective objects containing no non-zero subobject of
$\mathbf{A}$. What precedes implies that $\operatorname{Hom}(E, K)$ is
zero if and only if $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}}(C, I)$ and
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}}(D, J)$ are zero.

In other words, $(C, D, \sigma)$ belongs to $\mathbf{A}$ if and only if
$C$ and $D$ belong to $\mathbf{C}'$ and $\mathbf{D}'$; i.e.\ if and only
if $(C, D, \sigma)$ belongs to
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}'}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}'}$. This completes the
proof of the lemma.

Let $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ again be a noetherian prescheme. If $R$ is the
union of a family of closed subsets of $X$, we denote by
$\operatorname{Cat} R$ the full subcategory of $\mathbf{F}_X$ consisting
of the $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules whose support is contained in $R$.

\begin{proposition}
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] Every localizing subcategory of $\mathbf{F}_X$ is
      stable under injective envelopes.
\item[\normalfont b.] The map $R \leadsto \operatorname{Cat} R$ is a
      bijection from the set of subsets of $X$ which are unions of
      closed subsets onto the set of localizing subcategories of
      $\mathbf{F}_X$.
\end{description}
\end{proposition}

When $X$ is the affine scheme associated with a noetherian ring, the
proposition follows from Proposition~10 and Corollary~1 to
Proposition~7 of Chapter~V. By what was said at the end of Section~1,
it thus suffices to prove that the proposition holds for $X$ if it
holds for two open subsets $U$ and $V$ of $X$ such that
$U \cup V = X$. This last point follows from Lemma~3.

Return one last time to the diagram $(\star)$, and suppose the
hypotheses of Lemma~2 satisfied. If $\varphi$ is an element of the
centre $Z[\mathbf{C}]$ of $\mathbf{C}$, it is clear that there exists
one and only one element $F\varphi$ of $Z[\mathbf{B}]$ such that
$(F\varphi)(FC) = F(\varphi(C))$ for every object $C$ of $\mathbf{C}$.
The map $\varphi \leadsto F\varphi$ is a ring homomorphism from
$Z[\mathbf{C}]$ to $Z[\mathbf{B}]$. One defines analogously a
homomorphism $\psi \leadsto G\psi$ from $Z[\mathbf{D}]$ to
$Z[\mathbf{B}]$.

If $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are elements of $Z[\mathbf{C}]$ and
$Z[\mathbf{D}]$ such that $F\varphi = G\psi$, the morphisms
$(\varphi(C), \psi(D)) : (C, D, \sigma) \to (C, D, \sigma)$ evidently
define an element $\glueelt{\varphi}{\psi}$ of the centre of
$\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}$. Moreover it is clear
that the map $(\varphi, \psi) \leadsto \glueelt{\varphi}{\psi}$ is a
bijection from the fibred product
$\gluecat{Z[\mathbf{C}]}{Z[\mathbf{B}]}{Z[\mathbf{D}]}$ onto
$Z[\gluecat{\mathbf{C}}{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{D}}]$.

What precedes, and the fact that the centre of a ring $A$ is identified
with the centre of the category $\operatorname{mod} A$, imply ``by
gluing'' the following result: let $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ be a noetherian
prescheme, $z$ a section of $\mathcal{O}_X$ over $X$, $M$ an
$\mathcal{O}_X$-module and $z_M$ the endomorphism of $M$ defined by $z$;
the maps $z \leadsto z_M$ then define an isomorphism from
$\Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ onto the centre of the category
$\mathbf{F}_X$.
