\section{A Few Examples of Localizing Subcategories}

--- \emph{a. Sheaves of modules.} --- Let $E$ be a topological space, $A$
a sheaf of rings on $E$ and $\mathbf{A}$ the category of sheaves of
$A$-modules (or $A$-modules). If $F$ is a closed subset of $E$, $U$ the
open complement of $F$ in $E$, we shall use the following notations:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $M$ is an $A$-module, $M|U$ denotes its restriction to $U$;
\item $T_1$ denotes the functor $M\leadsto M|U$;
\item $\mathbf{B}$ denotes the category of $A|U$-modules;
\item $S_1$ denotes the direct image functor: if $N$ is an $A|U$-module,
$S_1N$ is the direct image of $N$ in $E$.
\end{itemize}

It is clear that $T_1$ is an exact functor and that
$\operatorname{Ker}T_1$ consists of the $A$-modules whose restriction to
$U$ is zero. Since $S_1$ is a functor adjoint to $T_1$ and
$T_1\circ S_1$ is isomorphic to $1_{\mathbf{B}}$, Proposition~5 shows that
the categories $\mathbf{B}$ and $\mathbf{A}/\operatorname{Ker}T_1$ are
equivalent.

--- \emph{b. Left exact functors.} --- The hypotheses and the notations
are those of Proposition~4 (Chapter~II). Let $\mathbf{A}$ be the category
$\operatorname{Fonct}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$, $\mathbf{B}$ the category
$\operatorname{Sex}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$, $T_1$ the functor $R^{0}$,
$S_1$ the canonical functor from
$\operatorname{Sex}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$ into
$\operatorname{Fonct}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$. Proposition~4 (Chapter~II)
says that $S_1$ is adjoint to $T_1$. Every injective object of
$\operatorname{Sex}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$ is therefore injective in
$\operatorname{Fonct}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$.

--- \emph{c. Satellite functors~[6].} --- Let $\mathbf{C}$ and
$\mathbf{D}$ be two abelian categories. We suppose, to simplify, that the
category $\mathbf{C}$ ``has enough projective objects''. Let $\mathbf{A}$
then be the following category:
\begin{itemize}
\item an object of $\mathbf{A}$ is a ``connected sequence of functors''
$(F_n)$ from $\mathbf{C}$ into $\mathbf{D}$, $n\geq 0$;
\item if $(F_n)$ and $(G_n)$ are two objects of $\mathbf{A}$, a morphism
from the first into the second is a sequence of natural transformations
$f_n\colon F_n\to G_n$; it is moreover supposed that the morphisms $f_n$
commute with the boundary operators (``connecting homomorphisms'');
\item the composition of morphisms is defined in the obvious way.
\end{itemize}

The objects $(F_n)$ whose first component $F_0$ is zero form a thick
subcategory $\mathbf{B}$ of the abelian category $\mathbf{A}$. Moreover,
the reader will easily verify that the functor
$(F_n)\leadsto F_0$ defines an equivalence between $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{B}$
and $\operatorname{Fonct}(\mathbf{C},\mathbf{D})$.

Conversely, let $F$ be a functor from $\mathbf{C}$ into $\mathbf{D}$ and
denote by $S_nF$ the $n$th satellite functor of $F$: the functor
$F\leadsto (S_nF)$ is then adjoint to the functor $(F_n)\leadsto F_0$.

The reader will find an analogous interpretation of the right satellite
functors $S^nF$ once he has stated the propositions dual to the
propositions of this chapter.

--- \emph{d. Localisation at the centre.} --- If $\mathbf{A}$ is an
additive category, the \emph{centre} of $\mathbf{A}$ is the set of natural
transformations from the identity functor $I_{\mathbf{A}}$ of
$\mathbf{A}$ into itself. Addition and composition of natural
transformations define a ring structure on this set. This ring will be
denoted $Z(\mathbf{A})$; the reader will verify that $Z(\mathbf{A})$ is a
commutative ring.

We now suppose that $\mathbf{A}$ is a $\Univ$-category with generators and
exact inductive limits. If $\Sigma$ is a multiplicative subset of
$Z(\mathbf{A})$, we denote by $\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$ the full subcategory
of $\mathbf{A}$ defined as follows: an object $M$ belongs to
$\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$ if $M$ is the supremum of the subobjects $N$ such
that $s(N)$ is zero for at least one element $s$ of $\Sigma$. It is clear
that $\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$ is a thick and closed subcategory of
$\mathbf{A}$. For every object $M$ of $\mathbf{A}$ we may therefore choose
an $\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$-envelope $j_M\colon M\to M_{\Sigma}$.

\begin{proposition}
Under the hypotheses above, the following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] $M$ is an $\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$-closed object.
\item[\normalfont b.] For every element $s$ of $\Sigma$, $s(M)$ is an
automorphism of $M$.
\end{description}
\end{proposition}

$(a)\Rightarrow(b)$: it is indeed clear that $s(M)$ is a monomorphism of
$M$ into $M$. It follows that $\operatorname{Im}s(M)$ is
$\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$-closed. By Lemma~1~$(b)$, \S2,
$\operatorname{Im}s(M)$ is therefore a direct factor of $M$, and every
complement of $\operatorname{Im}s(M)$ belongs to $\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$.
This is only possible if $\operatorname{Im}s(M) = M$.

$(b)\Rightarrow(a)$: it is indeed clear that $M$ contains no nonzero
subobject belonging to $\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$. We suppose, to simplify,
that $M$ is a subobject of $M_{\Sigma}$ and that $j_M$ is the canonical
morphism from $M$ into $M_{\Sigma}$. Let then $N$ be a subobject of
$M_{\Sigma}$, containing $M$ and such that $\operatorname{Im}s(N)$ is
contained in $M$ for at least one element $s$ of $\Sigma$. If $i$ denotes
the canonical monomorphism from $M$ into $N$, we have the equality
$1_M = s(M)^{-1}\circ s(N)\circ i$. This equality shows that $M$ is a
direct factor of $N$; it follows that $M$ is equal to $N$, hence to
$M_{\Sigma}$.

\begin{unnumberedcorollary}
The localisation functor $M\leadsto M_{\Sigma}$ is exact.
\end{unnumberedcorollary}

This follows from Proposition~11 and Proposition~7~$(b)$.

\begin{proposition}
The hypotheses and the notations are those of Proposition~11. It is
moreover supposed that $\mathbf{A}$ is a locally noetherian category. The
injective envelope of an object of $\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$ belongs to
$\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$.
\end{proposition}

Suppose indeed that $N$ is an essential extension of a subobject $M$
belonging to $\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$. We want to show that $N$ belongs to
$\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$; for this it suffices to prove that every noetherian
subobject $N'$ of $N$ belongs to $\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$; since $N'$ is an
essential extension of $M\cap N'$, we may thus reduce to the case where
$N$ is noetherian.

In this case, there exists an element $s$ of $\Sigma$ such that $s(M)$ is
zero. Since $N$ is noetherian, $\operatorname{Ker}(s(N))^n$ is equal to
$\operatorname{Ker}(s(N))^{n+1}$ for $n$ large enough. From this we deduce
the equality
$\operatorname{Ker}(s(N))^n\cap\operatorname{Im}(s(N))^n = 0$. Since
$\operatorname{Ker}(s(N))^n$ contains $M$ and $N$ is an essential extension
of $M$, $\operatorname{Im}(s(N))^n$ is zero. In other words $s^n(N)$ is
zero and $N$ belongs to $\mathbf{A}_{\Sigma}$.
