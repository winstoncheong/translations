\section{Equivalences of Categories}
--- Let $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ be two categories. An
\emph{equivalence} between $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ consists of the
data of two functors, $T\colon \mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}$ and
$S\colon \mathbf{B}\to\mathbf{A}$, and of two isomorphisms of functors,
$\Psi\colon I_{\mathbf{A}}\to S\circ T$ and
$\Phi'\colon I_{\mathbf{B}}\to T\circ S$. We suppose in addition that the
natural transformations $T\Psi\colon T\to TST$ and
$\Phi'T\colon T\to TST$ coincide.

It follows from the conditions imposed on $T$, $S$, $\Psi$ and $\Phi'$
that the natural transformations $S\Phi'\colon S\to STS$ and
$\Psi S\colon S\to STS$ coincide as well. If $\Psi'$ and $\Phi$ are the
inverse functorial isomorphisms of $\Psi$ and $\Phi'$, we see that $\Psi$
and $\Phi$ make $S$ a functor adjoint to $T$. In particular, the datum of
$T$ determines $S$ up to an isomorphism of functors. Likewise, the natural
transformations $\Psi'$ and $\Phi'$ make $T$ a functor adjoint to $S$.

Let $\psi$, $\psi'$, $\varphi$ and $\varphi'$ be the natural
transformations associated respectively to $\Psi$, $\Psi'$, $\Phi$ and
$\Phi'$, as this was explained in the preceding paragraph. The datum of
$\Psi$ determines $\psi$ and therefore $\varphi$, which is the inverse
functorial morphism of $\psi$. We deduce from this that if $S$ and $T$
are given, the datum of $\Psi$ determines $\Phi'$; and conversely\ldots

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:equivalence}
Let $(T,S,\Psi,\Phi')$ be an equivalence between $\mathbf{A}$ and
$\mathbf{B}$:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] For every pair $(M,N)$ of objects of $\mathbf{A}$,
      the map $T(M,N)$ is a bijection from
      $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(M,N)$ onto
      $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}}(TM,TN)$.
\item[\normalfont b.] Every object $P$ of $\mathbf{B}$ is isomorphic to
      an object of the form $TM$.
\end{description}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:conditions-for-equivalence}
If $T$ is a functor of $\mathbf{A}$ into $\mathbf{B}$ the following
assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] There exists a functor $S\colon \mathbf{B}\to
      \mathbf{A}$ and isomorphisms $\Psi\colon I_{\mathbf{A}}\to S\circ T$
      and $\Phi'\colon I_{\mathbf{B}}\to T\circ S$ such that
      $(T,S,\Psi,\Phi')$ is an equivalence between $\mathbf{A}$ and
      $\mathbf{B}$.
\item[\normalfont b.] The functor $T$ satisfies conditions (a) and (b) of
      Lemma~\ref{lem:equivalence}.
\item[\normalfont c.] There exists a functor $S\colon \mathbf{B}\to
      \mathbf{A}$ such that $S\circ T$ is isomorphic to the functor
      $I_{\mathbf{A}}$ and that $T\circ S$ is isomorphic to
      $I_{\mathbf{B}}$.
\end{description}
\end{proposition}

We shall henceforth say that two categories $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$
are \emph{equivalent} if there exists a functor $T$ satisfying the
assertions of the preceding proposition. In that case we shall also say
that $T$ defines an equivalence between $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$. We
note finally that assertion (c) fits conveniently into the following
formalism:

If $\mathfrak{B}$ is a universe of which the universe $\Univ$ is an
element, we can construct a new category $\mathbf{E}$: the objects of
$\mathbf{E}$ are the categories whose set of morphisms belongs to
$\mathfrak{B}$ (we identify the objects with the identity morphisms); if
$\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are two objects of $\mathbf{E}$,
$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})$ is the set of isomorphism
classes of functors of $\mathbf{A}$ into $\mathbf{B}$, composition being
made in the obvious way. One will observe that $\mathbf{E}$ is not a
$\Univ$-category. Assertion (c) says that the class of functors isomorphic
to $T$ is an isomorphism in the category $\mathbf{E}$.

To finish, we propose to the reader some easy exercises, whose results we
shall use freely: let $(T,S,\Psi,\Phi')$ be an equivalence between
$\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$. If $f$ is a monomorphism (resp.\ an
epimorphism), $Tf$ is a monomorphism (resp.\ an epimorphism). If $M$ is a
generator of $\mathbf{A}$ (resp.\ a projective object of $\mathbf{A}$),
$TM$ is a generator of $\mathbf{B}$ (resp.\ a projective object of
$\mathbf{B}$). If $P$ is the fibre product of diagram (1), $TP$ is the
fibre product of diagram (2); one also says that $T$ commutes with
fibre products. The functor $T$ ``commutes'' also with fibre sums,
inductive limits\ldots More generally, the ``homological'' properties of
two equivalent categories are the ``same''.

\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large, row sep=large]
    & M \arrow[rd, "f"] &     &     & TM \arrow[rd, "Tf"] & \\
(1) &                 & L & (2) &                  & TL \\
    & N \arrow[ru, "g"] &     &     & TN \arrow[ru, "Tg"] &
\end{tikzcd}
\]

The preceding exercises and Proposition~3 have the following
consequence:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:equivalence-additive}
Let $T$ be a functor defining an equivalence between two additive
categories $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$. Then $T$ is an additive
functor. If $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are two abelian categories,
$T$ is moreover an exact functor.
\end{proposition}

The last assertion results from
Proposition~\ref{prop:adjoints-exactness}.
