\chapter{Locally Noetherian Categories}
\markboth{Chapter IV. Locally Noetherian Categories}{}

Unless expressly stated to the contrary, all the categories considered
in this chapter are abelian $\Univ$-categories with generators and
exact inductive limits.

This chapter is devoted to the study of the injective objects of a
locally noetherian $\Univ$-category. We draw from this study the
following moral: a locally noetherian category is an ``extension'' of
locally finite categories; a locally finite category is comparable to
a category of modules.

\section{The Krull Dimension of an Abelian Category}

--- Let $\mathbf{A}$ be a category (abelian...). We shall associate to
every ordinal $\alpha$ a localizing subcategory $\mathbf{A}_\alpha$ of
$\mathbf{A}$. The ordinal possessing no element will be denoted $-1$
for historical reasons; likewise, the ordinal possessing a finite
number $n$ of elements will be denoted $n-1$. If $\alpha$ and $\beta$
are two ordinals, the symbol $\alpha \mathbin{T} \beta$ will denote the
ordinal obtained by taking ``first $\alpha$, then $\beta$''. If
$\alpha$ and $\beta$ are two finite ordinals, we have for example the
formula $\alpha \mathbin{T} \beta = \alpha + \beta + 1$. We denote by
$T_\alpha$ the canonical functor from $\mathbf{A}$ into
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$.

The construction of the subcategories $\mathbf{A}_\alpha$ is carried
out by transfinite recursion:
\begin{itemize}
\item[---] $\mathbf{A}_{-1}$ is the localizing subcategory of
      $\mathbf{A}$ whose only objects are the zero objects of
      $\mathbf{A}$. The functor $T_{-1}$ is therefore the identity
      functor of $\mathbf{A}$.
\item[---] If the ordinal $\alpha$ has a predecessor $\beta$,
      $\mathbf{A}_\alpha$ is the smallest localizing subcategory
      containing all the objects $M$ such that $T_\beta M$ is of finite
      length.
\item[---] If $\alpha$ is a limit ordinal, $\mathbf{A}_\alpha$ is the
      smallest localizing subcategory containing all the
      subcategories $\mathbf{A}_\beta$ for $\beta < \alpha$.
\end{itemize}

We denote by $\mathbf{A}_\omega$ the smallest localizing subcategory
containing all the subcategories $\mathbf{A}_\alpha$; the quotient
category $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\omega$ then possesses no simple
object. When $M$ is an object of $\mathbf{A}_\omega$, the \emph{Krull
dimension} of $M$ is the smallest ordinal $\alpha$ such that $M$
belongs to $\mathbf{A}_\alpha$ (notation:
$\operatorname{Kdim} M = \alpha$). If $\mathbf{A}_\omega$ coincides
with $\mathbf{A}$, the smallest ordinal $\alpha$ such that
$\mathbf{A}_\alpha$ is equal to $\mathbf{A}$ will be called the
\emph{Krull dimension} of $\mathbf{A}$ (notation
$\operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A} = \alpha$). The following proposition
results from the definitions:

\begin{proposition}
Let $\mathbf{C}$ be a localizing subcategory of $\mathbf{A}$. Then
$\mathbf{A}_\omega$ coincides with $\mathbf{A}$ if and only if
$\mathbf{C}_\omega$ and $(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C})_\omega$ coincide with
$\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$ respectively. In this case we
have the inequalities
\[
\sup(\operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{C},
\operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}) \le
\operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A} \le
(\operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{C}) \mathbin{T}
(\operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}).
\]
\end{proposition}

We suppose throughout the rest of this paragraph that the Krull
dimension of $\mathbf{A}$ is defined, i.e.\ that
$\mathbf{A}_\omega$ coincides with $\mathbf{A}$. For every ordinal
$\alpha < \operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}$, the category
$\mathbf{A}_{\alpha \mathbin{T} 0}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$ is then of
dimension zero.

If $M$ is an object of $\mathbf{A}$, the \emph{type} (\emph{type}) of
this object will be the set of objects of $\mathbf{A}$ isomorphic to
$M$. We denote by $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A})$
(\emph{spectre de $\mathbf{A}$}) the set of types of indecomposable
injective objects of $\mathbf{A}$. For every ordinal
$\alpha < \operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}$, we denote by
$\operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(\mathbf{A})$ the set of types consisting of
$\mathbf{A}_\alpha$-closed indecomposable injective objects containing
a nonzero subobject of
$\mathbf{A}_{\alpha \mathbin{T} 0}$. It is clear that the sets
$\operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(\mathbf{A})$ are pairwise disjoint and that
their union is equal to $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A})$.

If $I$ is an indecomposable injective object whose type belongs to
$\operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(\mathbf{A})$, $T_\alpha I$ contains a
subobject of finite length of $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$. It
follows that the socle of $T_\alpha I$ is nonzero and is a simple
object of
$\mathbf{A}_{\alpha \mathbin{T} 0}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$. We denote by
$s(I)$ the smallest nonzero $\mathbf{A}_\alpha$-closed subobject of
$I$. The canonical morphism from $s(I)$ into $I$ then induces an
isomorphism from $T_\alpha s(I)$ onto the socle of $T_\alpha I$. By
abuse of language, we shall say that $s(I)$ is the socle of $I$. We
shall treat $s(I)$ indifferently as an object of $\mathbf{A}$ or as an
object of $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$.

\begin{proposition}
Let $\mathbf{A}$ be a category whose Krull dimension is defined. For
every ordinal $\alpha < \operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}$, the map
$I \mapsto s(I)$ induces a bijection from
$\operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(\mathbf{A})$ onto the set of types of simple
objects of $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

Let indeed $I$ and $J$ be two indecomposable injectives whose types
belong to $\operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(\mathbf{A})$. The corollary to
Proposition~11 (Chapter~II) implies that $I$ and $J$ are isomorphic if
and only if the same holds for $s(I)$ and $s(J)$. Let on the other
hand $M$ be a simple object of
$\mathbf{A}_{\alpha \mathbin{T} 0}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$ and let
$u\colon M \to EM$ be an injective envelope of $M$ in
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$. If $S_\alpha$ is a functor adjoint to
$T_\alpha$, $S_\alpha EM$ is an indecomposable injective whose socle
is isomorphic to $M$ (as an object of
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$). This completes the proof.

If we compare Proposition~2 with Proposition~11 (Chapter~II), we see
how the notions of spectrum, simple object and coirreducible object are
related.

Let now $\mathbf{C}$ be a localizing subcategory of $\mathbf{A}$, let
$T$ be the canonical functor from $\mathbf{A}$ into
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$ and let $S$ be a functor adjoint to $T$. If
$I$ is an indecomposable injective of $\mathbf{C}$ and if $EI$ is an
injective envelope of $I$ in $\mathbf{A}$, it is immediate that $EI$
is still indecomposable. The map $I \mapsto EI$ therefore induces an
injection from $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C})$ into
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A})$; we shall always identify
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C})$ with the image of this injection.
Likewise, if $J$ is an indecomposable injective of
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C}$, $SJ$ is an indecomposable injective of
$\mathbf{A}$. The map $J \mapsto SJ$ induces an injection from
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C})$ into
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A})$; we always identify
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C})$ with the image of this
injection. Corollary~2 to Proposition~6 (Chapter~III) then shows that
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C})$ is the complement of
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C})$ in $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A})$.
Moreover Proposition~2 gives the

\begin{corollary}
Let $\mathbf{C}$ be a localizing subcategory of a category
$\mathbf{A}$ whose Krull dimension is defined. An object $M$ of
$\mathbf{A}$ belongs to $\mathbf{C}$ if and only if
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(M, I)$ is zero for every
indecomposable injective of $\mathbf{A}$ whose type belongs to
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C})$.
\end{corollary}

Let indeed $\mathbf{D}$ be the localizing subcategory of
$\mathbf{A}$ whose objects $M$ are such that
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(M, I)$ is zero for every
indecomposable injective whose type belongs to
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C})$ (corollary to
Proposition~8, Chapter~III). It is clear that $\mathbf{D}$ contains
$\mathbf{C}$. If $\mathbf{D}$ does not coincide with $\mathbf{C}$,
there exists an object $M$ of $\mathbf{D}$ which is nonzero and which
contains no nonzero subobject of $\mathbf{C}$. Let $\beta$ therefore
be the smallest ordinal such that $M$ contains a nonzero subobject of
$\mathbf{A}_\beta$. It is clear that $\beta$ has a predecessor
$\alpha$. There therefore exists a subobject $N$ of $M$ such that
$T_\alpha N$ is simple. If $I$ is the injective envelope of $N$, the
type of $I$ belongs to $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{C})$
and $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{A}}(M, I)$ is not zero: a
contradiction.

\begin{corollary}
Let $\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{D}$ be two localizing subcategories of
a category $\mathbf{A}$ whose Krull dimension is defined. Then
$\mathbf{C}$ coincides with $\mathbf{D}$ if and only if
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{C})$ coincides with
$\operatorname{Sp}(\mathbf{D})$.
\end{corollary}

This follows from Corollary~1.

If $I$ is an indecomposable injective of $\mathbf{A}$, we remark that
the ring of endomorphisms of $s(I)$ (in $\mathbf{A}$ or in
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$) is a field. If $\mathbf{A}$ is the
local ring of endomorphisms of $I$, this field is none other than the
quotient of $\mathbf{A}$ by its Jacobson radical: let indeed $f$ be an
endomorphism of $I$ (in $\mathbf{A}$ or in
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$); the image of $s(I)$ under $f$ is zero
or is a simple object (in $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$); the
morphism $f$ therefore induces an endomorphism $f'$ of $s(I)$; as $I$
is injective, every endomorphism of $s(I)$ extends conversely to an
endomorphism of $I$. This shows that the map $f \mapsto f'$ is
surjective. Moreover $f'$ is nonzero if and only if $f$ is an
automorphism. We have thus proved the

\begin{proposition}
Let $\mathbf{A}$ be a category whose Krull dimension is defined, and
$I$ an indecomposable injective object of $\mathbf{A}$. The field of
endomorphisms of the socle $s(I)$ is the quotient of the ring of
endomorphisms of $I$ by its Jacobson radical.
\end{proposition}

The following proposition will give us information on the injective
envelopes of the category $\mathbf{A}$:

\begin{proposition}
Let $M$ be an object of a category $\mathbf{A}$ whose Krull dimension
is defined. There exists a family $(f_\alpha)$ of morphisms of
$\mathbf{A}$, indexed by the ordinals
$\alpha < \operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}$ and satisfying conditions
(a), (b), (c) and (d):
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] $f_\alpha$ is a morphism from an object
      $N_\alpha$ into $M$.
\item[\normalfont b.] Every subobject of $N_\alpha$ belonging to
      $\mathbf{A}_\alpha$ is zero.
\item[\normalfont c.] $T_\alpha N_\alpha$ is a semi-simple object of
      $\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$.
\item[\normalfont d.] The morphism $f$ from $\sum_\alpha N_\alpha$
      into $M$ defined by the family $(f_\alpha)$ is an essential
      extension of $\sum_\alpha N_\alpha$.
\end{description}
\end{proposition}

For every family $(f_\alpha)$ satisfying (a), (b), (c) and (d),
$T_\alpha f_\alpha$ is an isomorphism from $T_\alpha N_\alpha$ onto
the socle of $T_\alpha M$.

Denote indeed by $M_\alpha$ the largest subobject of $M$ belonging to
$\mathbf{A}_\alpha$
($\alpha < \operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}$). Let $Q_\alpha$ be a
complement of $M_\alpha$ in $M_{\alpha \mathbin{T} 0}$
(Chapter~II, \S5); let $i_\alpha$ be the canonical monomorphism from
$Q_\alpha$ into $M$.

\begin{lemma}
The morphism $j$ from $\sum_\alpha Q_\alpha$ into $M$ defined by the
family $(i_\alpha)$ is an essential extension of
$\sum_\alpha Q_\alpha$.
\end{lemma}

This is clear if $\operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}$ is equal to $-1$; we
therefore suppose the property proved for all categories
$\mathbf{B}$ such that
$\operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{B} < \operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}$; we
prove that the property is then true for $\mathbf{A}$. Two cases are
indeed possible:

--- The ordinal $\operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}$ has a predecessor
$\beta$: it is known that the morphism $h$ from
$M_\beta \oplus Q_\beta$ into $M$ defined by the canonical morphisms
from $M_\beta$ and $Q_\beta$ into $M$ is then an essential extension.
Moreover, $j$ is the composite of a morphism $i$ from
$\sum_\alpha Q_\alpha$ into $M_\beta \oplus Q_\beta$ and of $h$; as $i$
is an essential extension by the recurrence hypothesis and Lemma~2
(\S5, Chapter~II), it follows that $j$ is an essential extension
(Lemma~1, \S5, Chapter~II).

--- $\operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}$ is a limit ordinal: $M$ is then
the upper bound of the $M_\beta$, for
$\beta < \operatorname{Kdim} \mathbf{A}$. Let $j_\beta$ be the
morphism from $\sum_{\gamma < \beta} Q_\gamma$ into $M_\beta$ defined
by the morphisms $i_\gamma$. As $j_\beta$ is an essential extension by
the recurrence hypothesis, the assertion follows from Proposition~13
(Chapter~II).

The lemma being proved, the construction of a family $(f_\alpha)$ is
simple: let indeed $N_\alpha$ be a subobject of $Q_\alpha$ and let
$g_\alpha$ be the canonical morphism from $N_\alpha$ into $Q_\alpha$;
we shall suppose that $T_\alpha g_\alpha$ is an isomorphism from
$T_\alpha N_\alpha$ onto the socle of $T_\alpha Q_\alpha$. If $P$ is
a nonzero subobject of $Q_\alpha$, $T_\alpha P$ contains a simple
subobject and $T_\alpha P \cap T_\alpha N_\alpha$ is not zero. It
follows that $P \cap N_\alpha$ is not zero. This proves that $g_\alpha$
is an essential extension. If we set
$f_\alpha = i_\alpha \circ g_\alpha$, conditions (a), (b), (c) and (d)
are satisfied.

Conversely, let $(f_\alpha)$ be a family of morphisms satisfying
conditions (a), (b), (c) and (d). It is clear that
$\operatorname{Im} T_\alpha f_\alpha$ is the largest subobject of
$\operatorname{Im} T_\alpha f$ belonging to
$\mathbf{A}_{\alpha \mathbin{T} 0}$. If $S$ is a simple subobject of
$T_\alpha M$, $S \cap \operatorname{Im} T_\alpha f$ is a nonzero
subobject of $T_\alpha M$ belonging to
$\mathbf{A}_{\alpha \mathbin{T} 0}$. It follows that
$S \cap \operatorname{Im} T_\alpha f$ is contained in
$\operatorname{Im} T_\alpha f_\alpha$; every simple subobject of
$T_\alpha M$ thus ``meets'' $\operatorname{Im} T_\alpha f_\alpha$,
which proves the last assertion.

\begin{theorem}
Let $I$ be an injective object of a category $\mathbf{A}$ whose Krull
dimension is defined. There exists a family
$(I_l)_{l\in\mathbf{L}}$ of indecomposable injectives of
$\mathbf{A}$, such that $I$ is isomorphic to the injective envelope of
the direct sum $\sum_{l\in\mathbf{L}} I_l$.

If $(J_n)_{n\in\mathbf{N}}$ is a second family of indecomposable
injectives, and if $\sum_l I_l$ and $\sum_n J_n$ have the same
injective envelope, there exists a bijection $h$ from $\mathbf{L}$
onto $\mathbf{N}$ such that $I_l$ is isomorphic to $J_{h(l)}$.
\end{theorem}

Let us first prove the existence of a family
$(I_l)_{l\in\mathbf{L}}$: we use the preceding proposition, choosing
$M$ equal to $I$. If $EN_\alpha$ is ``the'' injective envelope of
$N_\alpha$, it is clear that $I$ is isomorphic to the injective
envelope of the direct sum $\sum_\alpha EN_\alpha$. It therefore
suffices to prove that $EN_\alpha$ is the injective envelope of a
direct sum of indecomposable injectives. Now $N_\alpha$ contains no
nonzero subobject belonging to $\mathbf{A}_\alpha$; moreover,
$T_\alpha N_\alpha$ is a semi-simple object of
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$. We deduce the existence of a family
$(N_\alpha^\sigma)_{\sigma\in\mathbf{S}}$ of subobjects of $N_\alpha$
satisfying the following conditions: $T_\alpha N_\alpha^\sigma$ is a
simple object; the sum $N'_\alpha$ of the $N_\alpha^\sigma$ is
direct; the canonical monomorphism from $N'_\alpha$ into $N_\alpha$
induces an isomorphism from $T_\alpha N'_\alpha$ onto
$T_\alpha N_\alpha$. If $EN_\alpha^\sigma$ is the injective envelope
of $N_\alpha^\sigma$, it follows that $N_\alpha$ and
$\sum_\sigma EN_\alpha^\sigma$ have the same injective envelope. As
$EN_\alpha^\sigma$ is indecomposable, the first part of the theorem
is proved.

Let us prove the second assertion: denote by $\mathbf{L}_\alpha$
(resp.\ by $\mathbf{N}_\alpha$) the set of $l$ (resp.\ of $n$) such
that the socle of $I_l$ (resp.\ of $J_n$) ``belongs'' to
$\operatorname{Sp}_\alpha(\mathbf{A})$. With the notations of the
preceding proposition, it is then clear that $N_\alpha$,
$\sum_{l\in\mathbf{L}_\alpha} I_l$ and
$\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}_\alpha} J_n$ have the same injective envelope.
If we consider these objects as objects of
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$, it follows that $N_\alpha$,
$\sum_{l\in\mathbf{L}_\alpha} I_l$ and
$\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}_\alpha} J_n$ have the same socle; in other words,
the direct sums $\sum_{l\in\mathbf{L}_\alpha} s(I_l)$ and
$\sum_{n\in\mathbf{N}_\alpha} s(J_n)$ are isomorphic in
$\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{A}_\alpha$. The properties of semi-simple objects
imply the existence of a bijection $h_\alpha$ from
$\mathbf{L}_\alpha$ onto $\mathbf{N}_\alpha$ such that $s(I_l)$ is
isomorphic to $s(J_{h_\alpha(l)})$. Then $I_l$ is isomorphic to
$J_{h_\alpha(l)}$.

\begin{proposition}[exchange theorem]
Suppose the hypotheses of Theorem~1 satisfied. Let
$(I_l)_{l\in\mathbf{L}}$ be a family of indecomposable injectives and
$u\colon \sum_{l\in\mathbf{L}} I_l \to I$ an injective envelope. If $J$
is a direct factor of $I$, there exists a part $\mathbf{N}$ of
$\mathbf{L}$ satisfying the following condition: every maximal
essential extension of $u\left(\sum_{l\in\mathbf{N}} I_l\right)$ in $I$
is a complement of $J$.
\end{proposition}

Let indeed $\mathbf{N}$ be a maximal element of the set of parts
$\mathbf{M}$ of $\mathbf{L}$ satisfying the following condition: $J$
and $u\left(\sum_{l\in\mathbf{M}} I_l\right)$ have zero intersection.
We shall prove that $I$ is an essential extension of the sum
$J + u\left(\sum_{l\in\mathbf{N}} I_l\right)$: let indeed $L$ be a
maximal essential extension of this sum in $I$. As $\mathbf{N}$ is
maximal, $L$ ``meets'' every $u(I_l)$; in other words, $u(I_l)$ is an
essential extension of $L \cap u(I_l)$ for every
$l \in \mathbf{L}$. It follows that $I$ is an essential extension of
$L$. Whence the equality $L = I$. This completes the proof.
