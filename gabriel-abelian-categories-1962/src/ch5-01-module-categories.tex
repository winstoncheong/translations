\section{Module Categories}

% `propbis' reproduces the heading "Proposition 1 bis." of the original
% without advancing the proposition counter.
\newtheorem*{propbis}{Proposition 1 bis}

--- We take up here some properties of commutative rings.  We try to
generalise them to rings that are not necessarily commutative.

Unless expressly stated otherwise, all the rings considered in this
chapter admit a unit element.  All the modules considered are unitary
right modules.  We suppose moreover that the underlying set of a ring
or of a module is an element of the universe $\Univ$.

--- Let $A$ be a ring.  We call \emph{module category} (on the right)
over $A$, and we denote by $\mathbf{mod}\,A$, the following category:
\begin{itemize}
\item an object of $\mathbf{mod}\,A$ is a module over $A$;
\item if $M$ and $N$ are two modules over $A$, a morphism from $M$
      into $N$ is an $A$-linear map from $M$ into $N$;
\item composition of morphisms coincides with composition of maps.
\end{itemize}

It is clear that $\mathbf{mod}\,A$ is an abelian $\Univ$-category with
exact inductive limits.  Moreover, the right $A$-module $A_d$
underlying the ring $A$ is a projective generator of
$\mathbf{mod}\,A$.  The category $\mathbf{mod}\,A$ is locally
noetherian if and only if the ring $A$ is right noetherian.

The category of left modules over $A$ is defined analogously.
Consider now an object $U$ of an abelian category $\mathbf B$ and let
$\chi\colon A\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(U,U)$ be a unital ring
homomorphism.  As $M$ ranges over the objects of $\mathbf B$,
composition of morphisms defines a bilinear map from
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(U,M)\times A$ into
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(U,M)$:
$(f,a)\mapsto f\circ\chi(a)$.  This map makes
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(U,M)$ into an $A$-module.  One thus
defines a functor $M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(U,M)$ from
$\mathbf B$ into $\mathbf{mod}\,A$.  This functor will be denoted
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(\chi U,.)$, or
$\operatorname{Hom}(U,.)$ when no confusion is possible.

\begin{proposition}
Let $A$ be a ring, $\mathbf B$ a $(\Univ)$-category abelian and $S$ a
functor from $\mathbf B$ into $\mathbf{mod}\,A$.  The following
assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] $S$ is adjoint to a functor
      $T\colon\mathbf{mod}\,A\to\mathbf B$.
\item[\normalfont b.] There exists a homomorphism $\chi$ from $A$ into
      the endomorphism ring of an object $U$ of $\mathbf B$ satisfying
      the following conditions: $S$ is isomorphic to the functor
      $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(\chi U,.)$; moreover, every family
      of objects isomorphic to $U$ has a direct sum.
\end{description}
\end{proposition}

$(a)\Rightarrow(b)$: let indeed $\psi$ be a functorial isomorphism
from $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(T.,.)$ onto
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(.,S.)$.  Identify $A$ with the endomorphism ring
of $A_d$ and put $U=TA_d$, $\chi=T(A_d,A_d)$.  For every object $N$ of
$\mathbf B$, $\psi(A_d,N)$ is a bijective map from
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TA_d,N)$ onto
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(A_d,SN)$.  It is easy to see that this map is
$A$-linear.  Since $\operatorname{Hom}_A(A_d,SN)$ is isomorphic to
$SN$, there results a functorial isomorphism from
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(U,.)$ onto $S$.

On the other hand, let $(U_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of objects
isomorphic to $U$, indexed by a set belonging to $\Univ$.  Let $u_i$ be
an isomorphism from $U_i$ onto $U$ and put $A_i=A_d$ for every $i$.
If $v_i$ is the canonical map from $A_i$ into the direct sum
$\sum_i A_i$, the following equalities show that the morphisms
$(Tv_i)\circ u_i$ make $T(\sum_i A_i)$ into a direct sum of the family
$(U_i)_{i\in I}$:
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}\!\left(T\sum_i A_i,N\right)
  =\operatorname{Hom}_A\!\left(\sum_i A_i,SN\right)
\]
\[
=\prod_i\operatorname{Hom}_A(A_i,SN)
  =\prod_i\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TA_i,N).
\]

$(b)\Rightarrow(a)$: choose indeed for every $A$-module $M$ an exact
sequence
\[
\sum_j A_j \xrightarrow{q} \sum_i A_i \xrightarrow{p} M \to 0,
\]
where $(A_i)_{i\in I}$ and $(A_j)_{j\in J}$ are two families of modules
equal to $A_d$.  Put $U_j=U$ for every $j$ and $U_i=U$ for every $i$.
Every isomorphism from
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(\chi U,.)$ onto $S$ induces functorial
isomorphisms:
\[
\psi_0\colon
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}\!\left(\sum_{i\in I}U_i,.\right)
  \to\operatorname{Hom}_A\!\left(\sum_{i\in I}A_i,S.\right),
\]
\[
\psi_1\colon
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}\!\left(\sum_{j\in J}U_j,.\right)
  \to\operatorname{Hom}_A\!\left(\sum_{j\in J}A_j,S.\right).
\]
Moreover, there exists a morphism $q'$ from $\sum_j U_j$ into
$\sum_i U_i$ such that
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(q,.)\circ\psi_0
  =\psi_1\circ\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(q',.)$.

If we put $TM=\operatorname{Coker}q'$, $\psi_0$ induces an isomorphism
$\psi(M,.)$ from $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(TM,.)$ onto
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,S.)$.  This shows that the functor
$N\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,SN)$ is representable for every $M$;
the desired implication thus follows from the dual of
Proposition~10 (Chapter~I).

When $\mathbf B$ is the category of modules over a ring $B$, the
homomorphism $\chi$ makes $U$ into an $A$-$B$-bimodule ($A$ on the
left, $B$ on the right).  In this case, one may choose for $T$ the
functor $M\mapsto M\otimes_A U$.  The functorial isomorphism from
$\operatorname{Hom}_B(T.,.)$ onto $\operatorname{Hom}_A(.,S.)$
described in~\cite{cartan-eilenberg} will be called canonical.

\begin{propbis}
Let $A$ be a ring, $\mathbf B$ an abelian category and $T$ a functor
from $\mathbf{mod}\,A$ into $\mathbf B$.  The following assertions are
equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] There exists a functor $S$ adjoint to $T$.
\item[\normalfont b.] The functor $T$ is right exact and it commutes
      with direct sums.
\end{description}
\end{propbis}

$(a)\Rightarrow(b)$: Proposition~11 (Chapter~I) shows that $T$ is right
exact.  An argument analogous to the one used to prove the implication
$(a)\Rightarrow(b)$ of Proposition~1 shows that $T$ commutes with
direct sums.

$(b)\Rightarrow(a)$: put indeed $TA_d=U$ and $\chi=T(A_d,A_d)$.  We
shall define a functorial morphism from
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(T.,.)$ into $\operatorname{Hom}_A(.,S.)$,
where $S$ is the functor $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(\chi U,.)$;
for this, let $M$ be an $A$-module, $N$ an object of $\mathbf B$ and
$f$ a morphism from $TM$ into $N$.  If $m$ is an element of $M$, we
denote by $g_m$ the map $a\mapsto m\cdot a$ from $A$ into $M$.  One
verifies easily that the map $m\mapsto f\circ Tg_m$ is an $A$-linear
map $f'$ from $M$ into $SN$.  As $M$ and $N$ vary, the maps
$f\mapsto f'$ define a functorial morphism
$\psi\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(T.,.)
  \to\operatorname{Hom}_A(.,S.)$.

It remains to show that the maps $\psi(M,N)\colon f\mapsto f'$ are
bijective.  This is obviously true when $M$ is equal to $A_d$, $N$
being arbitrary.  Since the contravariant functors
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(T.,N)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_A(.,SN)$
turn direct sums into direct products, $\psi(M,N)$ is still bijective
when $M$ is free.  In the general case, one chooses an exact sequence
\[
L_1 \xrightarrow{q} L_0 \xrightarrow{p} M \to 0,
\]
where $L_0$ and $L_1$ are free modules.  The contravariant functors
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(T.,N)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_A(.,SN)$
are left exact and the maps $\psi(L_1,N)$ and $\psi(L_0,N)$ are
bijective.  It results by a classical argument that $\psi(M,N)$ is
bijective.

\begin{corollary}
Let $S$ be a functor from an abelian category $\mathbf B$ into
$\mathbf{mod}\,A$.  The following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] $S$ defines an equivalence between $\mathbf B$
      and $\mathbf{mod}\,A$.
\item[\normalfont b.] There exists an isomorphism $\chi$ from $A$ onto
      the endomorphism ring of an object $U$ of $\mathbf B$ satisfying
      the following conditions: $S$ is isomorphic to the functor
      $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(\chi U,.)$; the object $U$ is a
      projective generator of $\mathbf B$; every family
      $(U_i)_{i\in I}$ of objects isomorphic to $U$ has a direct sum;
      moreover, the canonical map from
      $\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(U,U_i)$ into
      $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(U,\sum_{i\in I}U_i)$ is bijective.
\end{description}
\end{corollary}

$(a)\Rightarrow(b)$: indeed, $S$ is adjoint to a functor
$T\colon\mathbf{mod}\,A\to\mathbf B$.  If we put $U=TA_d$ and
$\chi=T(A_d,A_d)$, we have seen that $S$ is isomorphic to the functor
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(\chi U,.)$.  Since $T$ defines an
equivalence, $T(A_d,A_d)$ is a bijective map.  Since $A_d$ is a
projective generator of $\mathbf{mod}\,A$, $TA_d$ is a projective
generator of $\mathbf B$.  Finally, since $S$ defines an equivalence,
$S$ commutes with direct sums.  The same holds for the functor
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(\chi U,.)$, which proves the last
assertion of~(b).

$(b)\Rightarrow(a)$: let us indeed show that the functor
$S'=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(\chi U,.)$ defines an equivalence
between $\mathbf B$ and $\mathbf{mod}\,A$: since $U$ is projective,
$S'$ is an exact functor.  Since $\chi$ is an isomorphism, $S'U$ is
identified with $A_d$.  If $(U_i)_{i\in I}$ is a family of objects
equal to $U$, the last assertion of~(b) shows that
$S'(\sum_{i\in I}U_i)$ is a free $A$-module.  If $(U_j)_{j\in J}$ is
another family of objects equal to $U$, the same condition furnishes
the following ``equalities'':
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}\!\left(\sum_i U_i,\sum_j U_j\right)
\]
\[
=\prod_i\sum_j\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf B}(U_i,U_j)
  =\prod_i\sum_j\operatorname{Hom}_A(S'U_i,S'U_j)
\]
\[
=\operatorname{Hom}_A\!\left(\sum_i S'U_i,\sum_j S'U_j\right)
  =\operatorname{Hom}_A\!\left(S'\sum_i U_i,S'\sum_j U_j\right).
\]

Denote by $\mathbf P$ (resp.\ by $\mathbf Q$) the full subcategory of
$\mathbf{mod}\,A$ (resp.\ of $\mathbf B$) consisting of the free
$A$-modules (resp.\ of the objects isomorphic to the direct sum of a
family of objects equal to $U$).  What precedes proves that $S'$
induces an equivalence between $\mathbf Q$ and $\mathbf P$
[Proposition~12~(b), Chapter~I].  The implication thus follows from the
dual of Proposition~14, Corollary~3 (Chapter~I).

One will observe that the last two conditions of assertion~(b) may be
replaced by the following stronger conditions: $\mathbf B$ is a
category with generators and exact inductive limits, and $U$ is an
object of finite type of $\mathbf B$ (i.e.\ every filtered increasing
family of subobjects of $U$ whose supremum is equal to $U$ is
stationary).

\begin{corollary}
Let $A$ and $B$ be two rings and $S$ a functor from $\mathbf{mod}\,B$
into $\mathbf{mod}\,A$.  The following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] $S$ defines an equivalence between
      $\mathbf{mod}\,B$ and $\mathbf{mod}\,A$.
\item[\normalfont b.] There exists an isomorphism $\chi$ from $A$ onto
      the endomorphism ring of a $B$-module $U$ satisfying the
      following conditions: $S$ is isomorphic to the functor
      $\operatorname{Hom}_B(\chi U,.)$; $U$ is a projective $B$-module
      of finite type; moreover, $U$ is a projective generator of
      $\mathbf{mod}\,B$.
\end{description}
\end{corollary}

It obviously amounts to the same to give a homomorphism $\chi$ from
$A$ into the endomorphism ring of a $B$-module $U$ or to give $U$ an
$A$-$B$-module structure ($A$ on the left, $B$ on the right).  In
Corollary~3, $U$ is such a bimodule; we denote by ${}_AU$ (resp.\ by
$U_B$) the left $A$-module (resp.\ the right $B$-module) underlying
$U$.  If $a$ is an element of $A$ (resp.\ $b$ an element of $B$),
$a_U$ (resp.\ $b_U$) denotes the endomorphism $x\mapsto a\cdot x$
(resp.\ $x\mapsto x\cdot b$) of the abelian group underlying $U$.

\begin{corollary}
(Morita~\cite{morita}). --- Let $A$ and $B$ be two rings and
$U$ an $A$-$B$-module ($A$ on the left, $B$ on the right).  The
following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] The functor $M\mapsto M\otimes_A U$ defines an
      equivalence between $\mathbf{mod}\,A$ and $\mathbf{mod}\,B$.
\item[\normalfont b.] The functor $N\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_B(U,N)$
      defines an equivalence between $\mathbf{mod}\,B$ and
      $\mathbf{mod}\,A$.
\item[\normalfont c.] $U_B$ (resp.\ ${}_AU$) is a $B$-module (resp.\ a
      left $A$-module), projective and of finite type; the map
      $a\mapsto a_U$ (resp.\ $b\mapsto b_U$) is an isomorphism from $A$
      (resp.\ from the ring opposite to $B$) onto the endomorphism ring
      of $U_B$ (resp.\ of ${}_AU$).
\end{description}
\end{corollary}

The equivalence of~(a) and of~(b) results from the fact that the
functor $S\colon N\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_B(U,N)$ is adjoint to the
functor $T\colon M\mapsto M\otimes_A U$.

$(a)\Rightarrow(c)$: one has indeed $TA_d=U$ and the map
$a\mapsto a_U$ coincides with $T(A_d,A_d)$.  It results from the proof
of Corollary~1 that $U_B$ is a $B$-module of finite type and that
$a\mapsto a_U$ is a bijection from $A$ onto the endomorphism ring of
$U_B$.

By Corollary~2, there is on the other hand a $B$-$A$-bimodule $V$ such
that $T$ is isomorphic to the functor $M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_A(V,M)$.
Moreover, $V_A$ is projective, of finite type, and the map
$b\mapsto b_V$ is an isomorphism from $B$ onto the endomorphism ring
of $V_A$.

The ring $B$ operates on the right on the abelian group
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(V,A_d)$; the endomorphism ring of $A_d$ operates
on the left on $\operatorname{Hom}_A(V,A_d)$; this group is thus
equipped with an $A$-$B$-bimodule structure.  Since $U$ is identified
with $TA_d$, the $A$-$B$-bimodules $U$ and
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(V,A_d)$ are isomorphic.  The properties of $V_A$
and the following lemma therefore imply that ${}_AU$ is projective,
of finite type, and that the map $b\mapsto b_U$ is an isomorphism from
the ring $B^0$, opposite to $B$, onto the endomorphism ring of
${}_AU$.

\begin{lemma}
If $M$ is a projective right $A$-module of finite type, the left
$A$-module $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,A_d)$ is projective and of finite
type.  The functor $M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,A_d)$ defines a
duality between the projective right $A$-modules of finite type and
the projective left $A$-modules of finite type.
\end{lemma}

The left $A$-module structure on
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,A_d)$ is obviously defined as follows: if $a$
belongs to $A$ and $f$ to $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,A_d)$, $a\cdot f$ is
such that $(a\cdot f)(x)=a\cdot f(x)$ for $x\in M$.  Lemma~1 is well
known, and we shall omit the proof.

$(c)\Rightarrow(b)$: by Corollary~2, it suffices to prove that $U_B$ is
a generator of the category $\mathbf{mod}\,B$.  For this we shall show
that $B_d$ is a direct factor of a product $U_B^n$:

Consider first two left $A$-modules $P$ and $Q$, and let ${}_sA$ be
the left $A$-module underlying $A$.  The abelian group
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,{}_sA)$ is naturally equipped with a right
$A$-module structure, so that it is legitimate to speak of the tensor
product $\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,{}_sA)\otimes_A Q$.  If $q$ is an
element of $Q$ and $f$ an element of
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,{}_sA)$, we denote by $v_{q,f}$ the $A$-linear
map $p\mapsto f(p)\cdot q$.  As $q$ and $f$ vary, the function
$(q,f)\mapsto v_{q,f}$ defines a linear map $v(P,Q)$ from
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,{}_sA)\otimes_A Q$ into
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,Q)$.  As $P$ and $Q$ vary, the maps $v(P,Q)$
define a morphism from the functor
$(P,Q)\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,{}_sA)\otimes_A Q$ into the
functor $(P,Q)\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,Q)$.  It follows in
particular that $v(P,Q)$ is a morphism of left modules over the ring
$C$ of endomorphisms of $Q$.

When $P$ is equal to ${}_sA$, $v(P,Q)$ is a bijection.  One deduces
easily that $v(P,Q)$ is a bijection whenever $P$ is a projective left
$A$-module of finite type.  If this is so, the left $C$-module
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,Q)$ is isomorphic to the left $C$-module
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,{}_sA)\otimes_A Q$, which is a direct factor of
a product $Q^n$.

One obtains the desired result by choosing $P$ and $Q$ equal to
${}_AU$.  The ring $C$ is then identified with the ring opposite
to~$B$.

\begin{corollary}
Let $A$ and $B$ be two rings and $U$ an $A$-$B$-bimodule ($A$ on the
left, $B$ on the right).  The following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] $U_B$ is a projective $B$-module of finite type;
      it is a generator of the category $\mathbf{mod}\,B$; moreover,
      the map $a\mapsto a_U$ is an isomorphism from $A$ onto the
      endomorphism ring of $U_B$.
\item[\normalfont b.] $U$ satisfies assertion~(b) of Corollary~3.
\item[\normalfont c.] ${}_AU$ is a projective left $A$-module of
      finite type; it is a generator of the category of left
      $A$-modules; moreover, the map $b\mapsto b_U$ is an isomorphism
      from the ring opposite to $B$ onto the endomorphism ring of
      ${}_AU$.
\end{description}
\end{corollary}

The equivalence of~(a) and~(b) results from Corollaries~2 and~3.  The
same goes for the equivalence of~(b) and of~(c), on condition that $A$
and $B$ be replaced respectively by $B^0$ and $A^0$.

We now give a few applications of the foregoing:

a.\ First, let $\mathfrak B$ be a universe with
$\Univ\in\mathfrak B$, $\Univ$ being the universe chosen once and for
all at the beginning of this work.  Let on the other hand
$\mathbf E$ be the category whose objects are the categories
$\mathbf C$ such that $\mathbf{MC}\in\mathfrak B$ and
$\mathbf{OC}\in\mathfrak B$, the morphisms of $\mathbf E$ being the
isomorphism classes of functors (cf.\ Chapter~I, \S8).  If $A$ is a
ring whose underlying set belongs to $\Univ$, the category
$\mathbf{mod}\,A$ is an object of $\mathbf E$; what precedes allows us
to determine the group $G(A)$ of automorphisms of this object:

By Corollaries~2 and~3 of Proposition~1, a functor
$T\colon\mathbf{mod}\,A\to\mathbf{mod}\,A$ defines an equivalence if it
is isomorphic to a functor $M\mapsto M\otimes_A U$, where $U$ is an
$A$-$A$-bimodule satisfying assertion~(c) of Corollary~3.  We may thus
identify the elements of $G(A)$ with the types of $A$-$A$-bimodules
satisfying assertion~(c) of Corollary~3.  Modulo this identification,
the law of $G(A)$ is defined by the map
$(U,V)\mapsto V\otimes_A U$.  Moreover, if $U$ satisfies
assertion~(c) of Corollary~3, the same holds for the $A$-$A$-bimodule
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(U,A_d)$; the types of $U$ and of
$\operatorname{Hom}_A(U,A_d)$ are then elements inverse to one another.

b.\ Let $B$ be a ring and $U$ the free $B$-module $B_d^n$.  The
endomorphism ring of $B_d^n$ is the ring $M_n(B)$ of $(n,n)$-matrices
with coefficients in $B$.  Corollary~2 shows that the functor
$N\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_B(B_d^n,N)$ defines an equivalence between
$\mathbf{mod}\,B$ and $\mathbf{mod}\,M_n(B)$.

In particular, if $M$ is a $B$-submodule of $B_d^n$,
$\operatorname{Hom}_B(B_d^n,M)$ is identified with the right ideal
$\mathfrak d(M)$ of $M_n(B)$ consisting of the endomorphisms $f$ of
$B_d^n$ whose image is contained in $M$.  One recovers in this way that
the map $M\mapsto\mathfrak d(M)$ is a bijection from the set of
$B$-submodules of $B_d^n$ onto the set of right ideals of $M_n(B)$.

c.\ The following example is due to Azumaya~\cite{azumaya-2}:
let $R$ be a commutative ring and $A$ a finite $R$-algebra (i.e.\ the
$R$-module underlying $A$ is of finite type) and faithful (i.e.\ the
map $r\mapsto r\cdot 1_A$ is an injection of $R$ into $A$).  We denote
by $A^0$ the algebra opposite to $A$ and by $A^e$ the algebra
$A\otimes_R A^0$~\cite{cartan-eilenberg}.  The map
$(x,a\otimes b)\mapsto b\cdot x\cdot a$ defines on $A$ a right
$A^e$-module structure; the map $(r,x)\mapsto r\cdot x$ defines a left
$R$-module structure on $A$: the algebra $A$ is thus equipped with an
$R$-$A^e$-bimodule structure ($R$ on the left, $A^e$ on the
right)\ednote{The original prints ``$R$-$A^c$-bimodule'' here; we
print $R$-$A^e$, the enveloping algebra defined above, as the
parenthesis that follows ($R$ on the left, $A^e$ on the right)
confirms.}.  It is this structure that we consider in the rest of the
paragraph.

\begin{lemma}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $A$ an $R$-algebra, $S$ a commutative
$R$-algebra, $B$ the $S$-algebra deduced from $A$ by extension of
scalars and $B^e$ the tensor product $B\otimes_S B^0$.  We suppose that
$A_{A^e}$ is a projective $A^e$-module; $B_{B^e}$ is then a projective
$B^e$-module.
\end{lemma}

Let indeed $\mu(A|R)$ be the map $a\otimes b\mapsto b\cdot a$ from
$A^e$ into $A$.  The $A^e$-module $A_{A^e}$ is projective if and only
if $\mu(A|R)$ induces an isomorphism from a direct factor of $A^e$
onto $A$.  In this case $\mu(A|R)\otimes_R S$ induces an isomorphism
from a direct factor of $A^e\otimes_R S$ onto $A\otimes_R S$.  The
lemma results from the fact that $\mu(A|R)\otimes_R S$,
$A^e\otimes_R S$ and $A\otimes_R S$ ``are identified'' respectively
with $\mu(B|S)$, $B^e$ and $B$.

\begin{lemma}
The hypotheses are those of Lemma~2; for every prime ideal $p$ of $R$,
$(A/pA)_p$ is then a semi-simple separable algebra over $(R/pR)_p$.
\end{lemma}

One applies Lemma~2 choosing for $S$ the algebra $(R/pR)_p$.  In this
case, $B$ coincides with $(A/pA)_p$.  Since $S$ is a field, one knows
on the other hand that $B_{B^e}$ is a projective $B^e$-module if and
only if $B$ is a separable $S$-algebra
(cf.~\cite{cartan-eilenberg}, Chapter~VI).

\begin{lemma}
The hypotheses are those of Lemma~2.  If $i$ is the canonical map from
the centre $Z(A)$ of $A$ into $A$, then $i\otimes_R S$ is an
isomorphism from $Z(A)\otimes_R S$ onto the centre of $A\otimes_R S$.
\end{lemma}

The centres of $A$ and of $B$ are indeed identified with
$\operatorname{Hom}_{A^e}(A,A)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_{B^e}(B,B)$.
The lemma results from this and from the fact that the canonical map
from $\operatorname{Hom}_{A^e}(M,N)\otimes_R S$ into
$\operatorname{Hom}_{B^e}(M\otimes_R S,N\otimes_R S)$ is a bijection
when $M$ is a projective $A^e$-module of finite type.

\begin{lemma}
If $R$ is a commutative ring and $M$ a projective $R$-module of finite
type, the following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] $M$ is a generator of $\mathbf{mod}\,R$.
\item[\normalfont b.] $M$ is a faithful $R$-module (i.e.\ the
      annihilator of $M$ is zero).
\end{description}
\end{lemma}

The implication $(a)\Rightarrow(b)$ is clear.  Let us prove the
converse: it suffices to show that $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N)$ is not
zero if $N$ is not zero; let therefore $m$ be a maximal ideal of $R$
such that $N_m$ is not zero.  It is clear that $M_m$ is a projective
$R_m$-module (hence free) and that the annihilator of $M_m$ is zero.
Since $M$ is of finite presentation,
$(\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N))_m$ is identified with
$\operatorname{Hom}_{R_m}(M_m,N_m)$~\cite{bourbaki-commutative}.
Since this last module is not zero, the same holds for
$(\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N))_m$, hence for
$\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N)$.

\begin{proposition}
Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $A$ a finite faithful $R$-algebra.
The following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{description}
\item[\normalfont a.] The functor $N\mapsto N\otimes_R A$ defines an
      equivalence between $\mathbf{mod}\,R$ and $\mathbf{mod}\,A^e$.
\item[\normalfont b.] The functor
      $M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{A^e}(A,M)$ defines an equivalence
      between $\mathbf{mod}\,A^e$ and $\mathbf{mod}\,R$.
\item[\normalfont c.] The right $A^e$-module $A_{A^e}$ is projective;
      moreover, the map $r\mapsto r\cdot 1_A$ is an isomorphism from
      $R$ onto the centre of $A$.
\item[\normalfont d.] The left $R$-module ${}_RA$ is projective;
      moreover, the map $b\mapsto b_A$ is an isomorphism from the ring
      opposite to $A^e$ onto the endomorphism ring of ${}_RA$.
\item[\normalfont e.] The left $R$-module ${}_RA$ is projective; for
      every maximal ideal $m$ of $R$, $A/mA$ is moreover a simple
      central algebra over $R/m$\ednote{The original prints ``sur $A$''
      (over $A$); we print over $R/m$, the residue field over which
      $A/mA$ is an algebra, as the proof below via Lemmas~3 and~4
      confirms.}.
\end{description}
\end{proposition}

The equivalence of~(a) and~(b) results from Corollary~3 of
Proposition~1.  The equivalence of~(a) and~(d) results from Lemma~5 and
Corollaries~2 and~4 of Proposition~1.  The implication
$(b)\Rightarrow(c)$ results from Corollary~3 of Proposition~1 and from
the fact that $\operatorname{Hom}_{A^e}(A,A)$ is the centre of $A$.

$(c)\Rightarrow(b)$: by Corollary~3 of Proposition~1 it suffices to
prove that $A_{A^e}$ is a generator of $\mathbf{mod}\,A^e$.  It is
equivalent to say that, for every maximal right ideal $m$ of $A^e$,
there exists an $A^e$-linear map $f\colon A\to A^e$ such that
$\operatorname{Im}f$ is not contained in $m$.  Now $m\cap R$ is a
prime ideal $p$ of $R$ and $A^e/m$ is a simple module over the ring
$B^e=B\otimes_S B^0$, where $S=(R/pR)_p$ and $B=(A/pA)_p$.  Since $B$
is a simple central algebra over $S$ by Lemmas~3 and~4, $B^e$ is a
simple ring and the abelian group
$\operatorname{Hom}_{B^e}(B,A^e/m)$ is not zero.  Since
$\operatorname{Hom}_{B^e}(B,A^e/m)$ is identified with
$(\operatorname{Hom}_{A^e}(A,A^e/m))_p$, one deduces that
$\operatorname{Hom}_{A^e}(A,A^e/m)$ is not zero.  Finally, since $A$
is projective, every non-zero morphism from $A$ into $A^e/m$ lifts to
$f\colon A\to A^e$ with $\operatorname{Im}f\not\subset m$.

$(c),(d)\Rightarrow(e)$: follows from Lemmas~3 and~4.

$(e)\Rightarrow(d)$: put indeed $L_R(A)=\operatorname{Hom}_R(A,A)$.
Let on the other hand $\varphi(A|R)$ be the map $b\mapsto b_A$ from
the ring $A^0\otimes_R A$, opposite to $A^e$, into $L_R(A)$.

Since ${}_RA$ is projective, $L_R(A)$ and $A^0\otimes_R A$ are
projective $R$-modules of finite type.  To show that $\varphi(A|R)$ is
a bijection, it therefore suffices to prove that
$\varphi(A|R)\otimes_R R/m$ is a bijection for every maximal ideal $m$
of $R$.  Now $(A^0\otimes_R A)\otimes_R R/m$,
$L_R(A)\otimes_R R/m$ and $\varphi(A|R)\otimes_R R/m$ are identified
respectively with $B^0\otimes_S B$, $L_S(B)$ and $\varphi(B|S)$, where
we have put $B=A/mA$ and $S=R/m$.  Since $B$ is a simple central
algebra over $R/m$, $\varphi(B|S)$ is a bijection for every $m$;
assertion~(d) results\ednote{The original prints ``assertion~(c)'';
the paragraph headed $(e)\Rightarrow(d)$ proves the map of~(d)
bijective, so we print~(d).}.

\begin{unnumberedcorollary}
Suppose the equivalent conditions of Proposition~2 satisfied.  The map
$a\mapsto a\cdot A$ is a bijection from the set of ideals of $R$ onto
the set of two-sided ideals of $A$.
\end{unnumberedcorollary}

The canonical injection of $a$ into $R$ indeed defines an injection
from $a\otimes_R A$ into $R\otimes_R A=A$.  The image of this injection
is none other than $a\cdot A$, and we identify $a\otimes_R A$ with this
image.  Since the functor $N\mapsto N\otimes_R A$ defines an
equivalence between $\mathbf{mod}\,R$ and $\mathbf{mod}\,A^e$, the map
$a\mapsto a\otimes_R A$ is a bijection from the set of $R$-submodules
of $R$ onto the set of $A^e$-submodules of $A$; the latter coincide
with the two-sided ideals of $A$.  We refer the reader
to~\cite{auslander-goldman} for other applications of
Proposition~2.
